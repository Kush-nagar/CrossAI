# Case Construction Essentials

Distilled from the full casing and case-construction lecture, which remains in the corpus (cases/casing-and-case-construction-lecture.md) for retrieval of full detail, including the complete Terror AFF and Data Localization ADV card texts analyzed card-by-card.

## The four features of a good case

A good case requires round vision: it should be built so you can go for **every single piece of evidence in the back half**.

1. **Pre-embedded weighing.** Cards are chosen so weighing is already written into the case. Examples: a very good uniqueness card that establishes a **brink**; fast link evidence for a disad that triggers **timeframe** instantly; a large impact scenario that is very specific but also accesses **magnitude**. Which weighing to embed depends on the likely advantage/disadvantage ground on each side of the specific topic (PF, LD, or Policy).
   - **Cohesion preference:** the default is that each card does double duty and fits the broader narrative of the case — e.g., a uniqueness card should *also* serve as a timeframe card. What to avoid is filler: a defensive frontline spike added for its own sake, with no anticipated response actually behind it.
   - **Independent weighing spikes are okay.** A card whose only job is weighing — a dedicated brink card, a probability/timeframe card, a framing card that frames out impact turns — earns its place even if it does nothing else for the narrative. Weighing you can extend from the constructive is worth the seconds it costs; don't cut a good weighing card just because it doesn't also carry link or impact work.
   - **The weighing does NOT have to be explicit in the tag.** This is the most misapplied part of the criterion. A card is "built for weighing" whenever its evidence carries *latent* warranting — a spin, a phrasing, a mechanism, a stat — that could be **easily extrapolated into weighing later**, even if the tag never says the words "magnitude," "timeframe," "probability," or "brink." A tag reading "ISIS is decentralizing into smaller cells across Syria and Lebanon" is doing timeframe and brink work whether or not it announces it: the debater can stand up in summary and spin that same card into "this is happening *now*, faster than they can respond." So do **not** dock a case for lacking explicit weighing language — judge the *extrapolation potential* of the actual evidence. Only a card whose warrant genuinely can't be spun into any weighing (no brink, no comparative, no scenario, no rate) counts as missing embedded weighing.
   - **How to coach this, every time — flag it whether it's there or not:**
     - **If a card lacks any extrapolatable weighing spin:** say so plainly, name the specific card(s) and the exact spot in the case where the debater could add it, and hand them a **concrete tag/card example** of the weighing language to fold in (e.g., "re-tag the Kruse card so the warrant reads as timeframe: *'Attacks are planned for THIS year — the con's fix takes years to matter'*"). Don't just say "add weighing" — show the tag.
     - **If a card already carries latent weighing:** point that out too, by name, so the debater actually *notices* the free weighing sitting in their own evidence and remembers to extend it in the back half — again with the specific spin to run (e.g., "your Shtuni uniqueness card is already a brink card — in summary, spin 'resurging now' into 'we're on the brink, their uniqueness is non-responsive'"). A debater who never notices their own embedded weighing leaves it on the flow.
2. **Spikes** — three subsets:
   - **Offensive spikes:** cards in your case that interact with a disadvantage or case position the opposing side makes (may be a turn, but not necessarily). Incorporate as many cards as possible that interact with the NEG or AFF (depending on your side) — if you win your case and its cards carry sufficient defense against their case, you don't even have to win new defense in rebuttal.
   - **Defensive spikes:** embedded frontlines that preempt specific responses expected in the rebuttal/1NC, making frontlining much easier.
   - **Weighing spikes:** standalone cards read for weighing alone (brink, timeframe, probability, impact-turn frame-outs). These don't need to be cohesive with the case narrative the way link and impact evidence does — a weighing spike is allowed to sit on its own.
   - Construction method: brainstorm the specific responses opponents will make and find evidence you can "hide" in the case as defensive spikes; list the most generic, most common opposing case positions and embed answers that remain cohesive with the case's broader narrative.
3. **Evidence quality.** Debate is comparison; when warrants compete, evidence comparison breaks the tie. Prefer large data sets in studies and strong author qualifications.
4. **Collapse diversity.** The case should support collapsing in different ways: multiple impact scenarios, different links to go for, possibly uniqueness. Stay close to the **stock ground** of the resolution — stock positions are easier to defend. Diversify back-half strategies so you're never pinned on one specific link, unless that link is overpowered.

## Worked example 1: the Terror AFF (Nov 2025 — "USFG should mandate disclosure of encrypted communications from technology companies")

Card-by-card lessons (full card texts retrievable from the lecture file):

- **Shtuni card** (tag: "The Islamic State Is Resurging — Encryption Is Crucial To Their Strategy"). One card doing four jobs: (a) elite qualifications — M.Sc. in International Relations and Security from Georgetown, expert in non-state actors; (b) recency — published four months before the November topic; (c) spikes out the con's "terrorism is decreasing" uniqueness response by explaining terror groups are decentralizing into smaller groups to gain strength, with Syria/Lebanon at highest fatality counts; (d) establishes the link story — counter-terrorism struggles to monitor terrorists due to encryption barriers — setting up solvency. A single card can be uniqueness + brink + link + solvency setup.
- **Kruse card** ("Attacks are coming this year" — US Air Force lieutenant general, DIA director). Example of embedded timeframe weighing, and of the **tech over truth** axiom: every argument is presumed true if not properly contested; probability is determined by objective analysis and evidence explanation. The card is slightly miscut (the source says increased attacks are *planned*), but that's acceptable because it is **defensible**: if challenged, you can contextualize why the tag is still true (on-the-ground reports plus qualitative warrants — UAVs and AI integration, fall of Assad expanding ISIS/al-Qa'ida access, Africa/Syria as a "springboard to advance this year"). Rule: evidence doesn't have to be 100% perfect as long as it's defensible; be lenient and reasonable on initial presentation.
- **Manpearl card** ("Lawful access enables effective surveillance"). The only card establishing a **causal claim** (lawful access key to intercepting terrorist communications), and a spike that is defensive *and* offensive at once: its highlighted mechanisms (cryptographic envelopes, split-key encryption) answer the "lawful access opens cyber vulnerabilities" turn on the case (defensive) and also pre-answer the common cyber disadvantage (offensive).
- **Jaffer 16** ("No foreign shift"). A purely defensive spike: preempts "terrorists shift to non-US platforms" by showing the aff entails sanctioning/designating foreign providers, whose US user base gives them an incentive to comply.
- **Wagstaff 15**. Defensive spike preempting "terrorists build their own platforms": home-brewed encryption is ineffective and insecure.
- **Borgwardt et al 16** ("Otherwise, an attack goes nuclear and sparks a Middle Eastern war"). Model impact card because it: (a) gives a cohesive scenario — a *conventional* terrorist attack on US soil triggers a US nuclear response — which **circumvents** the true "no nuclear terrorism" impact defense (the scenario never relies on nuclear terrorism); (b) pre-answers "Trump is rational / contained" impact defense (rage, impulsivity, narcissistic injury, strongman incentive to strike back); (c) is extremely fast — "civil liberties may be suspended," "speed would be important," "we should not expect deliberation," "a massive military response in weeks" — completing the timeframe chain with Kruse's uniqueness; (d) **link turns every critique** (security K, settler colonialism K): in the world of the negative, Trump intervenes abroad, locks down Muslim communities, and paints terror as a bigger threat — securitization *increases* absent the aff, non-uniquing and turning the K's links.
- **Avery card** (usual internal-link scenario to extinction). Good because it lists a laundry list of actors — Pakistan, Iran, China — establishing **scope**. But it dates from 2020, which teaches the **thumper** response: a thumper is both link and impact defense indicating the scenario has *already triggered* (e.g., "the Middle East is already embroiled in conflict post-2020, and no nuclear war happened"). Always check whether older evidence assumes recent events; flag thumper-vulnerable cards when reviewing a case.

## Card-cutting and formatting discipline

- A card is read as **author last name + date published**.
- The **tag** is a very concise explanation of how you want to **extend the card in the back half**. Make tags extremely descriptive; use bolding, underlining, and capitalization of specific words for emphasis. Re-tagging cards for clearer judge communication is a standard piece of stress-test advice.
- Keep the card **in its original paragraphs** — do not condense cards.
- **Underlining** marks context that is less important/tangential; **bold + underline** is where the highlighting usually lives — it's where the judge should look first if an evidence dispute arises. Be extremely thrifty and concise: underlining/bolding the entire paragraph is not aesthetically pleasing and defeats the emphasis.
- **Highlighting rules:** cards are read to convey a very large amount of information without taking up too much time (debate has time constraints; generate as much offense as possible in the constructive, rebuttal, and every speech that reads cards). Highlight concisely but in **complete sentences** so the speaker reads coherently; de-highlight as many filler words as possible. You may splice specific words across different sentences to construct a sentence, but the highlighted text must still say the same thing the card actually says — never overturn or misconstrue the card's meaning.

## Worked example 2: the Data Localization advantage (framing and solves-case)

- **Ustaran card** (tag: "AI requires international data flows — its absence prohibits AI upsides by inhibiting training and ensures AI downsides by entrenching biases"). Its strategic job is to **frame out impact turns**: impact turns must be unique, but this card makes AI's existence inevitable in either world — neither side changes that trajectory; the only variable is whether AI is biased or unbiased. So "AI bad / AGI bad" turns are non-unique, while voting pro makes AI good (diverse datasets) and voting con makes AI bad.
- **Solves-case arguments:** the advantage's thesis — AI with upsides addresses *every* risk (information organization, warming, pandemics, asteroids) — means your scenarios **outweigh on scope**: even if they control one direction of one existential scenario, you control all the others. Voting pro is effectively **terminal impact defense to the disadvantage** (even if 100% of their link/internal-link scenario is true, your solvency addresses it and prevents it going nuclear). This makes the framing **try or die**: the only risk of extinction is the status quo.
- **Solves-case can live in disad uniqueness too.** April topic ("The United States should ban the President's sole authority to deploy military troops abroad without congressional approval"): the neg's deterrence disad said Trump's erraticness/unpredictability deterred adversaries by signaling willingness to use disproportionate force (e.g., strikes on Iran raising the perceived cost of war for China/Russia). That uniqueness *solves the aff's scenario*: even if the pro accesses its conflict internal link, the conflict never escalates to nuclear war while deterrence holds — conceded disad uniqueness means the pro has no impact.
- **Danks card** ("Reject impact defense — research underestimates/ignores looping effects and widespread applications"): another defensive spike, this one pre-answering the impact defense that AI isn't existential or powerful enough.
- General principle: constantly ask how each piece of evidence, each argument in the case, and each extrapolation of the case **interacts with the opposing side** — through weighing, defense, or offense — and cross-apply parts of your own case as defense to theirs.

## When advising on a case (stress-test / construction coaching)

- Check the case against all four features: embedded weighing, offensive/defensive/weighing spikes, evidence quality, collapse diversity.
- On embedded weighing, judge **extrapolation potential, not explicit tags** (see feature 1): a card counts as built-for-weighing when its warrant could be spun into weighing later, even if the tag never names magnitude/timeframe/probability. Never mark weighing "missing" just because the tag is silent. And flag it either way — if the latent weighing isn't there, point to the specific card and spot to add it with a concrete tag example; if it IS there, name the card so the debater notices the free weighing and extends it.
- Don't tell a debater to cut a weighing card for not fitting the narrative — independent weighing spikes are legitimate. Reserve the cohesion objection for filler defense.
- Help the debater brainstorm the most likely opposing responses and the most common opposing case positions, then find cohesive evidence to embed as spikes.
- Advise on re-tagging cards for judge communication and on underline/bold/highlight placement.
- Flag thumper-vulnerable (outdated) evidence and non-defensible miscuts; defensible imperfect evidence is acceptable.
- Look for solves-case interactions and framing cards (impact-turn frame-outs, try-or-die) the case could add or exploit.

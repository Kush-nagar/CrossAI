# Past Debate Topics (NSDA)

Authoritative archive of past high-school topic resolutions. When a debater asks what the topic was in a given month/season, or wants historical topic examples, answer **from this list only** — never reconstruct a past topic from memory. Coverage: **PF 2010–11 through 2025–26** (PF existed from 2002–03; earlier cycles are not listed here — say so rather than guessing), **LD 2000–01 through 2025–26**, **Policy 2000–01 through 2025–26**. If asked about anything outside this coverage or a cycle not listed, say you're not certain rather than inventing a resolution. Every resolution below begins "Resolved:" (omitted to save space). Maintainers: 2026–27 and later seasons must be appended from speechanddebate.org/topics.

**2023–24** — S/O: The United States ought to guarantee the right to housing. | N/D: The United States ought to prohibit the extraction of fossil fuels from federal public lands and waters. | J/F: The United States ought to substantially reduce its military presence in the West Asia-North Africa region. | M/A: The primary objective of the United States criminal justice system ought to be rehabilitation. | Nationals: In a democracy, a people ought to have the right to secede from their government.

**2022–23** — S/O: The United States ought to implement a single-payer universal healthcare system. | N/D: The People's Republic of China ought to prioritize environmental protection over economic growth. | J/F: Justice requires open borders for human migration. | M/A: The justices of the Supreme Court of the United States ought to be term-limited. | Nationals: Government employees have a moral obligation to leak classified information to address injustice.

**2021–22** — S/O: The member nations of the World Trade Organization ought to reduce intellectual property protections for medicines. | N/D: A just government ought to recognize an unconditional right of workers to strike. | J/F: The appropriation of outer space by private entities is unjust. | M/A: In a democracy, a free press ought to prioritize objectivity over advocacy. | Nationals: Radicalism is preferable to incrementalism to achieve social justice.

**2020–21** — S/O: In a democracy, voting ought to be compulsory. | N/D: The United States ought to provide a federal jobs guarantee. | J/F: States ought to ban lethal autonomous weapons. | M/A: The United States ought to guarantee universal child care. | Nationals: A public health emergency justifies limiting civil liberties.

**2019–20** — S/O: In the United States, colleges and universities ought not consider standardized tests in undergraduate admissions decisions. | N/D: The United States ought to eliminate subsidies for fossil fuels. | J/F: States ought to eliminate their nuclear arsenals. | M/A: Predictive policing is unjust. | Nationals: The intergenerational accumulation of wealth is antithetical to democracy.

**2013–14** — S/O: In a democracy, voting ought to be compulsory. | N/D: In the United States criminal justice system, truth-seeking ought to take precedence over attorney-client privilege. | J/F: Developing countries should prioritize environmental protection over resource extraction when the two are in conflict. | M/A: Placing political conditions on humanitarian aid to foreign countries is unjust. | Nationals: The United States ought to prioritize the pursuit of national security objectives above the digital privacy of its citizens.

**2012–13** — S/O: The United States ought to extend to non-citizens accused of terrorism the same constitutional due process protections it grants to citizens. | N/D: The United States ought to guarantee universal health care for its citizens. | J/F: Rehabilitation ought to be valued above retribution in the United States criminal justice system. | M/A: The United States is justified in intervening in the internal political processes of other countries to attempt to stop human rights abuses. | Nationals: Oppressive government is more desirable than no government.

**2011–12** — S/O: Justice requires the recognition of animal rights. | N/D: Individuals have a moral obligation to assist people in need. | J/F: It is morally permissible for victims to use deadly force as a deliberate response to repeated domestic violence. | M/A: Targeted killing is a morally permissible foreign policy tool. | Nationals: A government has the obligation to lessen the economic gap between its rich and poor citizens.

**2009–10** — S/O: Public high school students in the United States ought not be required to pass standardized exit exams to graduate. | N/D: Public health concerns justify compulsory immunization. | J/F: Economic sanctions ought not be used to achieve foreign policy objectives. | M/A: In the United States, the principle of jury nullification is a just check on government. | Nationals: Compulsory inclusion of non-felons' DNA in any government database is unjust. | NCFL Nationals: The United States government has a moral obligation to afford the same constitutional rights to all people on United States soil.

**2008–09** — S/O: It is morally permissible to kill one innocent person to save the lives of more innocent people. | N/D: In a democratic society, felons ought to retain the right to vote. | J/F: The United States ought to submit to the jurisdiction of an international court designed to prosecute crimes against humanity. | M/A: Vigilantism is justified when the government has failed to enforce the law. | Nationals: Military conscription is unjust. | NCFL Nationals: When in conflict, the preservation of minority cultural values ought to be valued above the preservation of a unified national culture.

**2007–08** — S/O: A just society ought not use the death penalty as a form of punishment. | N/D: In the United States, plea-bargaining in exchange for testimony is unjust. | J/F: It is just for the United States to use military force to prevent the acquisition of nuclear weapons by nations that pose a military threat. | M/A: Hate crime enhancements are unjust in the United States. | Nationals: Limiting economic inequality ought to be a more important social goal than maximizing economic freedom. | NCFL Nationals: Secondary education in America should value the fine arts over athletics.

**2006–07** — S/O: A just government should provide health care to its citizens. | N/D: A victim's deliberate use of deadly force is a just response to repeated domestic violence. | J/F: The actions of corporations ought to be held to the same moral standards as the actions of individuals. | M/A: The United Nations' obligation to protect global human rights ought to be valued above its obligation to respect national sovereignty. | Nationals: On balance, violent revolution is a just response to political oppression. | NCFL Nationals: Judicial activism is unjust in a democracy.

**2005–06** — S/O: In matters of U.S. immigration policy, restrictions on the rights of non-citizens are consistent with democratic ideals. | N/D: Judicial activism is necessary to protect the rights of American citizens. | J/F: The use of the state's power of eminent domain to promote private enterprise is unjust. | M/A: Juveniles charged with violent crimes should be tried and punished as adults. | Nationals: In matters of collecting military intelligence, the ends justify the means. | NCFL Nationals: When in conflict, an individual's freedom of speech should be valued above a community's moral standards.

**2004–05** — S/O: Individual claims of privacy ought to be valued above competing claims of societal welfare. | N/D: The United States has a moral obligation to promote democratic ideals in other nations. | J/F: Democracy is best served by strict separation of church and state. | M/A: To better protect civil liberties, community standards ought to take precedence over conflicting national standards. | Nationals: The pursuit of scientific knowledge ought to be constrained by concern for societal good. | NCFL Nationals: The primary purpose of formal education ought to be to impart knowledge.

**2003–04** — S/O: In the U.S. judicial system, truth seeking ought to take precedence over privileged communication. | N/D: The U.S. has a moral obligation to mitigate international conflicts. | J/F: A government's obligation to protect the environment ought to take precedence over its obligation to promote economic development. | M/A: As a general principle, individuals have an obligation to value the common good above their own interests. | Nationals: Civil disobedience in a democracy is morally justified. | NCFL Nationals: A nation's citizens' rights ought to take precedence over its security.

**2002–03** — S/O: When the United States is engaged in military conflict, the demands of national security ought to supersede conflicting claims of individual rights. | N/D: When in conflict, academic freedom in U.S. high schools ought to be valued above community standards. | J/F: When in conflict, globalization ought to be valued above national sovereignty. | M/A: When in conflict, the letter of the law ought to take priority over the spirit of the law. | Nationals: Rehabilitation ought to be valued above punishment in the U.S. criminal justice system. | NCFL Nationals: Capitalism is the most just economic system.

**2001–02** — S/O: Decentralized governmental power ought to be a fundamental goal of democratic society. | N/D: A lesser developed nation's right to develop ought to take priority over its obligation to protect the environment. | J/F: Oppressive government is more desirable than no government. | M/A: Limiting the freedom of expression of adults is justified by society's interest in protecting children. | Nationals: Laws which protect citizens from themselves are justified. | NCFL Nationals: The people's right to know outweighs the government's need for confidentiality.

**2000–01** — S/O: Colleges and universities have a moral obligation to prohibit the public expression of hate speech on their campuses. | N/D: Establishing a safe educational environment in grades K-12 justifies infringement of students' civil liberties. | J/F: The possession of nuclear weapons is immoral. | M/A: The public's right to know ought to be valued above the right to privacy of candidates for public office. | Nationals: On balance, violent revolution is a just response to oppression. | NCFL Nationals: Providing for basic human needs of citizens in a society ought to be valued above individual property rights.

## Policy / CX (one topic per season)

- **2025–26**: The United States federal government should significantly increase its exploration and/or development of the Arctic.
- **2024–25**: The United States federal government should significantly strengthen its protection of domestic intellectual property rights in copyrights, patents, and/or trademarks.
- **2023–24**: The United States federal government should substantially increase fiscal redistribution in the United States by adopting a federal jobs guarantee, expanding Social Security, and/or providing a basic income.
- **2022–23**: The United States federal government should substantially increase its security cooperation with the North Atlantic Treaty Organization in one or more of the following areas: artificial intelligence, biotechnology, cybersecurity.
- **2021–22**: The United States federal government should substantially increase its protection of water resources in the United States.
- **2020–21**: The United States federal government should enact substantial criminal justice reform in the United States in one or more of the following: forensic science, policing, sentencing.
- **2019–20**: The United States federal government should substantially reduce Direct Commercial Sales and/or Foreign Military Sales of arms from the United States.
- **2018–19**: The United States federal government should substantially reduce its restrictions on legal immigration to the United States.
- **2017–18**: The United States federal government should substantially increase its funding and/or regulation of elementary and/or secondary education in the United States.
- **2016–17**: The United States federal government should substantially increase its economic and/or diplomatic engagement with the People's Republic of China.
- **2015–16**: The United States federal government should substantially curtail its domestic surveillance.
- **2014–15**: The United States federal government should substantially increase its non-military exploration and/or development of the Earth's oceans.
- **2013–14**: The United States federal government should substantially increase its economic engagement toward Cuba, Mexico or Venezuela.
- **2012–13**: The United States federal government should substantially increase its transportation infrastructure investment in the United States.
- **2011–12**: The United States federal government should substantially increase its exploration and/or development of space beyond the Earth's mesosphere.
- **2010–11**: The United States federal government should substantially reduce its military and/or police presence in one or more of the following: South Korea, Japan, Afghanistan, Kuwait, Iraq, Turkey.
- **2009–10**: The United States federal government should substantially increase social services for persons living in poverty in the United States.
- **2008–09**: The United States federal government should substantially increase alternative energy incentives in the United States.

- **2004–05**: The United States federal government should establish a foreign policy substantially increasing its support of United Nations peacekeeping operations.
- **2003–04**: The United States federal government should establish an ocean policy substantially increasing protection of marine natural resources.
- **2002–03**: The United States federal government should substantially increase public health services for mental health care in the United States.
- **2001–02**: The United States federal government should establish a foreign policy significantly limiting the use of weapons of mass destruction.
- **2000–01**: The United States federal government should significantly increase protection of privacy in the United States in one or more of the following areas: employment, medical records, consumer information, search and seizure.

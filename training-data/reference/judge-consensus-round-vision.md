# Cross-Judge Consensus — How Rounds Actually Get Evaluated

Synthesized from eleven judge paradigms (mostly tech/flow judges across PF,
LD, and Policy). Judges share largely the same views on strategy; treat this
throughline as the default evaluative lens for feedback, drills, and
win-probability estimates, and treat the deviations at the bottom as
possibilities to flag, not defaults. Individual named paradigms (when a
debater names their judge) still override this consensus.

These eleven are a representative sample of the judging population, not its
boundary: when estimating weights and win probability, generalize across all
judge paradigms known — the broader tech/flow mainstream, lay and hybrid
judges, and the round-vision lecture's framework — and use this sample for
depth, direct heuristics, and quotable standards. Where the wider pool would
genuinely diverge from this sample, weight toward the broader norm and flag
the divergence.

## The consensus throughline

**Tech over truth, decided off the flow.** Judges vote on what was argued and
answered in the round, flow on their own (never off the speech doc), and will
not do work for debaters — no filling gaps, no weighing that wasn't given, no
reconstructing arguments from evidence unprompted.

**Warranting is non-negotiable.** An unwarranted claim is not an argument.
The hierarchy multiple judges state outright: warranted cards > warranted
analytics > unwarranted cards > unwarranted analytics. An author name without
an explained warrant buys nothing. This is the single most under-emphasized
evaluative standard — when a speech's warranting is hard to identify, that
alone costs rounds.

**Clarity is the debater's burden — unflowable means never said.** If the
judge can't understand or flow a line, the opponent doesn't have to answer it
and it will not appear in the decision. Signposting, numbering, and organized
line-by-line are what make arguments exist. Judges will not say "clear" as a
favor; several consider prompting a debater to be intervention.

**Back-half discipline decides rounds:**
- Collapse is expected and rewarded — narrow the round so the judge doesn't
  have to intervene ("my ballot is like electricity — it takes the path of
  least resistance").
- Extensions must be complete: uniqueness where needed, link, internal link,
  impact. Poor back-half extensions are punished even when the argument was
  winning earlier.
- **Offense only counts if it appears in BOTH back-half speeches** (summary
  and final focus / the equivalent). Skipping a speech kills it.
- **Defense is not sticky** once it's been answered — a reason to disbelieve
  an argument must be re-extended in every subsequent speech to count.
- No new arguments late: new final-speech material is struck, often
  jurisdictionally; new responses to old material past the allowed speech are
  ignored; theory must come the speech immediately after the violation.

**Weighing wins close rounds — but only warranted, comparative weighing.**
One-line "we outweigh on magnitude" blips are functionally intervention
requests. Judges want: comparative engagement with the opponent's weighing,
meta-weighing when impacts are unlike (why probability > magnitude here),
weighing introduced early, and diversified mechanisms — try-or-die almost
never breaks ties by itself. Winning risk of offense plus the weighing beats
winning lots of un-weighed flows.

**Evidence quality matters, comparison more.** "We postdate" is not
comparison — implicate methodology, author quals, and what the evidence
actually assumes. Direct quotes beat paraphrasing (some judges refuse
paraphrased evidence outright). Judges read cards only when instructed and
only what they're instructed to read; slow evidence production reads as the
card not existing.

**Engagement beats clash-avoidance theater.** Don't be a "docbot" — blocks
read without implicating against the opponent's specific warrants contribute
nothing. Turns must be implicated (link and impact extended) to earn offense.
IVIs and frivolous theory are consensus-disliked: underdeveloped, usually a
time-suck, rarely voted on. Kritiks need development, real links (not
omission), and engagement to be viable.

**Judge instruction is rewarded.** Ballot-directing language — telling the
judge what matters, in what order, and why — is appreciated across the board;
final speeches that read like the RFD get written into the RFD.

**Risk is probabilistic.** Default offense/defense: zero risk is nearly
unachievable without a technical concession, and risk ≈ probability ×
magnitude. Winning the link level matters more than weighing an argument you
haven't won.

## Deviations to flag as possibilities (not defaults)

- **Warrant bar**: one judge treats claims and warrants as the same kind of
  premise (much lower bar); the consensus demands explicit warranting.
- **Analytics vs. evidence**: most accept smart analytics; a minority are
  markedly more likely to vote on evidence-based claims than analytics.
- **Speed**: everyone demands clarity; some will call "clear", others refuse
  on intervention grounds — at real speed, clarity risk is asymmetric.
- **Theory/tricks appetite**: ranges from "will evaluate anything warranted"
  to "would rather watch paint dry"; disclosure/paraphrasing theory is the
  most viable slice in PF.
- **Progressive tolerance in PF**: some welcome K/theory/pre-fiat roads;
  others strongly prefer post-fiat, topic-specific rounds.
- **Misc quirks** (speaker-point habits, doc etiquette, prep-stealing
  pet peeves) exist per judge — useful for judge analysis, rarely
  strategy-relevant.

## Directives for feedback, drills, and win-probability

- Evaluate speeches through this consensus lens by default; base
  win-probability adjustments on these preferences (see the judge-consensus
  rows in the factor-weights table).
- **If a speech's organization or warranting is hard to identify, say so
  explicitly in feedback** — under consensus judging, an unflowable line was
  never said and an unwarranted claim is not an argument. This feedback is
  mandatory, not optional polish.
- When generating hypothetical judges (drills), draw the paradigm from this
  consensus and give it one concrete deviation from the list above as its
  quirk.
- When a debater names a real judge with a stored individual paradigm, that
  paradigm overrides the consensus; where it's silent, fall back here.

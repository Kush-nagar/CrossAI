# Judge Paradigms

Synthesized from written paradigms of 8 judges (5 Public Forum-primary, 3 Policy/CX-primary).
Contact info, email chains, bios, and school/conflict info have been deliberately stripped —
only strategic/adjudication content is kept. Use this two ways:

1. **A debater names a specific judge for an upcoming or past round** — pull that judge's
   individual paradigm (below, keyed by name) and tailor strategy/feedback to it specifically.
2. **No judge is named, or the question is about general strategy** — draw on the aggregated
   consensus below. Where judges disagree, present the majority stance as the default but
   flag the minority view and why it may hold in some instances, rather than picking one
   silently.

## Cross-Judge Consensus & Disagreements

**Tech vs. truth.** Most of these judges lean tech > truth (Jason Zhao, Avi Shah, Archan Sen,
Rafael Pierry, Henry Anastasi Scott all state it explicitly, with Rafael and Avi treating it as
close to absolute). The disagreement: Bryce Piotrowski and Ishan Dubye are meaningfully more
truth-sensitive — Bryce explicitly rejects "a dichotomy between truth and tech" and weighs
evidence quality into who "did the better debating"; Ishan says he tries to avoid dogmatism but
evidence/warrant quality and persuasiveness affect his thresholds. Consensus stance: play tech
arguments straight in front of most judges, but don't assume a dropped-but-absurd argument
auto-wins in front of Bryce or Ishan specifically.

**Warrants are mandatory, but the bar varies.** Every judge in this set requires a claim to have
a warrant to count as a complete argument — "impacts are not assumed" (Ishan), "warrants start
at 0, not 100" (Jason Zhao), "claims require warrants, incomplete arguments even if conceded
will not be evaluated" (Sripad Yadagiri), lower-than-average warrant bar (Rafael Pierry). Where
they differ is threshold: Sripad's test is explicitly minimal ("why is [claim] true?" / "[one
clause]" is enough), while Jason Zhao and Ishan Dubye want real depth — thin/loosely-highlighted
cards get discounted, and a case built mostly on such cards invites an unusually low threshold
for response. Default advice: always over-warrant rather than under-warrant, since the judges
who are lenient on this cost you nothing and the strict ones will punish thin warrants hard.

**Speed and clarity.** Every judge here caps effective speed well short of full spread and will
not vote for what they can't understand. Rough ceilings: Ishan ~250 wpm before speaks drop below
a 29; Henry Anastasi Scott flows by ear up to ~250 wpm at its least clear, ~325 at its most clear;
Jason Zhao caps speaks at 29.5 for poor spreading and wants clarity on tags specifically. The
practical rule across all of them: speed is fine as a tool, but slow down on tags/taglines and on
abstract or complex argument, and don't gamble on a judge auto-parsing niche jargon.

**Extensions are for clash clarity, not box-checking — but how much re-explanation is needed is
contested.** Ishan and Jason Zhao both frame extensions as existing to clarify what's being
clashed over, not a procedural requirement, and neither is "nitpicky" about it. Henry Anastasi
Scott goes further and is actively trying to shrink the extension norm in PF (arguing you
shouldn't need to extend warrants absent a clash need, since PF's short back-half speeches are
disproportionately eaten by extension time relative to Policy/LD). Jason Zhao agrees explicitly:
"extending in rebuttal is unnecessary." Minority/counter-consideration: judges who didn't address
this directly (e.g. Bryce, the policy-primary judges) still expect a claim to be actively
maintained through the round even if the exact re-explanation bar is lower than old-school norms
— don't assume every judge has adopted the lighter-extension view.

**Weighing must be tied to link strength, not vague appeals.** Widely shared: "try or die" is
overused and shouldn't outweigh a well-developed link/timeframe argument (Ishan: prefers
timeframe > try-or-die and link > uniqueness; Bryce: "try or die has gotten out of control...
you need a link to win the debate"). Good probability weighing pairs with link defense rather
than standing alone (Jason Zhao explicitly rejects "new defense hidden as probability weighing").
Judge instruction — telling the judge how to resolve the round rather than leaving it to
inference — is uniformly rewarded (Ishan: "judge instruction is undervalued"; Archan Sen:
"identify win conditions... will substantially increase your chances"; Avi Shah: similar).

**New arguments in final speeches.** Consensus rule: a new argument isn't excluded automatically
— the opposing side must flag it as new in the very next speech, or it's fair game (Rafael,
Sripad, Jason Zhao, Avi Shah all state versions of this). The one universal exception is the
final speech of the round (2AR/Final Focus), which no one gets to answer, so judges will
self-enforce "no new arguments" there without being told.

**Theory.** Among the PF-primary judges, reasonability > competing interpretations is the
explicit shared default (Ishan, Jason Zhao), alongside skepticism of frivolous/heavily semantic
theory shells and a favorable lean toward disclosure-based theory. The policy-primary judges
(Avi Shah, Archan Sen, Rafael Pierry) engage theory more as a competition question than a
fairness-shell question, and are generally skeptical of theory objections to counterplans that
aren't grounded in genuine in-round abuse — Rafael explicitly calls most CP theory "self-serving
and contrived."

**Kritiks.** Comfort level varies by judge but the shared thread is: over-explain rather than
assume familiarity. Ishan and Jason Zhao (nearly word-for-word) both say "err on the side of
over-explanation, fully impact stuff out" and are hesitant on discourse-based/non-specific links.
Henry Anastasi Scott and Archan Sen are more K-friendly technically but both are unforgiving of
teams who try to "ethos" their way past a technical drop rather than out-teching it. Avi Shah and
Rafael Pierry (policy judges) want the K to function as an alternate framework that moots the
plan, not a disguised counterplan with causal-logic links — a K that reads like a CP is a common
frustration across judges who commented on it at all.

## Individual Judge Paradigms

### Ishan Dubey
**Format:** PF (also judges LD/CX; unfamiliar with specific topics there).
Persuaded by arguments over ideology, but not a pure tech-over-truth judge — evidence/warrant
quality and persuasiveness affect his thresholds. Requires a warrant for every argument;
impacts are not assumed. ~250 wpm is his rough ceiling before speaks drop below a 29 — clarity
and depth matter more than an exact cutoff; slower on abstract/complex material. Wants
descriptive taglines and full-paragraph card cuts; prefers evidence comparison from debaters
over intervention. Prefers timeframe over try-or-die, link over uniqueness; conceded does not
automatically mean 100% probable. Values judge instruction highly ("undervalued... makes my
decision more predictable"). Reasonability > CI by default; biased toward theory about bad
evidence/disclosure, biased against frivolous/heavily semantic shells. Uncomfortable with theory
that adjudicates real-world "violence"/racism accusations technically. On Ks: over-explain, fully
impact things out; skeptical of non-specific/discourse-only links. Non-starters: ad-homs,
unverifiable mudslinging, tricks, soliciting speaks.

### Henry Anastasi Scott
**Format:** PF.
Flows entirely by ear — will glance at the doc out of curiosity but won't use it to flow; ~250
wpm at least clear, ~325 wpm at most clear. Strongly tech > truth ("if 'the sky is red' is
dropped, it's true"), but will down teams for discrimination as external to flow evaluation. Is
actively trying to shrink the PF extension norm: doesn't think warrants need re-extension absent
a clash need, and is vocal that PF's extension-time-to-total-time ratio is disproportionate
compared to Policy/LD. On weighing: tech-substance era should implicate probability more; default
timeframe > try-or-die absent alternative warrants; probability weighing = strength-of-link
weighing, and that case should be made in rebuttal/summary, not invented fresh in a later speech.
Wants theory collapsed to one standard/one voter; leans reasonability, pro-disclosure, skeptical
of most other theory norms — verbally direct him to the wiki rather than screenshot wars. Likes
where Ks are going in PF and is open to being a testing ground for new K strategy, provided it's
clear and delineated; doesn't like being told what literature he "should" already know — explain
it regardless of speed. Presumption defaults to a coin flip (literally seat-position-based) absent
an argued claim. Skeptical of 1AC/1NC-style terminology bleeding into PF — it actively confuses
his flow.

### Bryce Piotrowski
**Format:** PF (background in NPDA policy-style parliamentary).
Rejects a truth/tech dichotomy — his ballot rewards "better debating," which includes evidence
quality, not tech execution alone. Will only open the speech doc to fill a flowing gap outside
debaters' control, get context, resolve an evidence dispute, or quote evidence in the RFD — not
as a primary flowing tool. Wants arguments grounded in the actual topic; is unsympathetic to PF
strategies that "poorly imitate" Policy/LD or that ignore the resolution's actual question. Final
speeches should dramatically condense the debate — wider final speeches correlate with losses in
his view. Good for topicality with an evidence-based interpretation. Fine for theory but
reasonability threshold drops as an objection gets more contrived; skeptical of theory untethered
from in-round substance; tired of disclosure debates going beyond a binary yes/no. On weighing:
try-or-die "has gotten out of control" — a link is required to win, weighing follows from
strength of link rather than the reverse. Wants back-half speeches to substantively answer
arguments, not just weigh past them.

### Sripad Yadagiri
**Format:** Judges both HS Public Forum and college Policy.
Starts speaks at 28 (not a 30-truther) and moves debaters up/down from there based on evidence
comparison, smart cross-application, and identifying contradictions — no per-argument-type
preferences. Claims require warrants; incomplete arguments aren't evaluated even if conceded, but
once a complete argument is established, extending the claim alone is enough — no need to
re-explain a conceded complete argument in full. Warrant bar is explicitly low: the test is
"why is [claim] true?" answered by "[warrant]," judged as if by an experienced-but-topic-unfamiliar
audience member. Rewards a team that correctly flags an opponent's argument as unwarranted and
strategically declines to answer it (efficiency). Won't evaluate new arguments in the final
speech unless the prior speech explicitly flagged them as new. Low familiarity with
non-consequentialist frameworks, permissibility, or most applications of presumption beyond "aff
solvency is deleted" as a neg win condition — flag this if running unusual framework material.

### Jason Zhao
**Format:** PF.
Tech > truth if you can warrant it; wants comparative weighing. Warrants "start at 0, not 100" —
thinly/loosely-highlighted cards get little weight, and a case built mostly on such cards invites
an unusually low response threshold. Speed is fine for depth, not breadth — wants pen time
between arguments and slower tags; caps speaks at 29.5 for poor spreading. Tags must be
descriptive ("indeed, thus, and" don't count) and is receptive to "not in the tag, high threshold"
arguments. Extensions exist for clash clarity, not box-checking, and — notably — thinks extending
in rebuttal is outright unnecessary. First Final Focus weighing is fine, earlier is better; no new
weighing in second FF; defaults negative on presumption absent argument. Won't evaluate new
defense disguised as "probability weighing." Reasonability > CI, pro-disclosure, anti-frivolous
theory by default (mirrors Ishan Dubey closely on theory). On Ks: over-explain, fully impact out,
any response strategy is fine, better than most for framework/topicality responses to a K-aff.

### Avi Shah
**Format:** Policy (CX).
Tech over truth "in every instance." Will judge-kick conditional counterplans even unprompted,
and protects the negative from new 2AR arguments at his own discretion (otherwise it's on the
debaters to flag new args). Flows on computer, straight down, one constructive + one rebuttal per
debater; doesn't flow the 1AC but flows the entire 1NC; uses the doc to backfill his flow after a
speech, not live. On framework/planless-affs: good for "quick tricks" from either side if
identified and engaged; wants final rebuttals to resolve what "model of debate" the ballot is
supposedly creating, and rewards clash-forward strategic arguments over pure fairness-only
framing. On topicality: sympathetic to "plan in a vacuum," and has a strong initial presumption
that predictability outweighs debatability. On Ks: no ideological bias against them, best when
they moot the plan via an alternate framework (not dressed as a CP); will default to permutations
if the K team doesn't do this; wants K links matched to the actual framework interpretation being
read, and finds mismatched link/FW combinations hard to evaluate. On CPs: functional competition
alone is slightly more defensible than textual+functional; near-zero patience for "positional
competition only." Speaker points reward answering in presentation order, identifying win
conditions/relevant portions of the debate explicitly, and numbering arguments for a clean flow.

### Archan Sen
**Format:** Policy (CX), also judges LD.
Tech over truth, framed explicitly as "debate is a game, optimize for ballots" — rewards
strategic decisions over showy "bravery." Guaranteed-30 conditions exist (hyper-specific plan
text, zero prep taken, a sub-30-second winning 2XR, a genuinely novel argument, or a minimalist
1NC of ≤1 CP + DAs + case turns). On K/FW/K-Affs: good for technical K teams that treat the K as
a tool with independent win conditions, bad for teams that try to "ethos" past a technical drop;
views fairness as close to the only impact he finds coherent for framework, and is skeptical of
a vision of debate where the aff always wins. Wants more substantive answers to K-affs beyond
framework (DAs to the method, word PICs, impact turns) — lenient on link scrutiny there. On
topicality: doesn't see a real alternative to "plan text in a vacuum"; substance crowd-out is
reasonability's only real cost, and he's not convinced it's actually bad. On CPs: skeptical of
most CP theory as ad-hoc/incoherent, prefers competition-based objections instead; condo is the
one exception — thinks teams should go for it more, and neg flex is the strongest defense of it.
Impact turns are a personal favorite (heg bad, dedev, spark, wipeout) provided the cards are
current. For LD specifically: wants a clean off-time roadmap naming flows in order, explicit
signposting between flows, and treats a 2NR that's just "CP + DA" or "DA + case" with no
substantive engagement as a 29-speaks ceiling.

### Rafael Pierry
**Format:** Policy (CX).
Tech over truth without carve-outs — will vote on an argument regardless of its real-world
plausibility unless Tabroom forces otherwise; does not discriminate between arguments he
personally finds "real" and "not real." Lower-than-average bar for a warrant — unwarranted
arguments are easily beaten by contextualization or cross-application, so the correct response is
to say so, not expect the judge to reject it. Protects against new arguments if a team flags them
(except the 2AR, which he protects unprompted since neg can't respond). On planless affs: prefers
fairness or skills-framework impacts equally; wants a genuine topicality response, not just
"we're critical so it doesn't apply." On Ks: best when framed as an alternative evaluative
framework, not causal/CP-flavored; will resolve the framework debate for one side rather than
split an arbitrary middle ground. On topicality: defaults offense/defense, no strong standards
bias, reasonability is winnable but must be offensively developed. On theory/CPs: most CP theory
reads as self-serving/contrived to him (any CP "has a process," etc.); defaults to limitless
conditionality, strongest as a 1NC default and moderate in the block; process CPs and competition-
based strategies are treated more charitably than community consensus usually allows.

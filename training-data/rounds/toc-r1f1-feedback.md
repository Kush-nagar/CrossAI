This round was debated on the April 2026 Public Forum topic (presidential war
powers). AFF (Fairmont) won this debate.

NEG Feedback:

A few things the NEG could’ve done better.

First, the only credible defense on the NEG case is that your argument lacks link evidence because the website in question is broken. You have a way around this - when the AFF originally makes this response, the implication is to treat your link as an analytic. If the AFF has agreed to this, your response should just be that even if your link is analytical, it’s warranted, meaning it should still be evaluated. Even if the lack of evidence makes your link less credible, there’s no defense to your link, so the concession makes it true. Instead, the response you make is that “time travel is fiated”, meaning there’s no other way for the resolution to occur. The NEG has a response here that even if time travel is the only hypothetical way the resolution could occur, there’s no evidence that it’s possible. This all boils down to the evidence question, which you aren’t resolving.

Second, while your weighing push is diverse, it should be more warranted. You’re throwing around claims like “our timeframe is immediate” without enough warranting. Yes, the process of fiat is immediate, but that only explains why your link has immediate timeframe, not your impact. If that impact is what you’re weighing, then there is a mismatch between your impact and the weighing that you’re going for.

Third, you need to be answering the AFF’s weighing better. You make a metaweighing claim, which is efficient in that it allows you to filter out certain pieces of offense, but the fact that you’re conceding their actual weighing claims proper means that you’re pinning all of your hope on one piece of metaweighing, which is especially problematic given that your opponents have the 2nd final focus to parse through the weighing page. The best way to protect your 1st final focus is by contesting their individual claims, even if the responses are blippy, because it makes the job of the 2nd final focus much harder. Absent contestation, 2nd final focus was able to extend their conceded weighing and then spend a ton of time refuting your single metaweighing claim, putting them vastly ahead at the very end of the round.

AFF Feedback:

A few things the AFF could’ve done better.

First, your coverage on the NEG case in rebuttal needed to be much better. You overcover the first two contentions, slightly undercover the third contention, and barely cover the fourth contention. While it seems appealing to fully neutralize two out of four arguments, the fact that the last two are undercovered makes it very easy for the NEG to quickly collapse and generate a strong time tradeoff. This is especially an issue given that the fourth NEG contention is so squirrelly (time travel), making it a major threat if not answered sufficiently. I’d almost rather you go bottom-up on their case, or at least keep a better watch on your timer so that you’re splitting time more evenly between arguments.

Second, you’re spending too much time in the back-half of the round on responses that you never invested enough time into initially. For example, when answering the NEG time travel argument in 2nd final focus, you’re extending a response that indicts their Brazil empiric. This response doesn’t get you anywhere - it doesn’t meaningfully contest the NEG link nor their weighing, even if the response was technically conceded. When deciding what responses to extend in the back-half, you need to keep your win conditions in mind, asking yourself whether the responses you want to go for are contributing to those win conditions. Just because a response is conceded, it doesn’t mean that it’s the right response to go for.

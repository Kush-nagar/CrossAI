This round was debated on the April 2026 Public Forum topic (presidential war
powers). NEG (Lawrenceville) won this debate.

AFF Feedback:

A few things the AFF could’ve done better.

First, while the AFF summary is truth level ahead on Kant, it needs to rely fary less on buzzwords and hand-waving away arguments that admittedly do seem a bit silly. I appreciate the attention to detail when you’re extending the framework, but the one part of the speech that does need to solely focused on the LBL is when you’re frontlining your offense, and this speech just doesn’t have enough of that. You’re missing answers to a number of NEG responses - Congress isn’t a reflection of the will of the people, 500 members of Congress aren’t distinct from 1 President, etc.

Second, there needs to be more weighing between pieces of offense throughout the back-half. I understand that this isn’t normally a big part of Kant debates, but when the NEG is going for offense on two layers (Kant and utilitarianism), you need to be filtering out offense via weighing to make your job easier in the back-half. The NEG may have many different routes to the ballot, but they’re all underdeveloped, so focusing more on weighing is the best way to avoid getting spread thin. Don’t just weigh between frameworks, weigh between pieces of offense under those frameworks as well - cover all your bases.

NEG Feedback:

A few things the NEG could’ve done better.

First, I understand that this framework debate has you a bit confused, so you’re attempting to throw as many things at the wall as possible and hope one of them sticks. This is a perfectly fine strategy in rebuttal - it allows you to conserve prep and stretches their summary thin. But once you get to the back-half, you need to slow down and figure out what each of these responses mean in terms of their implication to the ballot. If you can’t figure out why a response wins you the debate, don’t go for it even if it seems like you’re winning it, because it won’t matter in my evaluation. For example, this “turn” that Kant collapses to consequences. It might seem like a turn because it shows a contradiction, but at the end of the day, it’s just defense - “their framework collapses to ours”.

Second, you should be leveraging cross much better. If you don’t understand the intricacies of Kant, ask as many clarifications as possible. What you end up doing is trying and getting them on technicalities - while this might be good for your ethos, it doesn’t matter as much in a technical debate, and you need to be cognizant of the fact that they’re more likely to be right than you are given your lack of baseline knowledge.

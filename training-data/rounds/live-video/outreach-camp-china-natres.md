## Patterns observed

- **A two-extinction round decided on one link race, not magnitude — clean as a
  joint structure.** Both cases terminalize in nuclear extinction (Con: NoKo
  famine/nuclear [33:29]; Pro: great-power/nuclear-winter [09:05], [39:37]), so
  the ballot turns on the **NoKo internal link** — whether reducing China's
  *international* extraction actually cuts the oil NoKo depends on. Whoever wins
  that link "wins the comparative link into a framing both teams have agreed
  controls" (+0.7). The whole legible back half is a fight over one card's
  geography.

- **An evidence indict executed on the opponent's own card in cross — clean
  (Pro), under-banked (Pro).** Pro command-Fs the Con's link card live ("command
  F to secure access to oil… only… South Sudan" [30:06]–[30:14]) and turns the
  Con's own dissad card against its link ("your own evidence… [a] mine in a
  Chinese province" [30:15]–[30:24]) — the round's cleanest "+0.7 evidence indict
  with the actual text quoted." It converts on the flow as the link half of the
  NoKo defense, but the **grand-cross impact-scenario mismatch** ("no internal
  link to Great Power War" [45:04]) is never re-run in the final focus — the
  +0.7 there decays toward the "asserted but never fleshed out" 0. Cross wins
  with a one-speech shelf life unless re-executed on the flow.

- **A decisive collapse landing on a contested link — path-of-least-resistance
  and all-in at once (Con).** Con does the textbook thing: kicks the
  straight-turned Africa DA ("We're not going for this DA" [33:06]) and collapses
  to one voter with weighing opened first (+1.0 timeframe, [34:31]). But the one
  voter is the exact link Pro spent **both** crossfires dismantling, and Con
  patches the cross-exposed domestic-oil hole with a **new card in summary**
  ("China 1025… offshore drilling" [34:06]) rather than resolving it. "Collapses
  to an argument whose central evidence-credibility dispute was left unresolved"
  (−0.6), and the strong try-or-die weighing is all-in on that link (−0.5) — the
  reason clean collapse mechanics do not simply close the round.

- **Timeframe/try-or-die weighing that actually interacts — partial (both).**
  Con opens the gap first and correctly ("first on time frame… their arguments
  take longer[:] countries have to plan a war" [34:31]–[35:16], +1.0), and Pro

  contested link, and Pro's "if I win timeframe it's sufficient" [50:50] over-

- **The new / kicked / dropped procedural as the round's shared dialect —
  partial (both).** Both final focuses run the new-in-the-back-half discount

  going for the window scenario" [21:14]) lets Con claim "you can't extend an

### Round-distinctive execution traits

1. **The first-speaking team is the NEG, and its collapse target is a disad it
   built in the 1st constructive.** Unlike a standard Pro-leads round, Team A
   reads pure disadvantages ("affirming cuts costs, killing North Korea['s] oil"
   [05:19]) and rides the NoKo DA — with a dated, energy-shortage trigger — all
   the way to the ballot, kicking its Africa DA and never engaging the Pro's fish
   flow at all. The whole round is organized around one team's constructive
   disad rather than a clash of two affirmative-vs-negative cases.

2. **The decision rides on one card's geography.** Both sides read nuclear
   extinction, so magnitude is a wash and the ballot reduces to a single
   question — does reducing China's *international* extraction reach the oil NoKo
   runs on, when the Con's own link card localizes that oil to a domestic Chinese
   field ([30:15])? A rare case where a corpus round collapses cleanly to a
   single, checkable evidentiary fact rather than a weighing vibe.

3. **Cross wins outrunning their conversion, on both sides.** Pro wins the NoKo
   link in the 2nd crossfire and the impact-scenario mismatch in grand cross, but
   banks only the link half in the final focus; Con wins the timeframe framing in
   summary but rests it on the link Pro took. Each team generates decisive-looking
   cross material and leaves part of it on the table — the corpus's recurring
   "cross output has a one-speech shelf life" pattern, here symmetrical.

4. **A camp tech-demo's transparency is itself analyzable.** The 225 WPM cap, the
   docs "with all analytics" [01:11], the announced "I'll slow down for the back
   half" [32:40], the Speech Drop "read version vs. cards" confusion [26:55], even
   a debater's pre-round "I'm a bit washed" [02:21] — the scaffolding a real round
   hides is on tape. The analytic payoff is a **legible back half where the
   decision turns on carded link warrants**, giving this file a much cleaner
   evidentiary read than the anonymous fast-circuit tapes elsewhere in this corpus.

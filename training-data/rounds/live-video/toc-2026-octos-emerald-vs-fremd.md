## 1. AFF 1st Constructive — [01:38]–[06:04]

**Near-total ASR paraphrase; shell architecture recoverable.** The AFF reads a
complete **Open Source disclosure theory shell**, then a case.

- **Interpretation** [01:38]–[02:03]: debaters "must properly open source [their]
  **cards and highlights** [with] the exact words [read]… [on] all broken [and]
  non-broken constructive positions… [on] the **2025–26 PF NDCA wiki**, under
  [the] correct **team code and school**, at least **15 [/30] minutes before the
  round**… **it's [the] cut cards, [it] clarifies, not full text**."
- **Violation** [02:03]–[02:05]: "**the full text** — check [the] screenshots
  [of] the wiki inserted below. You click the link and check if you need to."
- **Five standards** [02:07]–[03:18]:
  1. **Prep skew** — "ensures that we have time to look at and answer your
     arguments… we can't prep for years in 30 minutes" [02:08]–[02:13].
  2. **Evidence ethics** — "**full text prevents checking every card for
     miscutting, editing, abusive brackets or ellipses, or lack of [omitted]
     words**… that is the **worst fairness violation because it's definitionally
     cheating and a prerequisite to evaluating anything else, because [we can]not
     trust you or your evidence**" [02:18]–[02:29].
  3. **Ableism / accessibility** — "open source is the most fair for debaters with
     **dyslexia**, which links to… safety and fairness because they're pushing
     people out of the [activity]… **Evans 19**" [02:30]–[02:40].
  4. **Small schools** — "full text [helps] big schools… **key to teaching
     novices proper card cutting**, since they [need] a real model for
     highlight[ing]" [02:41]–[02:52].
  5. **Ground** — "**open source creates predictable ground because they know the
     exact warrants [and] highlights [we'll] read**… **full text explodes
     ground, because we don't know which portions of the article you'll use until
     the round starts**… people just won't bother with [your] disclosure if they
     can't interact with your specific warrant… **you should gut-check this even
     if the arguments sound good in theory[;] it's just not how people react when
     they open 60 pages of article text**" [02:52]–[03:18].
- **Voters and paradigm issues** [03:18]–[03:47]: **fairness** ("competitive
  equity, key to participation… and a **prior question** because [it] invalidates
  the rest of the [flow] if the debate was unfair"); **drop the debater**
  ("**deterrence prevents [them] doing the abusive practice in the future**…
  it's not worth risking the loss[;] **B, it's a time [trade]** — otherwise
  [they] read a bunch of abusive practices for the time tradeoff");
  **competing interps > reasonability** ("**reasonability encourages a race to
  the margins of what counts as sufficiently fair, which incentivizes as much
  abuse as possible in the round**"); and **no RVIs** with three warrants
  ("it deters good theory because [you'd] lose on a shell even if it's a good
  norm"; "baiting"; "it's a logical [non sequitur] to win [by] proving [you're]
  fair").
- **The no-new-answers spike** [03:47]–[04:09]: "**they must make every response
  and offensive argument to theory, including a counter-interp, in the second
  constructive**" — with three warrants: **theory is the highest layer** ("we
  might respond [on] substance and they can scope [out] for theory, making it
  useless"), **resolvability** ("otherwise we have less speeches… forcing judge
  intervention… A, it destroys the basis of theory debates, and B, takes the
  debate out of our hands"), and **reciprocity** ("we invested [heavily] into
  theory in the constructive, so you should do the same").
- **The case** [04:14]–[06:04]: C1 hyper war — military AI integration, autonomous
  systems removing human oversight, AI-decided strikes, US norm-setting; solvency
  through "strong congressional oversight [that] repeals [the] previous AUMF"
  [04:54]; a nuclear-calibration scenario ("Orlando" [05:24]) with "95% of
  simulations" escalating. **C3 is cut for time** ([05:53]).

- **A complete, technically well-built shell.** Interp with a specified platform,
  timing, and format; a violation with screenshot evidence; five differentiated
  standards (two fairness-internal, one accessibility, one small-school, one
  ground); voters; and every major paradigm issue pre-empted. This is the
  reference implementation of shell architecture in this corpus.
- **The pre-emption is the strategic choice, and it is aggressive.** By reading
  competing-interps, no-RVIs, DTD, *and* a "all theory answers in the 2nd
  constructive" spike, the AFF tries to foreclose the opponent's whole procedural
  toolkit before it is used. Two of these pre-empts turn out to be **unused
  time**: the NEG never runs an RVI, and it **concedes competing interps
  outright** ([40:39]). Reading pre-empts cheaply as time-sucks is the "0"
  midpoint; here the no-RVI block and the competing-interps block are read at
  real length against a team that was never going to contest them.
- **Reading a theory shell *and* a case in the first constructive costs the
  case.** C3 is cut ([05:53]) and the case is never extended again by either
  team. The AFF effectively spends its constructive buying a procedural round —
  a coherent choice, but it means the shell must win, because there is no
  substantive fallback.

## 3. 1st Crossfire — [11:04]–[14:32]

Legible, and unusually consequential — two of the round's live disputes are set
here.

- **Fairness as a meta-constraint** [11:07]–[12:14]: NEG — "why is topical debate
  valuable?" — AFF probes back and the NEG lays out the **fairness paradox**:
  "everyone relies on fairness because it's **intrinsic to the activity** — the
  game, strikes, spread… and **every response relies on fairness to be evaluated,
  because otherwise you're justified hacking against the argument unfairly, which
  means it has to be a [meta] constraint on any other argument**" [11:29]–[11:44].
  AFF presses the obvious counter — "should we have a topical debate if the
  debate itself was unfair?" — and the NEG relocates rather than answers: "**we
  have contested your ability to say we were unfair in the first place, because
  that's unpredictable out of round and not something that we can predictably
  answer in the speech**" [12:01]–[12:10].
- **The accessibility exchange — the round's sharpest** [12:15]–[13:05]: NEG —
  "**why didn't you contact us if you had accessibility concerns** about
  disability on the open source shell?" — AFF — "[we] contacted you before and
  I've read this on you before and you didn't change your norms" — NEG —
  "**I've never debated you, bro**" [12:25] — AFF — "I've debated [others]…" —
  NEG — "**I don't know how that's possible, man**" [12:34]. Then the substantive
  clash: AFF — "**I shouldn't have to tell you to not be ableist. You should just
  not be ableist.** Just because your wiki says 'contact us so we know not to be
  ableist,' it does not mean… we should have gone, 'oh, they said we have to
  contact them'… **there's no reason why this solves**" [12:49]–[13:03]; NEG —
  "we haven't said you should contact us for **being ableist**. We have said
  people have thousands of accessibility concerns… **for example, we didn't know
  that full text is ableist or isn't**" [13:05]–[13:19].
- **The ground standard pressed** [13:30]–[14:32]: AFF — "you said teams will
  research and read the articles more… **insofar as you've dropped that judges
  should gut-check this because people actually see 60 pages of text and go 'oh
  hell no'**… explain why this argument should still be relevant" [13:30]–[14:07];
  NEG — "**this one sounds like a cop-out**" / AFF — "**it's a cop-out that you
  dropped**" / NEG — "**we get new answers [in] the next speech, so I haven't
  dropped it**" — AFF — "**you've dropped it**" [14:08]–[14:15].

- **The "have we debated before?" dispute is an unresolvable out-of-round factual
  claim inside a theory debate**, and it matters structurally: the AFF's
  "you didn't change your norms" is a **deterrence/commodification** warrant for
  drop-the-debater, and it rests on a claim the NEG flatly denies on tape.
  Neither side resolves it, and — tellingly — **both later adopt
  "out-of-round claims are non-verifiable" as a weapon** (see Patterns).
- **The [14:08] exchange is the whole round in ten seconds**: whether an argument
  was *dropped* depends entirely on who wins the **contested paradigm issue**
  about when theory answers are due. This is meta-theory doing real work — the
  no-new-answers spike is not decoration, it determines what the flow says.
- **The accessibility exchange is a genuine criterion clash, not a generic
  appeal.** The AFF argues the burden shouldn't fall on the affected debater
  ("I shouldn't have to tell you"); the NEG argues its interp *solicits* the
  information and the AFF didn't use it (a solvency/commodification answer) and
  **counter-turns on the merits** (highlights hurt colorline and dyslexic
  debaters). Both sides contest the criterion directly (+0.3 side of that row)
  rather than trading analogies.

### Round-distinctive execution traits

1. **The only round in this corpus with zero substantive clash.** The topic is
   read once ([04:14]–[06:04], with a contention cut for time) and never
   mentioned again by either side. Three procedural sheets — an OS shell, a T
   shell against it, and a full-text counter-interp — consume all eleven speeches.
   For a corpus otherwise full of extinction-vs-extinction weighing, this file is
   the reference sample of what a **pure theory round** looks like end to end,
   including its characteristic pathology: both teams end up arguing about *what
   was said* at least as much as about *what is true*.

2. **Both teams claim the same standard in opposite directions.** Evidence ethics
   is the AFF's collapse ("full text prevents checking for miscutting" [02:18])
   *and* a NEG standard ("full text forces you to read the whole article… teams
   unquestioningly read bad evidence from good teams' wikis" [08:34]). The 2nd
   crossfire nearly surfaces why — **open source verifies which words were read;
   full text verifies what the article says** ([27:19]–[27:46]) — but neither team
   says it, and both instead claim their model checks everything. The round's
   biggest missed clarity, and the reason the evidence-ethics debate ends in
   dueling assertions.

3. **A layered strategy narrated aloud.** "**If we win T, double-down is
   non-topical and should be presumed false. If we lose T, we go to the norming
   debate. If we lose that, we [say] it works the other way around. There's just
   a series of layers**" [26:29]–[26:46] — a team explaining its own conditional
   architecture in crossfire. Strategically generous (it hands over the map) but
   analytically the clearest statement of **layered theory** in this corpus, and
   the reason the NEG's back half is legible despite the speed.

4. **Two unresolvable out-of-round factual disputes inside a round about
   disclosure.** Whether these teams have debated before ("**I've never debated
   you, bro**" [12:25]) and whether top card-cutters draw evidence challenges
   ([26:57]–[28:00]) both go unresolved — and both matter, because the first is
   the AFF's deterrence warrant and the second is the NEG's evidence-ethics

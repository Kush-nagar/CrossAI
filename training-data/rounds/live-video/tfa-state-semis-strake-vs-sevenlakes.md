### Round-distinctive execution traits

1. **A politics DA versus a structural advantage — the ballot runs through
   incommensurable layers on purpose.** NEG's offense is electoral probability
   (Act → GOP win → Golden Dome → extinction); AFF's is social structure
   (corporate ownership → dead communities → dead democracy → can't solve
   anything). Neither impact is the other's mirror, so weighing is comparative
   by necessity, not by choice — and the teams mostly meet it, which is why the
   round is closer to interacting than washing.

2. **The strongest single move is a concession, not a card.** AFF wins its
   whole rebuttal by *giving up* the fight NEG prepared for — severing the
   advantage from affordability and daring NEG to turn a harm that doesn't
   depend on price. NEG never re-tools; its second-half turns are still pitched
   at a price debate AFF exited at [15:03].

3. **Both teams weaponize the opponent's own cards over reading new ones.**
   AFF turns NEG's Harrison (Golden Dome won't be built) and calls a double-turn
   out of NEG's 5×-worse-economy card against NEG's midterms; NEG turns AFF's
   own Kramer-Kemp/Bapari deck against the democracy internal link
   (Porter/Gibbon as the filter that AFF's cards can't survive). The back half
   recycles material rather than extends new offense.

4. **A cross-conversion asymmetry decides the crystallization race.** Both
   sides generate a decisive-looking crossfire win on the other's weakest link;
   only AFF banks its (Harrison → the FF's spine). NEG spends grand cross and
   two crossfires winning that AFF's internal link is empirically false, then
   spends its final focus on the eight-link Golden Dome chain and leaves the
   internal-link takeout un-collapsed. The clean voter (the Porter/Gibbon
   filter) is the one NEG under-invests in.

5. **Try-or-die inflation on both sides, and the probability debate stays
   under-warranted where it's actually decided.** NEG: "any risk of uniqueness"
   on a 36–50% uniqueness and an eight-link chain. AFF: "structural harm 100%
   happens" on an internal link it never warranted past the joint NEG pinned in
   the first crossfire. Each team claims the safe side of a risk it hasn't
   grounded; the round's true fulcrum — does democracy-engagement actually solve
   the terminal impact, and does the Golden Dome actually get built — is argued
   least rigorously by both.

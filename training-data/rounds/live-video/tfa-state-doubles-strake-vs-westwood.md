## 1. NEG 1st Constructive — [00:01]–[04:06]

**Heavily garbled; architecture recoverable.** Three layers, read in order.

- **Truth-testing ROTB** [00:01]–[00:33]: "the role of the ballot is to determine
  the truth or fals[ity] of the resolution," with three warrants —
  **jurisdiction** ("a resolution declares something… it can be true or false…
  [the judge's] jurisdiction… [is] the constitutive rules [of] the activity"
  [00:04]–[00:15]); **debatability** ("only [a] true/false binary [gives a
  standard] for determining the winner — other metrics like desirability
  necessitate judge intervention [about] what's sufficient" [00:17]–[00:23]); and
  **regress** ("every role of the ballot requires saying something that is true or
  false in order to provide a win condition, [and] the resolution is the only
  non-arbitrary stasis point" [00:25]–[00:31]).
- **Skepticism — six independent warrants** [00:41]–[02:04]: (1) **deception /
  evil demon** ("you['re] being deceived by [a] demon… manipulat[ing] your sensory
  impressions… how can you know the world outside your skin?" [00:47]–[00:57]);
  (2) **epistemic regress** ("to come to any conclusion we must have a valid
  supporting claim, but there's no foundational one… P1 [holds] only if there's a
  P2… only if there's a P3" [00:58]–[01:14]); (3) **external world** ("all we know
  about the world comes to us via light waves and electrical impulses running at a
  finite speed — a distant mountain, the not-very-distant light fixture on the
  ceiling, all [are] past times you are no longer part of" [01:24]–[01:33]);
  (4) **Zeno's paradox** ("to go anywhere you must go halfway first, then half of
  the remaining distance… thus action is impossible" [01:33]–[01:45]);
  (5) **determinism** ("we're a collection of molecules… free will require[s] us
  to step outside of our brain structures[;] science doesn't show we can"
  [01:52]–[01:58]); and (6) **linguistic skepticism** ("every statement… rel[ies]
  on words with infinite mean[ings] to define them. **'Single' in the resolution
  also means a one dollar bill, which means you have no clue what the aff [is]**"
  [01:58]–[02:04]).
- **The absolute-truth burden + presumption** [02:04]–[02:57]: "**absolute truth
  [is] necessary**" — "if any contrary hypothesis I cannot rule out, I am not
  justified… [that] is all the skeptic needs to show" (Sinnott-Armstrong 24
  [02:06]–[02:12]); "**truth is an absolute burden** — anywhere [it's] wrong is a
  neg ballot" [02:13]; "'**resolved**' means settled, finished, with no need to
  revisit… 'should' is mandatory and expresses moral duty… proving the obligatory
  nature of the resolution is a negative win condition" [02:27]–[02:42]; plus the
  **burden of proof** ("the burden [is on] the aff to prove the resolution — if
  they fail, they lose" [02:42]–[02:48]) and **presumption** ("the current system
  works sufficient[ly] until proven otherwise" [02:52]–[02:57]).
- **The dollar DA** [03:00]–[04:06]: aff → investor flight → home prices fall →
  housing collateral (two-thirds of assets backing global finance) erodes → dollar
  loses primacy → "only sudden shocks guarantee de[dollarization]… otherwise
  extinction" [03:53]–[04:00].

- **This is a complete truth-testing/skep trigger package**, and it is
  architecturally sound: the ROTB establishes that only truth matters, the six
  warrants establish that nothing can be known, and the absolute burden converts
  "any doubt" into a neg ballot. Crucially, **the six warrants are independent**
  — the aff must beat all of them, and beating five leaves the sixth sufficient.
  Reading six independent, individually-cheap warrants against a burden that only
  needs one is an efficient time trade by construction.
- **The linguistic warrant is the sleeper**, and the neg knows it — it is the one
  the neg collapses to in the final focus ([32:07]), precisely because it is the
  hardest to answer from *inside* a framework that depends on defining terms
  ("God," "objective," "Catholicism"). Planting a warrant whose target is the
  opponent's own definitional apparatus is front-half round vision.
- **The DA is dead on arrival and should probably not have been read.** It costs
  roughly a minute of a four-minute constructive in a round the neg intends to win
  on epistemology, and neither team touches it again. In a round this
  time-constrained, that is a minute the skep warrants could have used.

  aff attacks the warrant on its own terms.
- **But the aff has built a burden it cannot discharge under the neg's frame.**
  Its route to God is *evidentiary* (miracles, fine-tuning, historical
  testimony) — and the neg's regress and external-world warrants attack the

- **The Fátima pre-empts are the aff's best construction work.** Reading the
  naturalistic counter-explanations and answering them *inside the constructive*
  is "case construction predicts the common responses" (+0.9 direction) — and it
  pays: the neg never contests the miracle evidence on its merits, only through
  the meta-level skep warrants.

- **The neg runs its own warrant against the aff's framework live on tape**, and
  the aff has no answer in the moment. This is the strongest cross exchange in
  the file: rather than asserting the regress, the neg **instantiates** it against
  the specific proposition the aff needs. The neg banks it in every later speech

## Patterns observed

- **A truth-testing + skepticism trigger built to be un-outrun — six independent
  warrants against a burden that needs one.** The neg's package is
  architecturally efficient by construction: a ROTB establishing that only truth
  matters, **six independent skep warrants** ([00:41]–[02:04]), and an
  **absolute-truth burden** that converts any surviving doubt into a ballot
  ("for a statement to be labeled true, you cannot be even the slightest bit
  skeptical" [22:38]). The aff must beat all six; beating five leaves the sixth
  sufficient. Reading many cheap independent warrants against a burden of
  certainty is the structural insight this file documents.

- **The aff concedes truth-testing while running a risk framing — the round's
  decisive incompatibility.** "**We're going to concede the truth-testing
  argument**" [15:26] accepts a burden requiring 100% certainty; "**if there's a
  risk that God is real, you should auto-affirm**" [27:03], [35:01] claims only
  probability. The neg cashes it in one line — "**they've dropped [Unger]…
  proving that you need 100% to affirm**" [24:28] — and the aff's final focus
  restates the wager without repairing it. **Conceding a layer is a positive time
  trade only if your own offense survives the layer you conceded**; here the
  concession retired the aff's win condition.

- **The regress instantiated live in crossfire, then banked — clean (NEG).**
  Rather than asserting the epistemic-regress warrant, the neg runs it against
  the aff's specific proposition on tape: "**P1 is 'God is objective.' Do you have
  P2?**" — "**what**" [10:53]–[11:13]. The aff eventually supplies a P2 in the 2nd
  summary ("every creator is seen in the forces of the world — fine-tuning"
  [25:52]), three speeches later, which is itself an evidentiary claim the
  regress then reaches. **Cross output re-executed on the flow** (+0.4 at full
  leverage) — and one of the few instances in this corpus where a crossfire
  *demonstration*, not just a question, does the work.

- **Collapse selection by structural compatibility — clean (NEG).** Of six
  warrants the neg keeps exactly three: **external world, linguistics, and
  epistemic regress** ([22:48]–[23:52]) — the three a God-framework **cannot**
  absorb (they concern perception, language, and justification), dropping the
  three it can (evil demon, Zeno, determinism — all answerable by "God grounds
  existence"). Then in the final focus it narrows further to **linguistics**
  alone [32:07], aiming at the aff's *definitional apparatus* rather than its
  metaphysics. **Taking the path of least resistance** (+0.9) executed with real
  discrimination about which of your own arguments the opponent has actually
  neutralized.

- **Faith as the one route that survives global skepticism — the aff's best
  argument, found too late.** "**Insofar as [skepticism] is the truth, you should
  probably just affirm, because having faith in God… does not rely on evidence…
  It's literally the only way to do anything if evidence does not exist**"
  [28:33]–[28:44]. This answers skepticism **on skepticism's own terms** — an
  even-if reframe that turns the opponent's strongest claim into a reason to vote
  aff (+0.5). It appears in the **last thirty seconds of the summary**, after two
  and a half minutes re-extending the evidentiary case skep had already
  neutralized. **A collapse-relevant argument whose setup was never done in time**
  (−0.7 to −0.3) — the single clearest "right idea, wrong speech" in this batch.

- **Mutual self-refutation, identified by both sides and resolved by neither.**
  Skepticism read *from evidence* is reflexively unstable, and both teams see it:
  the aff — "their case [does] not matter because it relies on evidence — **and
  our case also relies on evidence**" [28:33]; "**their response above is also
  circular**" [35:47]; "**this is self-defeating… neither side's argument
  [survives]**" [35:23]. The neg's answer is a **bootstrap** — "**you must
  evaluate our evidence to [reach] skep first**… then you negate on presumption"
  [33:20]–[33:36] — which is a respectable reductio but is asserted rather than
  defended as one. The round's deepest question is raised by both sides in the
  last four minutes and closed by neither.

- **Internal theological turns beat external ones — clean (NEG).** The neg's most
  effective Christianity answers use the aff's **own doctrine**: "**no terminal
  impact — all sinners go to hell anyway**" and "**solvency deficit — your own
  evidence says hell is avoided by belief in Jesus' name**, [not by a ballot]"
  [13:18]–[13:25]. The solvency deficit is never answered. Turning a framework
  with its own commitments is the theological analog of turning a card with its
  own text (+0.7 direction), and it outperforms the neg's external answers
  (declining religiosity, prayer studies), which the aff dispatches easily.

### Round-distinctive execution traits

1. **The most LD-flavored round in a Public Forum corpus.** Truth-testing,
   skepticism triggers, presumption, P1/P2/P3 regress notation, absolute burdens,
   the principle of double effect, omnipotence vs. omnibenevolence, original sin —
   and **the housing resolution is never debated on its merits by either side**.
   Both teams litigate the topic exclusively through their frameworks. For a
   corpus otherwise full of link chains and extinction weighing, this file is the
   reference sample of **philosophy-framework PF**.

2. **A framework that answers skepticism structurally rather than dismissing
   it.** The aff's God argument is the *correct shape* of answer to a regress —
   a foundation that terminates it — and the aff explicitly asks in cross which
   warrants it reaches ("does [objective morality] take out skepticism **other
   than the tricks and the paradoxes**?" [09:59]). The answer it should have drawn
   from its own question is that **Zeno and linguistics are not foundational
   problems** and so are not answered by a foundation — which is exactly where the
   neg collapses. Diagnosing which of an opponent's warrants your framework
   actually reaches, and pre-empting the ones it doesn't, is the transferable
   lesson.

3. **Wall-to-wall speech: 36:02 of file for ~35:00 of speaking.** Unlike every
   other file in this batch (48–52 minutes for the same speech time), this round
   uses essentially **no prep**. It shows in both directions: the density is
   extreme and the ASR suffers, but it also means every argument was constructed
   in advance — this is a round of pre-built blocks meeting pre-built blocks, with
   almost no in-round adaptation visible except the aff's late faith pivot.

4. **Certainty tested behaviorally in grand cross.** "**Are you 100% sure?**" —
   "**Yeah, 100%**" — "**so if I… [said], put a million dollars that the Fátima
   sun miracle [is real]?**" — "**yeah**" — "**put a billion?**" — "**yeah**" —
   "**okay, I'll put my [ballot]**" [31:07]–[31:26]. Converting an abstract
   epistemic claim into a wager is a genuinely clever probe, and it works because
   the aff had already over-committed to certainty ("the lack of responses means
   this is 100% true" [26:25]) under a burden it conceded. A model of how to
   cross-examine a certainty claim.

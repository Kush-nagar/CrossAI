  +0.8 row, both sides).** NEG explicitly names and defends its layer ("treat

## Round-distinctive execution traits

1. **A progressive round where *both* teams correctly play the layer game — and
   the layers actually interact.** Unlike a policy-style round decided on
   probability/timeframe, this is decided on *ordering*: safety-prerequisite vs.
   fairness-prior-question. Both sides name their highest layer and try to filter
   the other's, and — the distinctive part — the filters clash directly (NEG:
   "you can't evaluate T if we're excluded"; AFF: "T/fairness is prior to method
   goodness, and your own sexism premise makes the K self-defeating"). It is a
   working example of the round-vision framework's progressive section executed by
   both teams, versus the more common progressive round where one side simply
   under-covers the higher layer.

2. **The decisive turns are *self-contradiction* turns, not evidence turns.** The
   two biggest moves on the tape both weaponize the opponent's own commitments:
   NEG turns AFF's "you should engage nuclear energy" concession into the
   independent link, and AFF turns NEG's "debate is sexist" ontology into the
   judge-intervention paradox that makes the K self-undermining. Neither is
   carded; the round's central clash (fairness paradox vs. cognitive dissonance)
   is litigated entirely by analytics — the least evidence-disciplined flow is
   again the one that decides the round (mirroring the exemplar's ISR pattern).

3. **The substantive resolution is a ghost.** Neither team ever reads a nuclear-
   energy case or states the resolution; the topic exists only as the *object the
   K critiques* (the gender-and-the-bomb link) and the *thing T would force
   debate about*. This is the pure form of the "the K is about the debate space
   itself" round — contrast the philosophy-K rounds (where a framework governs an
   otherwise-substantive resolution) and policy-style rounds (where the resolution
   is the whole game). Here the resolution's only function is to give the K a
   topic-specific link and give T something to demand.

4. **"Conceded" is doing structural work for both sides against a contested
   record — and both over-reach.** NEG claims "no response to nor's impact"
   [44:03] and "they also conceded [the burden is on men]" [57:14]; AFF claims
   the counter-method "was just conceded the entire [round]" [60:57]. On the
   tape, each of these was at least touched by the other side. Where the claimed
   drop is *real* (AFF's engage-the-topic concession, AFF's "debate is sexist")
   it is decisive; where it isn't (the counter-method, nor's impact) it produces
   assertion loops — the shared −0.3 unwarranted-assertion tax recurring exactly
   as in the policy-style exemplar.

5. **Doc-integrity housekeeping is unusually visible.** Two on-tape episodes of
   cutting/sharing exact lines ("I cut the whole line in bold, then started
   reading again at T-answers-role-of-the-ballot" [15:47]–[15:53]; "there was a
   card… can you send that?" [27:11]–[28:54]) show the fast-circuit norm of exact
   evidence exchange — relevant to any future feature on evidence sharing, but
   not round argument, and excluded from the speech analysis above.

### Round-distinctive execution traits

1. **The round is won on the opponent's flow.** PRO's ballot story is not its
   own case — it is a turn plus a try-or-die built *on CON's UN-collapse
   contention* (broke-by-August + Reiter incentives), with Korea only as the
   backup path. Most rounds in this corpus collapse to the team's own best
   contention; this one shows the alternative: when the opponent's central
   flow has a conceded uniqueness problem, offense planted there is cheaper
   than anything on your side of the flow, because every speech the opponent
   spends extending their case extends your voter's relevance too.

2. **A filter maintained all round, lost at its edge case.** CON's
   "only great-power conflict is existential" overview successfully
   disciplined PRO's climate and WMD impacts (both die or get kicked) — and
   then PRO's final focus walked North-Korea-strikes-the-US *through* the
   filter instead of around it. Filters need boundary-guarding in the same way
   cards need recutting: the team that owns the frame must also own its
   classification calls before the last speech.

3. **Live evidence-accountability culture, unusually visible.** This tape
   captures the norms themselves: a marked-doc protocol ("I marked the doc
   where I caught it in red... The analytics weren't on the doc"
   [18:58]–[19:11]), mid-speech card cuts announced aloud ([15:47], [17:30],
   [24:37]), a stat requested and admitted unfound on the spot ([34:46]–
   [34:52]), and a card read back verbatim against its own team ("between the
   two Koreas, verbatim" [41:19–41:21]). The round's evidence disputes are
   litigated between speeches as much as within them — and the outcomes
   (WMD kick, hotline survival) track who converted those disputes onto the
   flow, not who won them in the hallway-audio layer.

### Round-distinctive execution traits

1. **Two K-affs in one batch, built on opposite solvency theories.** UKSO's K-aff
   claims a **topical we-meet** and runs "**fiat is illusory**" (offense is the
   in-round subjectivity shift); this aff is **openly non-topical in
   implementation** and grounds solvency in **what the ballot does to the
   activity** (ballots shape norms; the Towson precedent). The two files together
   are a clean study in the K-aff's central design choice — claim the topic and
   deny fiat, or deny the topic and claim the community — and in the different
   neg attacks each invites (ordering/we-meet contradiction vs. ballot-solvency
   turns and "give them 30 speaks instead").

2. **The whole round is a fight about what a ballot *does*, litigated
   empirically.** Unlike the corpus's usual framework debates (abstract limits vs.
   abstract education), both sides argue from **observable community trends**: the
   aff cites Towson 2014 and "debate now is unrecognizable from 40 years ago"
   [16:35]; the neg cites the proliferation of T-blocks and the opponents' own
   **transhumanism aff from a prior tournament** [24:00], [47:18]. A rare
   framework debate with checkable empirics on both sides — and the reason the
   trends exchange dominates both crossfires.

3. **"Give them 30 speaks instead" — solvency attacked without touching merits.**
   The neg's cheapest path is not to deny the aff's value but to deny the ballot's
   necessity: the aff's own card says the archive is "intrinsically valuable"
   ([10:33] read-it-and-lose), and speaker points can reward excellence ([25:44]).
   Attacking the **necessity of the ballot** rather than the merits of the
   advocacy is the path-of-least-resistance play against any K-aff whose offense is
   recognition-based — and here it is the neg's most under-extended good idea.

4. **A high-stakes identity-adjacent clash conducted almost entirely at the level
   of models.** Both teams repeatedly steer the dispute away from persons and
   toward **models and norms** — the neg's "do you think that *we* have been racist
   to you today in [this] round?" [43:03] and the aff's "it's not necessarily to be

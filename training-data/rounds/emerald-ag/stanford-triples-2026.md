Bhardwaj, Ken
I vote AFF to mitigate high-probability cyber harms that can directly wipe out people’s finances and destabilize basic safety.
On framework, NEG tries to re-orient the round around structural violence first and indicts util/extinction weighing as exclusionary but that argument doesn’t get them out of the AFF world for me. The AFF is explicitly telling a structural violence story too (i.e. financial ruin, poverty impacts from cyber exploitation), so even if I grant NEG’s lens, it doesn’t function as an AFF filter. Also I don't think NEG’s “we’re not doing extinction” cleanup out of the 1NR is a full fix like kicking court clog doesn’t magically erase that the initially operation was in big-scope impact framing while now claiming that kind of calculus is illegitimate.
In terms of offense access in the back half, NEG collapses hard to the black market arg and shoves their chips on microbetting/addiction/poverty. AFF cyber is conceded at the point of collapse and on my flow the 1NR doesn’t do the work to meaningfully answer or reduce that risk beyond a thin “historically we haven’t seen large attacks," which AFF answers with a threat is now framing (e.g. China Iran other actors adopting AI, etc.).
NEG’s black market offense doesn’t close the gap for me. I buy the AFF double turn thing NEG needs gambling to be normalized/increased by the plan but AFF gives a plausible deterrence story (i.e. FTC regulation signaling risk can reduce participation), which at minimum makes NEG’s link direction unstable. NEG never gives me a crisp comparison explaining why gambling SV outweighs a conceded cyber impact under either framework especially when AFF is also explicitly weighing cyber as structural violence (poverty/financial collapse). So even if I’m generous to the NEG link, they don’t win the comparative story

## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing
**ABSENT:** The case contains no explicit weighing mechanism—no magnitude, probability, timeframe, or scope comparisons between impacts. Cards present raw numbers (80k deaths, 4.9M jobs, 20x death increase) but never tie them to a weighing framework (e.g., "structural violence outweighs," "deaths precede economic impacts," "irreversible harm > reversible harm"). A card filling this gap would carry a tag like **"Weighing: Structural violence and loss of life outweigh economic disruption — magnitude (80k+ deaths), irreversibility, and moral priority precede trade impacts"** and be placed as an OV or Contention 1 subpoint to frame all subsequent extensions.

### Offensive spikes
**ABSENT:** No card directly preempts likely Pro positions such as "surveillance stops fentanyl," "technology reduces agent contact/mortality," "smart borders facilitate legal trade," or "deterrence works long-term." The case asserts deterrence fails (Chambers 19, Rumbaut 07, HRW 24) but does not anticipate and spike Pro's best offense. A spike card would read **"Surveillance expansion fails to interdict fentanyl — cartels adapt via tunnels, drones, corruption; CBP data shows record seizures *with* current tech, proving diminishing returns"** (cite: CBP 2024 or GAO 2023) and live in Contention 1 or a dedicated "Solvency Spike" block.

### Defensive spikes
**Chambers et al 19** — "Deterrence-based immigration policy creates a funneling effect where immigrants use different migration paths to avoid detection – These paths result in a 20x increase in death and disappearance" — This card functions as a embedded frontline against the inevitable Pro response "surveillance saves lives by deterring crossing." It preemptively warrants that *any* deterrence-based infrastructure (the Pro's mechanism) structurally increases mortality via geographic displacement, making the Pro's core solvency claim self-defeating.

### Evidence quality
**Human Rights Watch 24** — "Deterrence Kills 80k" — High-quality evidence: HRW is a globally recognized NGO with investigative capacity; the 2024 web feature cites Border Patrol data (10k reported) and local rights groups' estimate (80k), ground-truthed via mass graves, family testimony, and former agent accounts. The dataset spans 30 years (1994–2024), includes qualitative + quantitative strands, and is authored by a named researcher (Ari Sawyer). Wins evidence-comparison tiebreaks vs. think-tank op-eds or single-study cards.

### Collapse flexibility
**Solomon and Gottesdiener 23** — "Cycles of migration" — Provides an independent, non-funnel-effect link: cartel violence (not border policy) drives Mexican family migration (180k/yr, 4x increase, 88% violence-driven). This lets the Con collapse to **"Migration is push-driven; surveillance doesn't address root causes; Pro solves 0% of 180k families fleeing Chiapas turf wars"** if the funnel-effect link is neutralized. The card also doubles as a trade/econ internal link (violence → instability → trade disruption).

### Warrant depth
**Katy Murdza 2022** — "Empirics prove" — Tag claims a study on "virtual wall in Arizona" shows remains shift outside tech range onto harder routes. **However, the card quotes only the study's conclusion without naming the study, authors, methodology, sample size, or publication venue.** The warrant is an unsourced assertion ("one study found..."). A proper warrant would cite: **"Sanchez et al. 2022, 'Virtual Walls, Real Deaths,' *Journal of Border Studies*, n=1,200 remains over 5 years, GIS-matched to tower coverage, p<0.01."** As written, the card is claim + cite without a traceable warrant.

### Internal consistency
**ABSENT — Contention tension:** The case simultaneously argues (1) **Mexican economy is improving** (World Bank: 3.2% growth, poverty down 8.8M) and (2) **Low Mexican econ growth sends more migrants** (Rodriguez-Sanchez 23: "if lower rate of economic growth… low-income households may send more migrants"). These cards undercut each other — if the economy is improving, the Rodriguez-Sanchez push factor weakens; if the push factor is strong, the World Bank progress claims are overstated. No framing reconciles them (e.g., "growth is uneven; cartels capture gains"). A consistency card would read **"Mexican growth is cartel-captured — World Bank aggregates mask regional violence economies; Chiapas GDP stagnates while cartels extort 29% of municipalities (Montes 24)"** and sit in Contention 2.

### Overall construction score
**Score: 5/10** — The case assembles strong, recent evidence on funnel-effect mortality and cartel-driven migration (two cohesive narratives) but lacks embedded weighing, offensive spikes, and internal consistency; one key card (Murdza) has no traceable warrant; and the trade/econ contention fights itself. It functions as a solid evidence repository but not a strategically engineered case.

A migration funnel effect has resulted from border security operations at the U.S.-Mexico border. Security
forces deliberately amassed along established migration routes—that until the late 1990s were traditionally urban zones
posing relatively low physical crossing risk—in order to deter migration with the prospect of high risk crossings in more remote, rugged,
wilderness areas (Doty, 2011; USBP, 1994). Over the years, the deterrence orientation of border security funneled
undocumented migration into increasingly high-risk geography leading to ensuing discussions of the funnel effect centered on
the exceptional and structural violence of U.S. border control (Doty, 2011; Magaña, 2008). When the funnel effect was first identified, it
was mainly in relation to the number of found migrant bodies, those of individuals who died crossing
the border. After 2000, [as a result] migrant deaths along the border increased to hundreds per year,
most notably in Pima County, Arizona where an average of 181 deaths of migrants have been recorded per year from 2001 to 2016 (Humane Borders and PCOME,

2017; Martínez et al., 2013). These facts make the violence entailed in border security unavoidably a primary concern. But, they also lead to scholarly discussions
that unintentionally valorize U.S. border security as effective, if effectively deadly, with little possibility for the agentive actions of undocumented migrants to impact
outcomes due to their overwhelming death tolls. On the other side of the coin and as many others have argued, the frenetic demonization of migrants, often
associated with right-wing populism, is a vehicle for securing legitimacy for the project of sovereignty in the face of increasing globalization (Heyman, 1999, p. 292;
cf.; Andreas, 2009; Brown, 2010). Either way, there is a conceptual reification of border security as either omnipresent in its violence, or as urgent in light of the
interminable threat of migrant incursion. By extension, depictions of the funnel effect are often tethered to a certain standard view of state power and sovereignty
as equally monolithic and persistent, to the neglect of the myriad frictions and slow uncertain evolutions that underlie the day-to-day exercise of sovereign power
(De Certeau, 2011; Joseph and Nugent, 1994; Painter, 2006).

## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing
ABSENT: The case contains no explicit comparative weighing mechanism—no "magnitude," "probability," "timeframe," or "scope" tags, and no pre-written framing card that instructs the judge how to evaluate human-rights impacts against the Aff's likely security/economy impacts. A card filling this gap would be a 2023-2024 framework/weighing piece (e.g., a rights-based ethical framework or a "structural violence outweighs" card from a peace-studies or critical-security-studies scholar) placed at the top of the Sole Contention or as a short Overview before the contention.

### Offensive spikes
ABSENT: No card directly preempts or link-turns the Aff's core offense (e.g., "surveillance reduces illegal crossings," "surveillance stops fentanyl," "technology pays for itself"). The Kan 2017 and Vaughan 2024 cards describe cartel adaptation but are framed as solvency deficits, not offensive turns. An offensive spike would be a 2023-2024 card tagged "Surveillance expansion increases cartel profits by professionalizing smuggling networks" or "AI towers displace migration to deadlier routes, increasing humanitarian crisis—turns Aff's safety narrative," placed in the "surveillance fails" section.

**Soto 2018** — "A migration funnel effect has resulted from border security operations… Security forces deliberately amassed along established migration routes… Over the years, the deterrence orientation of border security funneled undocumented migration into increasingly high-risk geography…"  
**HOW it executes:** This card functions as an embedded frontline to the inevitable Aff response "surveillance deters migration so fewer people die." Soto warrants that deterrence *creates* the death toll by design, neutralizing the deterrence framing before the Aff extends it.

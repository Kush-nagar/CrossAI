Previous article View issue table of contents Next article Besides using physical walls to attempt to restrict movement across the U.S.-Mexico border, the United States government has also spent billions of dollars on the
construction of “virtual walls.” A virtual wall, sometimes called a “virtual fence” or a “smart wall,” uses advanced surveillance technologies to detect and
deter unauthorized border crossings.The following explores the political economy of virtual walls with focus on two negative consequences—the entanglement of private defense
firms with government through crony relationships and the violation of the liberties of both non-U.S. and U.S. persons. We begin with the origins and evolution of the idea of the virtual wall. The idea of a virtual wall emerged
gradually as America’s border security agencies acquired ever more surveillance technology. Politically connected interest groups then successfully lobbied political officials to maintain and expand the virtual wall. The
resulting virtual wall enhances the efficiency of government institutions in controlling both foreign and domestic peoples. In addition to undermining privacy rights for citizens and migrants alike, the virtual wall can contribute
to migrant deaths. The virtual wall’s surveillance capacities deter migrants fromtraversing heavily monitored routes, pushing them
into riskier desert terrain. In 1994, the Border Patrol adopted a policy known as “prevention through deterrence,” which was designed to deter migration by making entering the
country more costly for prospective migrants. Border Patrol officials described how natural hazards could be their ally, deterring migrants from entering the country. They therefore sought to
fortify relatively safe entry points around American cities, forcing migrants into dangerous routes in the desert. Officials interpreted the increased migrant deaths as evidence that their policies were effective. Efforts to
construct a virtual wall have exacerbated this deadly deterrence technique. After the SBI-net project was initiated, migrants altered their routes to avoid the watchful eyes of surveillance towers. That meant traveling along
more strenuous and perilous terrain, causing a higher risk of death. More corpses were found along more dangerous routes after SBI-net was implemented.Observed geographical patterns of human remains provide empirical
evidence that SBI-net deterred migrants from traveling along comparatively safe routes. Surveillance may seem like a relatively nonviolent form of social control, but it can have deadly consequences.

of different changes [in Egypt], and rising food prices is going to create a very unstable atmosphere.

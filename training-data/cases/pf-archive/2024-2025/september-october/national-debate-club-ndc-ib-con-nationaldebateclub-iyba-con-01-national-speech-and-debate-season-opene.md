Bearce ‘19 finds
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12116-018-9277-y
This paper reconsiders the proposition that remittances act as a political curse by reducing the poor’s demand for economic redistribution. With a newer democratization model focused on the demand for income protection from the rising groups in society, remittances may instead function as a political blessing. Since remittances increase income not only for the middle-class citizens that receive most of them, but also for the merchant and working classes that do not receive them per the multiplier effect, remittances should increase the demand for political rights to protect the economic assets of these societal groups. Using an error correction model with both country and year fixed effects, it reports a significant positive relationship between the change in democracy and net remittance inflows as a share of GDP using three different operational measures for democracy. It also reports results consistent with the underlying causal argument, showing how remittances increase national income and societal economic freedom.

## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing

**ABSENT:** The case presents multiple terminal impacts (nuclear extinction, poverty mortality, political instability, disaster vulnerability) but contains no explicit weighing mechanism — no magnitude, probability, timeframe, or reversibility comparisons between the Ukraine/nuclear scenario and the remittances/poverty scenario. A card filling this gap would carry a tag like "Weighing — Nuclear extinction outweighs on magnitude and irreversibility (Starr '15 / Shalom '22)" and sit as a standalone subpoint after Contention 1 or as an overview before the contentions, explicitly comparing the extinction tail-risk to the economic harms using the existing Starr '15 and Garza '18 evidence.

### Offensive spikes

**ABSENT:** No cards preempt likely Pro arguments such as "surveillance reduces illegal crossings," "technology pays for itself via deterrence," "border security prevents fentanyl deaths," or "surveillance infrastructure ≠ militarization." A card filling this gap would carry a tag like "Spike — Surveillance expansion fails to deter migration / displaces rather than reduces crossings (Andreas '20 / CBP OIG '23)" and belong in the 1NC as a preemptive frontline to Pro's solvency claims, ideally drawn from Government Accountability Office or Congressional Research Service reports on border technology effectiveness.

### Defensive spikes

**ABSENT:** The case lacks embedded frontlines to the responses it will actually face: Pro will argue remittances are unaffected by surveillance (only physical barriers matter), Ukraine aid is a separate budgetary process, and poverty impacts are non-unique. A card filling this gap would carry a tag like "Frontline — Surveillance infrastructure enables enforcement that disrupts remittance corridors / increases migrant debt / reduces earning capacity (Vogt '21 / IACHR '22)" and sit embedded in Contention 2 after the Rivera '24 card, preempting the "no link to remittances" response with evidence that enforcement infrastructure raises migrant costs and reduces remittance flows.

### Evidence quality

**Partially demonstrated — Tag: "Starr '15" (Starr, 2015, ratical.org)** — The nuclear winter/extinction evidence cites a peer-reviewed climatologist (Steven Starr) summarizing peer-reviewed studies (Robock, Toon, et al.) on nuclear winter, providing dataset-level warrants (smoke injections, ozone destruction, temperature drops) that can win an evidence-comparison tiebreak on magnitude. However, the 2015 date is stale for a 2024-25 topic; a 2022-2024 update (e.g., Nature Food 2022 Xia et al.) would strengthen recency. Other cards (Sou '24, Rivera '24, Undr '22) are recent but from advocacy outlets (Left Voice, Fox 5, UNDRR) rather than primary datasets or peer-reviewed journals, making them vulnerable to evidence-indict comparisons.

### Collapse flexibility

**Demonstrated — Tag: "Contention Remittances" with subpoints "Natural Disasters" (Undr '22, Combes '10, Lopez '07) and "Political Instability" (Bearce '19, Veiga '23)** — The remittances contention offers three distinct link chains: (1) surveillance → reduced remittances → disaster vulnerability (Combes '10 insurance function), (2) surveillance → reduced remittances → political instability (Bearce '19 democracy promotion), (3) surveillance → reduced remittances → poverty mortality (Garza '18, Millian '20). This allows the 2NR to collapse to disaster vulnerability, democratic backsliding, or poverty mortality independently if one link is mitigated. The Ukraine contention lacks equivalent flexibility — it funnels entirely through nuclear extinction with no alternative impact scenarios.

### Warrant depth

**Mixed — Tag: "Combes '10" (Combes et al., 2010, World Bank Policy Research Working Paper)** — Provides actual warrants: "remittances significantly reduce household consumption instability… play an insurance role by dampening effects of natural disasters… stabilizing role stronger in less financially developed countries" — explaining the causal mechanism (countercyclical flows, consumption smoothing) with methodological detail (panel of developing countries, endogeneity corrections). Contrast with **Tag: "Sou '24" (Sou, 2024, Left Voice)** — which asserts "Far Right argued that the United States would have to choose between funding Ukraine's war effort and militarizing the border" with no warrant for the tradeoff claim (no budget data, no appropriations evidence, no congressional record cite). The Klain and McFaul cards similarly assert US support is "deciding factor" without quantifying the marginal impact of supplemental aid on battlefield outcomes.

### Internal consistency

**Partially consistent — Tension between Contention 1 (Ukraine) and Contention 2 (Remittances)** — Contention 1 relies on a "guns vs. butter" tradeoff narrative (Sou '24: Far Right forces choice between Ukraine aid and border militarization), while Contention 2 argues border surveillance expansion *itself* destroys remittances and triggers poverty/disaster/political harms. These are not contradictory but they are strategically disconnected: the Ukraine contention's link is *budgetary tradeoff*, the remittances contention's link is *enforcement effect on migrant flows/earnings*. A Pro team can concede the budgetary tradeoff is false (supplemental bills pass separately) while winning that surveillance doesn't reduce remittances. Uniform framing would require a unified theory of the link — e.g., "border militarization diverts resources AND disrupts migrant economies" — with a card explicitly bridging both mechanisms.

### Overall construction score

**Score: 4/10** — The case assembles high-magnitude impacts and recent evidence but lacks nearly all strategic embedding: no weighing, no spikes, disconnected contentions with different link stories, and several key cards that are claims without warrants; it reads as a research collection rather than a constructed strategic instrument.

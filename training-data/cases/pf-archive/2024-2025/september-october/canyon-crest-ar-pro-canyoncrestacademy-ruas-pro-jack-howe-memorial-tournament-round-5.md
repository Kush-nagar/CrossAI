## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing
**ABSENT:** The case contains no explicit impact framing, magnitude/probability/timeframe comparisons, or pre-written weighing mechanisms. Cards are stacked as discrete facts (polling numbers, border encounter statistics, cartel revenue figures) without connective tissue explaining why, e.g., "electoral pressure on Harris" outweighs "cartel adaptation" or how "faster asylum adjudication" compares in magnitude to "legal pathway expansion." A weighing card would tag something like "Weighing — Scope: Surveillance expansion solves the *entire* funnel (deterrence + adjudication + legal pathways) while Con's alternatives only address one layer" and cite a RAND or CRS analysis comparing policy lever effectiveness.

### Offensive spikes
**ABSENT:** No cards preempt specific negative positions (e.g., "Surveillance expansion fuels biometric mission creep," "Deterrence fails without asylum reform," "Tech contracts enrich defense contractors"). The case reads as a pure affirmative construction with zero anticipation of clash. An offensive spike would be a card tagged "Spike — Surveillance doesn't require new authorization: Existing 6 U.S.C. § 112 and CBP's statutory mandate already permit tower/sensor deployment; Con's 'executive overreach' DA is non-unique" citing a Congressional Research Service 2024 memo on CBP surveillance authorities.

### Defensive spikes
**ABSENT:** Zero embedded frontlines. The case does not insulate its key warrants against the most predictable attacks: (1) "Surveillance displaces rather than reduces crossings" — no card showing net reduction vs. displacement; (2) "Asylum ban triggers CAT/withholding claims" — no card on how the bipartisan bill's CAT carve-out functions; (3) "Legal pathway caps remain trivial vs. demand" — Bier 24 admits 250k green cards is "barely half a percent of the 9 million applicant backlog" but the case never addresses this concession. A defensive spike would tag "Frontline — Displacement empirically false: Treyger 24 + Cook 23 show enforcement *with* legal pathways reduces *total* attempts, not just reroutes" and cite the Cato/Leitf joint analysis on H-2A/H-2B substitution effects.

### Evidence quality
**Quinnipiac University 24** — High-recency (Sept 2024), reputable pollster (A-/B+ 538 grade), n=1,603 likely voters, transparent methodology. Wins evidence comparison on *current* electoral dynamics.  
**Treyger 24 (RAND)** — Gold-standard think tank, author is senior policy researcher with 20+ yr DHS experience, uses FY2023 CBP/ICE/USCIS administrative data (universe, not sample). Wins on *system capacity* facts.  
**Bier 23/24 (Cato)** — Lead author is immigration policy director, cites USCIS/DOS visa bulletin data, but 2023 study lacks post-Title 42 expiration updates; 2024 blog post is reactive commentary, not peer-reviewed. Vulnerable on recency vs. government OIG reports.  
**Warrick 24 (Atlantic Council)** — Bipartisan Senate bill summary, useful for *procedural* details but advocacy-tinged; no independent modeling.  
**Cook 23 (LEITF)** — Independent researcher, cites 39 endnotes but no dataset; assertion-heavy on "enforcement failed" without regression controls.  
**CFR 24** — Authoritative backgrounder but descriptive, not causal; homicide count (431k since 2006) is government data but attribution to cartels is contested.  
Overall: Strong on *descriptive* evidence (polling, encounter volumes), weak on *causal* evidence (surveillance → deterrence, legal pathways → cartel revenue reduction). No RCTs, quasi-experiments, or DHS OIG evaluations of surveillance ROI.

### Collapse flexibility

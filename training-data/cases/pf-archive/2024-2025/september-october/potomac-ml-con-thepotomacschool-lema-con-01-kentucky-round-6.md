crossers. A recent analysis by the Cato Institute, a libertarian think tank, compared processing of

years of the Biden administration. Cato found that Biden’s DHS has deported more illegal border

towers allowing agents to track border crossers day and night. The towers detect any movement within
miles of the border, allowing agents to detect incoming drugs or weapons well in advance. CBP has also used
these funds to add additional surveillance aircrafts to sweep over border regions. Many of these vehicles now operate autonomously, saving
agents time in managing thousands of miles of border. These technological improvements mean a safer border for our
country. CBP can now scan six times as much cargo entering the country each day. In real terms, CBP has gone
from inspecting 2% of passenger vehicles and 17% of cargo vehicles to 40% of passenger vehicles and 70% of cargo vehicles. Consequently,
we have increased our fentanyl seizures at the border from an average of 3,077 pounds per year under
President Trump to 17,633 pounds per year under President Biden.2 More fentanyl intercepted at the border means
less fentanyl in our country. Those who claim that President Biden has created open border policies must contend with these facts. Our
southern border is simply not wide open. CBP is better funded and better equipped under President
Biden than at any time in American history. With these increased resources, CBP is capturing more fentanyl and deporting
more illegal border crossers today than ever before.

House Rule XXI, clause 10, currently referred to as the CUTGO (or cut-as-you-go) rule, prohibits
the consideration of legislation that is estimated to result in a net increase in mandatory spending
over either of two periods: (1) the six-year period consisting of the current fiscal year, the budget year, and the four ensuing fiscal years or (2)
the 11-year period consisting of the current year, the budget year, and the ensuing nine fiscal years. The CUTGO rule applies to legislation
affecting mandatory spending only. It does not apply to discretionary spending or revenues: Any budgetary effects treated (or “scored”) as
discretionary spending or revenues are not counted for purposes of determining compliance with the House CUTGO rule. As a result, in practical
terms, the rule generally requires legislation that includes provisions estimated to increase mandatory
spending to also include offsetting reductions in other mandatory spending over the two specified
periods.

## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing
**ABSENT:** The case lacks pre-written weighing mechanisms that compare the magnitude, probability, or timeframe of its impacts (poverty increases, agricultural losses) against likely affirmative impacts (border security, fentanyl interdiction, national security). No card contains comparative language such as "outweighs," "precedes," or "turns" the affirmative's core advantages. A card filling this gap would carry a tag like "Economic collapse outweighs marginal border security gains — poverty kills 130k+/year vs. speculative terrorism risk" and would be placed after the C1 impact cards (CBPP-23/Trisi-19) to frame the round's decision calculus.

### Offensive spikes
**ABSENT:** No cards proactively answer or preempt core affirmative positions such as "surveillance stops fentanyl," "surveillance deters illegal entry," "technology reduces agent endangerment," or "border security is a prerequisite for immigration reform." The case only reacts with a trade-off DA (C1) and an ag DA (C2). An offensive spike would be a card tagged "Surveillance expansion fails to interdict fentanyl — 90%+ seized at POEs, not between ports" (citing CBP/OIG data) placed in C1, or "Virtual fence pilot programs proved ineffective — GAO 2023 found $1B+ wasted with no measurable apprehension increase" placed as a solvency takeout in C2.

### Defensive spikes
**ABSENT:** The case contains no embedded frontlines to the specific responses it will face: "surveillance pays for itself via seized contraband," "agriculture has H-2A/guest worker alternatives," "poverty impacts are non-unique because Congress won't actually cut," or "DHS budget shortfalls are temporary/continuing resolution artifacts." A defensive spike would be a card tagged "H-2A program cannot replace undocumented labor — caps, wage rules, and timing gaps leave 40%+ seasonal demand unmet" (citing USDA ERS or Farm Bureau 2023) placed at the end of C2, or "CUTGO rule does not bind discretionary DHS appropriations — Katz-24 shortfall is discretionary, not mandatory" placed after CRS-23 in C1.

### Evidence quality
**ABSEDent:** The evidence base is severely dated and author-qualification-thin for a 2024-2025 topic. **Katz-24** (Government Executive reporter, not a budget analyst) is the only 2024 card on the central trade-off link. **Washington Post-16** (2016, Bush-era "virtual fence" estimate) is 8 years outdated and irrelevant to modern AI/tower surveillance. **Luckstead-11** (2011) is 13 years old — predating H-2A expansion, mechanical harvesting advances, and post-COVID labor markets. **CRS-23**, **Pardue-23**, **CBPP-23**, **Trisi-19**, **Zelizer-17** are all 2017-2023 but address generic fiscal rules/poverty trends, not the specific DHS-surveillance trade-off. **USDA-24** is a projection document, not a study of surveillance impacts. No card cites a peer-reviewed study, government audit (GAO/OIG), or subject-matter PhD on *current* surveillance technology costs or agricultural labor elasticity. This evidence would lose an evidence-comparison tiebreak against a team reading 2023-2024 GAO reports, CBP budget justifications, or agricultural economics journals.

### Collapse flexibility
**ABSENT:** The case is pinned to two rigid, linear DAs with no alternative link chains or impact scenarios. C1 *only* functions if: (a) surveillance costs $30B+ (WP-16), (b) CUTGO forces mandatory cuts (CRS-23), (c) mandatory cuts hit safety net (Pardue-23/Zelizer-17), (d) safety net cuts increase poverty (CBPP-23/Trisi-19). If *any* link breaks (e.g., surveillance funded via discretionary emergency supplemental, or CUTGO waived, or poverty impacts non-unique), the entire contention collapses. C2 *only* functions if surveillance cuts undocumented flow *and* no substitute labor exists. No "surveillance causes mission creep → civil liberties violations" link, no "surveillance diverts agents from POEs → fentanyl increases" link, no "surveillance contracts enrich contractors → corruption" link. A flexible case would include at least three distinct link chains per contention with different terminal impacts (e.g., C1: fiscal trade-off, opportunity cost of tech R&D, political capital trade-off; C2: labor shortage, food safety risk from unharvested crops, rural economic collapse).

### Internal consistency
**INCONSISTENT — The Odrich-24 framing cards directly undercut both contentions.**  
- **Odrich-24 (first excerpt)** states: "CBP has created a network of surveillance towers allowing agents to track border crossers day and night... CBP can now scan six times as much cargo... increased our fentanyl seizures... from 3,077 lbs/yr under Trump to 17,633 lbs/yr under Biden." This *affirms* that surveillance infrastructure works and is already expanding under current funding — contradicting C1's claim that affirming "breaks the bank" (surveillance is already happening within current budgets) and C2's claim that surveillance "cuts off flows of undocumented immigrants" (Odrich says CBP is *already* capturing more crossers *and* more fentanyl with current tech).  
- **Odrich-24 (second excerpt)** states: "No increase in funding, technology, or physical barrier will change these global migratory patterns." This *concedes* that the affirmative's surveillance expansion won't reduce migration flows — but C2's entire link chain *requires* that surveillance cuts off undocumented immigrant flows to devastate agriculture. If flows don't change (per Odrich), C2's impact disappears.  
The case simultaneously argues: (1) surveillance is already working (Odrich), (2) more surveillance is unaffordable (C1), (3) more surveillance will stop migration and hurt ag (C2), (4) migration won't stop regardless of surveillance (Odrich). These four claims cannot all be true.

### Overall construction score
**Score: 3/10** — The case has a coherent two-contention structure and some warranted fiscal-policy cards (Katz-24, CRS-23, Pardue-23), but suffers from fatal internal contradictions (framing undercuts both DAs), critically outdated evidence (13-year-old ag study, 8-year-old cost estimate), zero embedded weighing or spikes, and no collapse flexibility — making it strategically brittle and evidentiarily non-competitive against prepared affirmative teams.

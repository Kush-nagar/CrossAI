## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing

### Collapse flexibility
ABSENT: Every card funnels toward a single link chain — “more scanning technology → more fentanyl/gun interdictions” — with no independent impact scenarios (e.g., human trafficking disruption, precursor chemical interdiction, border-crossing mortality reduction, trade facilitation economics). A collapse card like “AI surveillance cuts human trafficking 40% — GAO 2023 finds thermal + LIDAR detects concealed migrants in 90% of test runs” (tag: “Advantage 2: Human trafficking”) would give the 2NR a second, unlinked impact to collapse to if the fentanyl link is mitigated.

### Warrant depth
Bernstein 2019 — Tag claims “X-ray imaging systems… provide clearer view… detect drugs, weapons, cash” but the quoted text only describes procurement plans and volume challenges; no warrant explains *how* the VACIS IP6500 distinguishes fentanyl in concealed compartments versus legitimate cargo.  
France 24 2021 — Tag asserts “militarization failed… new framework focuses on underlying causes” yet the excerpt only quotes an unnamed “senior US administration official” with no study, metric, or mechanism linking surveillance expansion to root-cause reduction.  
Epstein 2019 — Tag claims “limiting access to guns may reduce violence in Mexico” but the cited UMass study only correlates assault-weapon access with homicide spikes; no warrant connects *border surveillance* to cutting the gun pipeline (the case’s own solvency mechanism).

### Internal consistency
The case simultaneously argues (1) cartels use drones for countersurveillance against Border Patrol (Homeland 24) and (2) expanded ground-based surveillance (fiber, X-ray, AI) will interdict cartel operations — but never explains why cartels won’t simply shift drone surveillance to monitor and evade the new fixed sensors, undercutting the solvency claim. Additionally, Lowy & Moreno 23 and Epstein 2019 emphasize *gun supply* as the primary cartel enabler, while the case’s sole mechanism is *narcotics interdiction*; the two contentions pull toward different solvency mechanisms without reconciliation.

### Overall construction score
**Score: 4/10** — Strong primary-source evidence on scanner deployment and fentanyl mortality, but zero embedded weighing, no offensive/defensive spikes, single-link collapse vulnerability, thin warrants on key cards, and an unresolved tension between gun-pipeline and narcotics-interdiction solvency narratives.

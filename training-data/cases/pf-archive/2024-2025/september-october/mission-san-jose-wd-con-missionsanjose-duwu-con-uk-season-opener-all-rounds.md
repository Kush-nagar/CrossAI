immigrants and immigration-related matters appear in the context of the National Security Strategy (NSS). The framing of
immigration-related themes in the periodically updated NSS documents offer thus an important glimpse into one aspect of
public, presidential securitisation of immigration worth exploring.

### Embedded weighing
**ABSENT:** The case presents distinct impact scenarios (deaths on dangerous routes, deportation harms, economic loss) but contains no pre-written comparative weighing mechanism — no "magnitude," "probability," "timeframe," or "scope" tags linking cards to a unified calculus. A card filling the gap would be a framing card tagged **"Weighing: Structural violence outweighs — deportation and border deaths constitute systemic, irreversible harm to 11M+ people vs. speculative security benefits"** (e.g., a Galtung structural violence cite or a CBP mortality dataset analysis) placed after the UQ block to frame both contentions.

### Offensive spikes
**ABSENT:** No card explicitly preempts likely Pro positions (e.g., "surveillance deters crossings," "technology reduces agent contact," "fentanyl interdiction requires sensors"). A spike card would be tagged **"Spike: Surveillance expansion fails deterrence — CBP data shows apprehensions drop without tech expansion due to Mexico enforcement"** (citing Isacson 2024 or CBP stats) placed in UQ, or **"Spike: Fentanyl crosses at legal ports — surveillance infrastructure misses 90% of seizures"** (citing DEA/CBP testimony) to undercut the core Pro narrative before it’s read.

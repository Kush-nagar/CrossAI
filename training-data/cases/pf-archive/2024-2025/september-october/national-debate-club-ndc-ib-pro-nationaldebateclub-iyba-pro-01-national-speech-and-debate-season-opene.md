## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing
**ABSENT:** The case lacks any pre-written impact calculus or comparative weighing mechanisms. Cards present standalone statistics (e.g., 183,000 poverty deaths, 110,000 overdose deaths) without framing why one impact outweighs another, establishing timeframe precedence, or explaining how surveillance expansion uniquely accesses these impacts relative to alternative policies. A card filling this gap would carry a tag like "Surveillance expansion is the *only* mechanism that simultaneously disrupts cartel finance *and* drives AI innovation — dual-track solvency makes magnitude and probability of solving both fentanyl and poverty crises uniquely high" and belong at the end of each contention as a mini-overview.

### Offensive spikes
**ABSENT:** No cards preempt likely Con positions such as "surveillance infrastructure expands government overreach/civil liberties violations," "virtual wall tech has failed historically (ISDS/SBInet)," "cartels adapt to surveillance via tunnels/corruption," or "AI arms race with China is inevitable regardless of border spending." An offensive spike card would cite a 2024 GAO or CBP report tagging "C-UAS and tower systems have *already* reduced drone incursions by X% in RGV sector — Con's 'adaptation' argument assumes static tech, but AI-driven surveillance evolves in real-time" and sit in Contention 1 after the Higgins '24 drone card.

### Defensive spikes
**ABSENT:** The case contains no embedded frontlines. It does not preempt "surveillance doesn't stop money laundering because it's digital/crypto-based," "border tech contracts enrich contractors without operational results," or "poverty/death impacts are non-unique — US poverty drivers are structural (housing, healthcare), not border-tech solvable." A defensive spike would be a 2024 DHS OIG or CBP evaluation tagging "AI surveillance towers in RGV sector led to 40% increase in cash seizures at stash houses — directly disrupts the physical cash handoffs described in Carman/Lisa '24" and placed after the Lisa '24 laundering mechanics card.

### Evidence quality
**MIXED — Carman ‘24 (NBC News investigation) and Higgins ‘24 (House Homeland Security hearing transcript) are high-quality, primary-source evidence with strong author qualifications (investigative journalists, congressional testimony). However, Welch ‘24 (Breaking Defense summarizing Govini report), Moschella ‘23 (SME.org article), and Queen ‘24 (Podium.com blog) are secondary/tertiary sources with weak author quals for terminal impacts (AI war unwinnability, automation valley of death, small business innovation). Bornet ‘24 (cognitiveautomation.com commercial site) and Shrine ‘24 (NCBI book chapter) are misapplied — healthcare automation deaths have no warranted link to border surveillance. The case would lose an evidence-comparison tiebreak on Contention 2's terminal impacts.**

### Collapse flexibility
**ABSENT:** Contention 1 is pinned entirely to the "surveillance → disrupts cash handoffs/drone countersurveillance → solves fentanyl money laundering" link chain. No alternative link (e.g., surveillance enables financial intelligence sharing with Treasury/FinCEN, surveillance deters precursor chemical smuggling) is evidenced. Contention 2 collapses to "border surveillance spending → AI innovation → solves poverty/deaths" with no alternative pathway (e.g., surveillance creates high-skill border jobs, surveillance tech spin-offs boost local economies). A flexibility card would tag "CBP's AI procurement *1: DHS '24 — surveillance tower contracts require domestic manufacturing and data-analysis hubs, creating 15,000 direct jobs in border counties (Wallace '24 job market solvency)" and sit in Contention 2 after Maass '24.

### Warrant depth
**PARTIAL — Contention 1 laundering mechanics (Carman '24, Cava '24, Lisa '24, Jeong '24) provide detailed, warranted explanations of *how* Chinese brokers move cartel cash (bank transfers, trade-based laundering, bulk cash delivery). Higgins '24 warrants *why* drones matter for cartel ops (10,000+ incursions/year, countersurveillance). However, Contention 2 tags are largely claim+cite: Welch '24 asserts "US falling behind China in AI → war unwinnable" without warranting *which* AI capabilities, *why* border surveillance specifically closes the gap, or *what* the Govini report measures. Moschella '23 describes a generic "valley of death" in R&D but does not warrant that *border surveillance funding* uniquely bridges it. Queen '24, Bornet '24, Shrine '24, Wallace '24, Pfefferman '24, Fay '24, Jarow '23 form an unwarranted daisy chain from "small business innovation" → "healthcare automation saves millions" → "poverty causes death" with zero evidence linking *border surveillance expansion* to any step.**

### Internal consistency
**FAILS — Contention 1 argues surveillance disrupts *existing* cartel operations (cash handoffs, drone countersurveillance) implying cartels are currently successful and tech *stops* them. Contention 2 (Maass '24, Hellerstein '21) frames border surveillance as a "cash cow for private companies" and "smart border innovation" that *already exists and is profitable* — undercutting the "substantially expand" solvency claim (if it's already a lucrative mature market, marginal expansion yields diminishing returns). Queen '24 (small biz innovation) and Bornet '24 (healthcare automation) introduce entirely unrelated sectors with no framing mechanism tying them to border surveillance. The poverty/death terminal impacts (Jarow '23) are non-unique to the resolution — no card warrants that *border surveillance expansion* reduces US poverty more effectively than direct anti-poverty policy.**

### Overall construction score
**Score: 3/10** — The case assembles strong investigative reporting on fentanyl money laundering mechanics (Contention 1 evidence base) but fails to convert it into a strategic PF case: no weighing, spikes, or collapse flexibility; Contention 2 is an incoherent evidence dump with broken link chains and terminal impacts unwarranted to the resolution; internal contradictions between "cartels exploit vulnerabilities" (C1) and "surveillance is already a mature cash cow" (C2) undercut solvency.

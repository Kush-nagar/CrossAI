### Collapse flexibility
**Two distinct, independent impact scenarios exist:** (1) **Fentanyl/opioid deaths** via NII/AI scanner deployment at ports of entry (Strickler 2024, de Córdoba 2023, Sganga 2023), and (2) **Migrant human rights abuses** via AI-accelerated asylum processing clearing backlogs (Zitzman 2022, AmImmCouncil 2023, Warsi/PBS 2023). ### Warrant depth
**Mixed.** The fentanyl scanning cards provide **mechanistic warrants**: Strickler 2024 explains *how* NII works (drive-through X-ray, no stop), de Córdoba 2023 gives a *field result* (Brownsville: "drastic increase in narcotics seizures" then displacement), and Sganga 2023 establishes the *link* (90% of 80k opioid deaths = fentanyl, mostly southern border). The asylum-processing cards are thinner: Zitzman 2022 **claims** ML speeds identity checks and cites German/UK/Swiss pilots but does not quote their measured outcomes (error rates, time saved); AmImmCouncil 2023 and NatImmForum 2024 **describe backlog severity** (1.3M–1.5M pending, 4–6 yr waits) but do not warrant that *surveillance infrastructure expansion* specifically funds adjudication capacity (the plan-mechanism gap). Warsi 2023 warrants the *impact* (308 abuse complaints, systemic pattern) but not the *solvency* link.

### Internal consistency
**Tension detected.** Contention 1 frames surveillance infrastructure as **interdiction hardware** (NII scanners at POEs stopping vehicles/drugs) — a *border enforcement* tool. Contention 2 frames it as **humanitarian processing capacity** (AI biometrics speeding asylum adjudication) — a *border management* tool. The case never reconciles whether "substantially expand surveillance infrastructure" means *both* scanning portals *and* biometric databases, or if the plan simply "funds CBP broadly." The EU SIS and Australian Border Force analog cards are invoked for *both* contentions (terrorism/crime detection for Contention 1; AI targeting for Contention 2), blurring the mechanism. No framing card defines the term or prioritizes one mechanism, leaving the case vulnerable to "vague plan" or "double-bind" attacks (e.g., "scanners don't process asylum claims").

This Article began with a question: Can the ICC succeed? Answering that question requires that we first know what the ICC is supposed to accomplish. After all, it can only be called a success if it accomplishes its goals.187 A number of goals have been ascribed to the ICC in the scholarly literature. But not all these goals are equal. Rather, there is a hierarchy amongst them, and the court should be judged by whether it accomplishes those goals that have the highest expected value.188
The goal with the highest expected value is the prevention of violations of international criminal law.189 This is because serious violations of ICL have enormous costs for the victims of those crimes, as well as their friends, families, neighbors, and colleagues.190 Violations also have enormous societal costs ranging from the tens of billions to hundreds of billions of dollars per conflict.191 Thus, preventing those violations has enormous value. This means that if the ICC can prevent violations of international criminal law, then it is accomplishing its most important goal. **ABSENT:** The case lacks pre-written impact calculus comparing the magnitude/probability/timeframe of "ICC collapse" versus "US credibility loss" versus "atrocity prevention." No card explicitly weighs why saving the ICC from sanctions outweighs credibility gains from joining, or why atrocity prevention (Ford 20) is the highest-magnitude impact in the round. A filling card would be a tag like "**Magnitude: Atrocity prevention outweighs — Ford 20 finds ICC prevents conflicts killing hundreds of thousands; credibility impacts are speculative and non-unique**" placed after Ford 20 in C1.

### Offensive spikes
**ABSENT:** No cards preempt likely Con positions such as "US sovereignty/Article 98 agreements solve," "ICC targets US personnel," "ICC is biased against Israel/Africa," or "Rome Statute requires Senate ratification (political impossibility)." A filling card would be "**Sovereignty spike — Scheffer '23: US joining ICC *strengthens* sovereignty via complementarity; US courts retain primary jurisdiction**" placed in C2, or "**No US prosecution spike — CFR 2018: complementarity principle means ICC only acts when US unwilling/unable**" in C1.

### Defensive spikes
**ABSENT:** No embedded frontlines against the specific responses this case will face: "sanctions are inevitable regardless of accession," "US joining legitimizes a broken court," "Trump will withdraw/sanction anyway," or "Article 48 immunities don't bind non-parties." A filling card would be "**Sanctions spike — Anderson 2025: US sanctions work *because* ICC is non-party; accession triggers Art. 48 protections and EU blocking statutes**" placed after Anderson 2025, or "**Trump-proofing — Davies 2025: ICC already prepping for worst-case; accession is only way to unlock EU legal shields**."

### Evidence quality
**Davies 2025 (Guardian) — "ICC braces for swift Trump sanctions..."** — High-quality, breaking-news reporting from ICC officials/diplomats on the record; January 2025 recency makes it nearly impossible to out-date. Wins evidence-comparison on sanctions imminence/specificity (Microsoft Azure dependency, blocking statutes).

**Ford 20 (Loyola Int'l Comp. L. Rev.) — "Can the ICC Succeed?"** — Peer-reviewed law review article by a law professor; synthesizes multiple empirical studies (Hillebrecht, Meernik, Simmons, Jo, Appel) on violence prevention. Dataset spans ICC's full history. Wins evidence-comparison on atrocity prevention vs. Con's likely anecdotal "ICC fails" cards.

**Anderson 2025 (Justice Info) — "Can the ICC survive US sanctions?"** — Specialist NGO publication with direct quotes from ex-State Dept. officials (Keith, Buchwald) and sanctions law prof (Sterio). Strong quals on mechanism of "civil death" via dollar dominance (90% global transactions). Recency matches Davies.

**Scheffer 2023 (Lieber Institute) — "US Should Ratify Rome Statute"** — Author is former US Ambassador-at-Large for War Crimes (1997-2001); primary architect of US Rome Statute signature. Highest possible practitioner authority on accession mechanics and credibility impact.

### Collapse flexibility
**Ford 20 (Loyola Int'l Comp. L. Rev.) — "Can the ICC Succeed?"** — Provides three distinct impact scenarios from one warrant: (1) prevents violence *before* outbreak, (2) shortens *ongoing* conflicts, (3) prevents conflicts *from occurring*. Allows 2NR to collapse to "prevention outweighs" even if Con wins "ICC can't stop current wars."

**Davies 2025 (Guardian) + Anderson 2025 (Justice Info) + Rome Statute Art. 48** — Three-layer sanctions solvency: (1) Davies establishes existential threat *now*, (2) Anderson explains *mechanism* (dollar dominance, financial cutoff), (3) Art. 48 provides *legal solution* (immunities, blocking statutes). If Con beats one layer (e.g., "Art. 48 doesn't bind US"), the other two still access "ICC survival" impact.

**C2 Credibility** — Two independent credibility links: (1) Savage/Riera historical: US non-membership *eroded* credibility via Art. 98 campaigns, (2) Scheffer/Vindman forward-looking: accession *restores* credibility and leadership. Not pinned to "Trump-specific" credibility loss (Al Jazeera/Fullilove).

### Warrant depth
**Ford 20 — tag: "Successful ICC prevents violent conflict, shortens ongoing conflict, and prevents conflict outbreak"** — Warrant delivered: cites specific empirical studies (Hillebrecht, Meernik, Appel, Jo, Simmons) showing "strong evidence the ICC does prevent violence" and "some evidence ICC can shorten ongoing conflicts and prevent conflicts from occurring." Explains *why* prevention has highest expected value (costs of violations: tens/hundreds of billions per conflict).

**Anderson 2025 — tag: "Sanctions spell doom for the ICC"** — Warrant delivered: explains *mechanism* — 90% global transactions in USD → asset freeze = "civil death" → financial institutions preemptively cut ties ("business prudence") → ICC loses banking, IT, insurance. Quotes ex-ambassador Buchwald on over-compliance.

**Davies 2025 — tag: "Reliance on US tech amplifies the danger"** — Warrant delivered: specific dependency — Microsoft Azure cloud stores *all evidence*; suspending access "would paralyse investigations." ICC already cutting US suppliers, exploring EU blocking statutes ("not a silver bullet").

**Scheffer 2023 — tag: "As such, affirming is a great way to restore some of our credibility in the Trump era"** — Warrant delivered: "stated intention [to ratify] will boost American credibility and blunt the double standards criticism that constrains U.S. foreign policy aims." Mechanistic: double-standards critique undermines US leadership; accession signals consistency.

### Internal consistency
**Tension: C1 sanctions urgency vs. C2 credibility timeline.** C1 (Davies/Anderson) argues sanctions are *imminent* (Trump executive order "this week," January 2025), requiring immediate accession to trigger Art. 48 protections. C2 (Scheffer) concedes accession "will take time (likely years) to achieve" but claims "stated intention" boosts credibility *now*. No card bridges this: does "stated intention" trigger Art. 48 immunities? Does it stop sanctions? The case treats accession as both an emergency brake (C1) and a long-term credibility investment (C2) without reconciling the timeframe.

**Tension: Handmaker 2018 (C2) vs. Ford 20 (C1).** Handmaker 2018 details ICC "legitimacy crisis": Lubanga prosecutor failure, gender crimes gaps, internal dysfunction, Bolton de-legitimization campaign. Ford 20 (2020) claims "ICC is already succeeding" at principal goal (violence prevention) based on empirical evidence. No card updates Handmaker post-2018 or reconciles "legitimacy crisis" with "empirical success." Con can argue: even if ICC prevents *some* violence, its legitimacy defects (Handmaker) make US accession a credibility *cost*, not gain.

**Uniform framing achieved on "ICC as institution worth saving."** All cards treat ICC survival as net positive; no contention argues "ICC is flawed but..." — consistent Pro narrative.

### Overall construction score
**Score: 6/10** — Elite evidence quality and warrant depth on the sanctions/atrocity-prevention core, with genuine collapse flexibility across three impact scenarios, but strategically incomplete: zero embedded weighing, zero offensive/defensive spikes against the topic's most predictable Con positions (sovereignty, Article 98, US personnel, political impossibility), and a structural timeframe tension between C1 urgency and C2 multi-year ratification that Con will exploit in crossfire and final focus.

For example, Payam Akhavan has argued, in the context of the ICTY, that the long culture of impunity created a world in which international law coud not deter future violations of human rights, but that the creation of the ICTY could, at least to some extent.2 He argued that “targeting political and military leaders and subjecting them to a threat of punishment, or even mere international opprobrium, can generate a form of immediate deterrence.3 In recent years, emergent empirical evidence has shifted the discussion to a conditional deterrence model, suggesting that the ICC’s capacity to deter will depend on the type of actor, the context, and the level of ICC involvement in a situation country. 4 In perhaps the largest and most in-depth empirical study of the ICC’s deterrence effect, published in 2015, Jo and Simmons present evidence supporting the conditional deterrence theory. ICC can prosecute drug trafficking
Molly McConville, 00, vLex, https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/global-war-on-drugs-685645025 1-1-2000, accessed 1-25-2025 //RD
Organized, internationally-based drug traffickers with vast financial resources pose a serious threat to the stability and security of the international community. They operate without concern for national boundaries, and individual nation-states are often ill-equipped to prosecute and punish the perpetrators. Despite the best efforts of nations to cooperate, prosecution under the current enforcement system fails to keep pace with the traffickers. The international level is the new arena for fighting the global "War on Drugs," yet nations struggle without the weapons of international prosecution. International drug trafficking is a problem in need of a solution; the newly created International Criminal Court ("ICC" or "Court") is a solution in need of problems. This Note advocates combining the two by granting subject-matter jurisdiction over drug trafficking to the Court. The Court would prosecute only serious drug trafficking cases of international concern, and only where individual nations are unwilling or unable to prosecute. It is crucial for the United States, as a world leader in enforcement efforts against drug trafficking, to support this alternate forum. The United States has a strong interest in seeing that more international drug traffickers are brought to justice, whether in the United States, in other national justice systems, or in an international court. Expanding the jurisdiction of the International Criminal Court to prosecute drug trafficking offenses will advance the drug policy objectives of the United States and help stem the growing threat of crime in the twenty-first century.

## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing
**ABSENT:** The case file contains no pre-written weighing arguments embedded in taglines or evidence. There are no cards with tags like "Magnitude — ICC deters atrocities at scale" or "Probability — US accession uniquely solves ICC funding gaps." A card filling this gap would carry a tagline such as "Weighing — US accession outweighs on magnitude and probability: US funding + enforcement capacity makes ICC deterrence operational globally, not just regionally" and would be placed after the solvency evidence (e.g., after the Minkoff or Jo & Simmons cards) to extend in the back half as a framing push.

### Offensive spikes
**ABSENT:** No evidence preemptively attacks common Con positions (e.g., "ICC lacks jurisdiction over US nationals," "Rome Statute threatens US sovereignty," "Article 98 agreements solve," "US bilateral immunity agreements suffice," "ICC is politicized/anti-Israel/anti-US"). A spike card would look like: "Offensive Spike — Article 98 agreements fail: they don't provide ICC funding, don't bind non-party cooperation, and create a patchwork that undermines universal jurisdiction — [cite: HRW 2023 / Amnesty 2024]" placed in Contention 1 or a dedicated spike block.

### Defensive spikes
**ABSENT:** No embedded frontlines preempting likely Con responses (e.g., "US already cooperates with ICC ad hoc," "US domestic courts can prosecute," "ICC has no enforcement mechanism," "US accession triggers Art. 124 opt-out abuse"). A defensive spike card would read: "Defensive Spike — Ad hoc cooperation fails: US non-membership means no Art. 86 obligation to cooperate, no budget contribution, and no leverage over OTP priorities — [cite: Rome Statute Arts. 86-87; Minkoff 2023]" placed after the Rome Statute Articles 86-87 cards.

### Evidence quality
**ABSENT:** The evidence base mixes high-quality sources (Koh 2017 Yale Law; Jo & Simmons 2016 Cambridge Core; Reuters 2025; OHCHR 2024) with low-quality or irrelevant sources (McConville 2000 vLex on drug trafficking; Britannica 2025 on UN membership count; NewsNation 2025 on Trump EO timing; Geneva Academy 2023 conflict counts without ICC link). The drug trafficking cards (McConville 2000) are 25 years old, pre-date the ICC's existence, and advocate *expanding* ICC jurisdiction — not relevant to US accession. The Britannica card establishes nothing about the resolution. A competitive case would cut the McConville and Britannica cards and replace them with post-2020 scholarship on ICC deterrence, US-ICC relations, or Rome Statute legal analysis (e.g., Akhavan 2021, Clark 2022, HRW 2023).

### Collapse flexibility
**ABSENT:** The case appears to have only one coherent impact pathway: US accession → ICC funding/legitimacy/enforcement → deterrence of atrocities (via Jo & Simmons, Hörtnagl). There is no second independent contention (e.g., US leadership/soft power, Kantian governance collapse, great power competition with China, victim reparations, complementarity strengthening). The Koh 2017 and Hardman 2024 cards gesture at "Kantian global governance" and "great power competition" but are not developed into tagged contentions with solvency evidence. A second contention would need tags like: "Contention 2 — US accession counters Chinese capture of multilateralism: US funding + leadership vacuums filled by China absent US ICC membership — [cite: Hardman 2024 CSIS; Ikenberry 2023]" with distinct solvency.

### Warrant depth
The **Jo & Simmons 2016** card ("Model 2 looks at the effect of ICC ACTIONS... one additional investigation each year over the three-year term is estimated to reduce intentional civilian killing by a factor of 0.570") provides a strong quantitative warrant: it specifies the model, the variable (ICC actions), the effect size (0.570 incidence-rate ratio), and the mechanism (new information demonstrating ICC operational authority). The **Hörtnagl 2021** card ("UN Security Council referral is associated with a three-fold decrease in civilian fatalities... qualitative research demonstrates this can be attributed to international community's early willingness to prosecute") provides a case-specific warrant with causal mechanism. However, the **Minkoff 2023** card ("U.S. membership would vastly improve the court's legitimacy and effectiveness... U.S. has the power... to seek trade-offs") is largely claim + cite with no empirical warrant for *how* US membership improves effectiveness (no data on budget increase, no precedent of US leverage in treaty bodies). The **Koh 2017** and **Hardman 2024** cards are framing/context cards without tagged warrants tied to the resolution.

### Internal consistency
**ABSENT:** The case file contains evidence that pulls in contradictory directions. The Koh 2017 card champions "Kantian global governance" and US leadership of the UN/WTO/Paris Agreement — but the Hardman 2024 card documents US *decline* in multilateral influence and Chinese ascendancy. The Reuters 2025 and OHCHR 2024 cards focus on ICC arrest warrants for Israeli officials and US sanctions retaliation — which undermines the "US accession improves ICC legitimacy" narrative by showing the US actively undermining the ICC *currently*. The McConville 2000 drug trafficking cards advocate *expanding* ICC subject-matter jurisdiction, which is irrelevant to US accession and implicitly concedes the ICC's current jurisdiction is insufficient. No unified framing (e.g., "US accession is necessary to reverse multilateral decline and operationalize ICC deterrence") ties these together.

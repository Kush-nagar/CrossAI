## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing
**ABSENT:** The case contains no explicit weighing mechanism — no magnitude, probability, timeframe, or scope comparisons between its impacts (US military deterrence vs. ICC conflict prolongation vs. global violence trends) and likely Pro impacts (accountability, deterrence of atrocities, US moral leadership). A card filling this gap would carry a tag like "Magnitude — US military deterrence prevents great-power war and nuclear escalation, outweighing any ICC accountability gains" (Goure ’13 / Bellamy 18) and belong at the end of Contention 1 or as a standalone "Weighing" block before the conclusion.

### Offensive spikes
**ABSENT:** No cards preemptively answer core Pro arguments such as "Article 98 agreements solve US personnel exposure," "US accession strengthens ICC legitimacy and deterrence," "complementarity means ICC only acts when US courts fail," or "US joining isolates Russia/China." An offensive spike would be a card tagged "Article 98 agreements are politically unstable and legally insufficient — ICC can still prosecute" (Bolton 2018 or similar) placed in Contention 1 after the Groves & Schaefer 9 card, or "US accession does not increase ICC effectiveness — court remains dependent on state cooperation" (Klamberg 2020) placed in Contention 2 after the Wilmshurst 16 card.

### Defensive spikes
**ABSENT:** The case lacks embedded frontlines against the specific responses it will face: "US already complies with ICC norms via domestic law," "ICC has safeguards against politicized prosecutions," "US non-membership undermines its credibility on human rights," "ICC only prosecutes when states are unwilling/unable." A defensive spike would be a card tagged "Complementarity does not protect US officials — ICC prosecutor determines 'unwillingness' politically" (Scheffer 2019 or similar) placed in Contention 1 after Groves '09, or "US non-membership has not weakened its human rights leadership — US still leads sanctions, tribunals, and accountability efforts" (Power 2021) placed as a new subpoint in Contention 2.

### Evidence quality
**ABSENT:** The case relies heavily on outdated or advocacy-sourced evidence: Groves & Schaefer 9 and Groves '09 are 15+ years old from the Heritage Foundation (advocacy think tank); Goure ’13 is 12 years old from a niche policy center; Prorok 2017 is the only peer-reviewed study but is 8 years old and covers 2002–2013 only; Brahm 23 is a funding-mechanics article, not an impact study; Bellamy 18 is a lecture transcript, not empirical research. No card carries a post-2020 author with direct ICC/Rome Statute expertise (e.g., an ICC prosecutor, ASP delegate, or recent IO scholar). A replacement card would be "ICC investigations increase conflict duration by 27% in new 2020–2023 data — Prorok & Appel 2024, Journal of Conflict Resolution" placed in Contention 2 replacing Prorok 2017.

### Collapse flexibility
**Card:** "Carpenter 22 … early indictments foreclose off-ramps, further backing authoritarian regimes into a politico-strategic corner … would only raise the stakes for Putin in winning at any cost … more likely to take drastic actions" (Carpenter 2022, Foreign Policy).  
**How it executes:** This card creates a second, independent impact pathway in Contention 2 — not just statistical conflict prolongation (Prorok) but a mechanism-specific risk that ICC indictments harden authoritarian resolve and trigger escalation (Putin example). The case can collapse to either "ICC prolongs conflicts statistically" or "ICC indictments corner dictators into escalation" without needing both, giving two distinct link stories to the same "counterproductive organization" thesis.

### Warrant depth
**Card:** "Prorok 2017 … Active ICC involvement in a conflict increases the threat of punishment for rebel and state leaders, which, under certain conditions, generates incentives for these leaders to continue the conflict as a way to avoid capture, transfer to the Hague, and prosecution … active involvement by the ICC significantly decreases the likelihood of conflict termination when the threat of domestic punishment is relatively low." (Prorok 2017, International Organization).  
**How it executes:** The tagline "ICC prolongs conflicts" receives a full causal warrant inside the card text: ICC involvement → raises punishment threat for leaders → leaders fight longer to avoid capture/prosecution → effect strongest where domestic accountability is weak. The card quotes the study’s theoretical mechanism and its empirical finding (2002–2013 civil conflict dyads), not just the conclusion.

### Internal consistency
**ABSENT:** Contention 1 argues US military projection *deters* conflict and *stabilizes* the international order (Garamone ’24, Goure ’13), while Contention 2 argues the ICC *prolongs* conflict by removing off-ramps for leaders (Prorok 2017, Carpenter 2022).

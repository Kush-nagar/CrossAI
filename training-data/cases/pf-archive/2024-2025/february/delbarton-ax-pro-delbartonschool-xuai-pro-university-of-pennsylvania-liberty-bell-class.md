because of a US mistake. This happened in 1998 at a special legal conference in Rome, where 148 countries were supposed to adopt the statute
of the future court. In the last hour of the last day of the conference, the head of the American delegation called for a vote on the text of the
statute. He hoped that most states would not vote, understanding that there was no unanimity — and there was none. The vote took place: only
seven participating countries voted against the statute. The US voted against, along with China and Israel. Despite this, the ICC was created.

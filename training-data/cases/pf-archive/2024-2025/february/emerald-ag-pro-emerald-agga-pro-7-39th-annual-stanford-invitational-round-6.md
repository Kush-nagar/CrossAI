## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing
ABSENT: The case contains no explicit impact framing or comparative weighing language embedded in card tags or text (e.g., "magnitude," "probability," "timeframe," "prerequisite," "outweighs"). Cards are stacked by thematic grouping (Sanctions, Funding, Legitimacy, Human Rights, ICC Effectiveness, US Leadership) but never articulate why one impact precedes or outweighs another. A weighing card would carry a tag like "Magnitude — ICC collapse triggers systemic impunity for atrocity crimes globally" (citing a political scientist on cascade effects) and belong at the top of the case as a framing card.

### Offensive spikes
**Card: "Acceding to the Rome statute means full cooperation, entailing an end to sanctions" — ICC, Rome Statute (Article 86)**  
This card functions as an offensive spike by defining accession as a legal obligation to "cooperate fully," which logically and legally precludes the sanctions regime the 1AC documents (Superville 2025, CICC 2025, Davies 2025). It preempts the Con's likely "sanctions continue regardless" or "accession doesn't bind Trump" responses by rooting the Pro's solvency in the treaty text itself, making sanctions a violation of the very statute the US would join.

### Defensive spikes
**Card: "If further study reveals that the Court does in fact breach due process rights, the United States should propose amendments... instead of abandoning the ideas of international justice" — McKENNEY 2013**  
This card operates as a defensive spike against the Con's standard constitutional/due-process objection (e.g., "ICC lacks jury trials, violates Bill of Rights"). It preemptively reframes the objection as a reason to engage and amend from within rather than stay out, neutralizing the "unconstitutional" turnout by converting it into a solvency argument for US leadership inside the ASP.

### Evidence quality
ABSENT: The evidentiary base is inconsistent and often weak on qualifications/recency. The McKENNEY 2013 card cites an author with uncertain credentials ("it's one of two people– either this one or this one, both with degrees in something PoliSci/Law Related") and is 12 years old on a rapidly evolving institution. The Barnett '11 card is 14 years old and argues for US military hegemony, not ICC accession. The Powell 2017 and Bosco 2013 cards are qualified (CFR adjunct fellow, Oxford professor) but predate the 2025 sanctions context. The only high-quality, recent, author-qualified evidence is Davies 2025 (Guardian, ICC officials on record) and Superville 2025 (AP News). A card filling the gap would be a 2023–2025 peer-reviewed study or major NGO report (e.g., Human Rights Watch, Amnesty) with a tag like "ICC deterrence effect: quantitative analysis shows 30% reduction in civilian targeting in member states" (author, PhD, 2024, dataset of 150 conflicts).

### Collapse flexibility
**Card: "Only U.S support will help – increased resources and military backing will solidify and increase capabilities of the ICC, bringing its jurisdiction over more areas" — Bosco 2013**  
Combined with the **Sanctions** module (Superville 2025, CICC 2025, Davies 2025, ICC Art. 86) and the **Legitimacy/HR** module (URI 2024, Landau 2023, Powell 2017), the case offers three independent link chains: (1) legal — accession ends sanctions via Art. 86; (2) material — US resources expand ICC capacity/jurisdiction; (3) normative — US accession boosts legitimacy/deterrence amid rising atrocity risk (Landau 2023, URI 2024). This allows collapse to any one if another is mitigated.

### Warrant depth
ABSENT: Most tags are claims without internal warrants in the cited text. Example: "ICC is the solution, it prosecutes actors of genocide and atrocities, punishing and deterring further violence – It's empirically worked" (Powell 2017) — the excerpt asserts deterrence but provides no empirical study, mechanism, or data. "US sanctions make the ICC useless" (Superville 2025) — the AP article reports the sanction event but does not establish "useless" as a warranted causal claim; that warrant comes from CICC 2025 (a separate card). A warranted card would include a tag like "ICC deterrence mechanism: prosecution risk raises expected cost of atrocity commission for rational leaders" (citing a 2022–2024 political science article with formal model or regression).

Standards:
1 – Strat Skew:
We disclose collapses in round reports, they can prep out the arguments we’ve gone for. This is an in-round abuse—they take less prep time because they can pre-prepare weighing and offense against an argument we go for, but we can’t for them.

2 – Small Schools:
Big schools can scout and collect flows, but round reports are key for small schools—they can give an idea of what arguments people go for to prepare their strategy. Leveling the playing field makes debate fairer for small schools that can’t afford coaching or the time to prep out everything.

3 – Pre-Round Prep:
Summaries reveal back half collapse strategy—they could go for theory, turns, one case argument. That allows debaters to engage more on the flow of arguments that have been gone for, increasing clash.

4 – Verifiability:
Round reports allow people to check back against mis-disclosure by verifying what arguments were read and gone for in round.
Since we both believe disclosure is a good norm, making sure that norms are properly maintained is a pre-req to future norms being set.

Voters:
1] Fairness — Abuse prevents objective evaluation of substance and the round, which is your intrinsic role as a judge, and people get pushed out the activity when its unfair.
2] Education — It’s the reason why schools fund debate and the only portable skill.

Drop the debater – two reasons
1) Norm-setting – Voting for us sets a precedent in favor of a positive model of debate—wins and losses determine the direction of activity.
2) Reciprocity – your ballot can resolve the in-round skew that we’ve been put under

Our new paper aims to empirically test this hypothesis. Building on the work by World Bank Economist Martin Ravallion (1952–2022) and others, we create a database of “spells” by combining each pair of consecutive and comparable household surveys within a country, arriving at a database of just over 1,604 spells. As we are interested in estimating the association between economic growth and poverty (as measured by the Bank’s $2.15-a-day line) in countries that still have poverty, we remove spells with poverty rates below 2%, largely excluding countries in North America and Western Europe. Our final sample consists of 575 comparable spells between 1981 and 2021, based on 715 nationally representative household surveys for 89 countries, representing over 92% of the population in the “developing world”. The analysis indeed suggests that the extent to which economic growth translates into poverty reduction is lower in SSA, even after differences in initial characteristics are accounted for. In the overall sample, the estimated elasticity between growth in GDP per capita and poverty reduction is 2.5 (indicating that a <mark>one percent growth in GDP </mark>per capita <mark>has on average been associated with a 2.5 percent reduction in the poverty rate)</mark>. However, in SSA it is only half that (the growth elasticity of poverty reduction is 1). To a large extent, however, this pattern could be expected: Countries with high initial poverty rates (many of which are in Africa) tend to have mechanically lower passthroughs between economic growth and poverty reduction. To avoid this bias, our analysis mainly focuses on the elasticity between growth in GDP per capita on the one side and growth in per capita household consumption (as measured by surveys) on the other, which does not suffer from the same base effects.

A collapse card would tag "Economic collapse in Somaliland → Al-Shabaab foothold → regional terrorism export" (citing a 2023-2024 IGAD or UNODC threat assessment) and live at the C1-C2 pivot.

### Warrant depth

**Ferragamo/Klobucista 24** — Tag "Somaliland is flailing" gets warrants: GDP $2B, remittance-dependent, youth unemployment, brain drain, GDP per capita among lowest, WB/IMF ineligible, economy risks political progress. **Rubin 23** — Tag "AU recognizing Somaliland would open floodgates" gets warrant: US diplomats explicitly condition recognition on AU action. **Schwartz 21** — Tag "Without legal status, Somaliland deprived of FDI/loans/airspace payments" gets warrant: historical precedent (Eritrea, Timor-Leste, Montenegro, South Sudan all required parent-state assent; Kosovo blocked by Serbia). **Said 23** — Tag "FDI stimulates development" gets warrants: tech transfer, labor training, infrastructure (Mozambique, Chinese projects in Africa), positive signaling → diaspora/investor attraction. **Yusuf et al. 20** — Tag "FDI critical to African GDP" gets warrant: dynamic fixed effects, 1996-2016 panel, long-run coefficient 0.26% growth per 1% FDI/GDP, tech transfer/spillovers/capital formation mechanism. **Wu et al. 24** — Tag "GDP growth reduces poverty" gets warrant: 1,604 spells, 715 surveys, 89 countries, elasticity 2.5 globally / 1.0 SSA. **Horton 19** — Tag "Terrorism growing; Somaliland holds via HUMINT" gets warrants: Al-Shabaab thrives in Somalia (shadow gov, operational freedom), failed in Somaliland since 2008, Somaliland's community-based governance → HUMINT → security virtuous circle, despite arms embargo/budget limits. **Khan 24** — Tag "Terrorism around Somaliland ~1/3 global" gets warrant: statistical analysis 2002-2020 showing South Asia 35.2%, Middle East 33.8%, SSA 13.7% — but *does not* warrant the "around Somaliland" claim (Somaliland is in SSA/Horn; the 13.7% is all SSA). **Weakest warrant:** Khan 24 tag overreaches its evidence.

### Internal consistency

**Tension 1:** C1 argues Somaliland *needs* recognition to unlock FDI/loans (Ferragamo, Schwartz, Said). C2 argues Somaliland *currently* contains Al-Shabaab effectively *without* recognition (Horton: "Somaliland exerts consistent control... Al-Shabaab failed to establish foothold"). If Somaliland is already stable on terror, the urgency of recognition-for-economy weakens; if economy is collapsing (Ferragamo: "anemic economy puts decades of political progress at risk"), then the Horton virtuous circle is already fracturing — but the case never reconciles this. **Tension 2:** Rubin 23 claims US *will not* recognize until AU does; Schwartz 21 implies US *could* push AU via mediation ("Strong sustained mediation will be required"). The case doesn't resolve whether Pro's advocacy is US bilateral recognition (bypassing AU) or US pressure on AU. **Tension 3:** Said 23 concedes FDI short-term harms local firms ("out-competed"); Yusuf 20 finds *no* significant short-run FDI-growth relationship. The case stacks these without addressing the conceded short-term pain. **Framing uniformity:** C1 frames as economic development; C2 frames as counterterrorism. No unified "Somaliland stability" frame bridging both.

## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing
ABSENT: The case does not include explicit pre-written weighing mechanisms — such as magnitude, probability, or timeframe comparisons — embedded within the contention tags or card explanations. While impacts like "600,000 civilian deaths" (Naranjo 2023) and "state collapse" (Diamond 2019) are large, no card or tag instructs the judge *how* to weigh them against likely aff impacts (e.g., "prioritize existential civil conflict over economic benefits" or "democratic backsliding is irreversible, outpaces short-term aid gains"). A card filling this gap would carry a tag like **"Weighing: Civil conflict and state collapse are terminal, irreversible impacts that outweigh any aff economic or security claims — probability 1.0 given current frontline in Sool"** and be placed at the end of Contention 1 or as a standalone "Weighing" module.

### Offensive spikes
**Present:** The 2NC block contains explicit offensive spikes against likely aff arguments. The tagline **"Turn. Follow-on implies the IMF: Haaretz-24 writes: International law recognizes Somaliland only as an autonomous district... Somaliland cannot take part in the IMF. IMF bad:"** (Haaretz 2024) directly spikes the aff's likely claim that recognition enables IMF access and economic growth. It then extends three sub-cites (Shandra 2012, Casper 2015, Shah 2013) documenting IMF-induced food insecurity, coups, and 500,000+ child deaths annually — turning the aff's solvency mechanism into a net harm. The spike is structured as a preempt: "Their mistake is not giving you a single example of a country international aid has helped" followed by a list of failed aid cases (Greece, Zambia, Venezuela, Sudan, Burkina Faso, Somalia).

### Defensive spikes
**Present:** The 1NC embeds a defensive spike in Contention 2 via the Eubank (2012) card tagged **"History supports the NEG:"** which explicitly preempts the aff argument that recognition brings beneficial foreign aid. The warrant states: *"this analysis does suggest that the volume of foreign aid that Somaliland would likely receive with international recognition could harm the relationships that have helped shape Somaliland’s political success to date... resisting the urge to reward Somaliland’s government with regular flows of foreign assistance is its best hope of encouraging the country’s continued success."* This functions as a pre-written frontline to the aff's "aid solves democracy/economy" narrative, cutting the link before it's made.

### Evidence quality
**Mixed / PARTIAL:** Some cards meet elite standards (Eubank 2012 — peer-reviewed journal article, *Journal of Development Studies*, DOI provided, author is established political economist; Hoehne 2024 — African Arguments, subject-matter expert on Horn of Africa, recent; Crisis Group 2024 — high-prestige NGO report, on-the-ground analysis). Others fall short: Naranjo 2023 (El País) cites an estimate from a geography professor (Jan Nyssen) without primary sourcing; Diamond 2019 (Foreign Policy) is an op-ed, not empirical evidence; Tusalem 2015 (JSTOR abstract only, no full text access verified); Yusuf 2024 (Saxafi Media) is a partisan blog; Haaretz 2024 is paywalled and uncited in full. The IMF turn chain (Shandra 2012, Casper 2015, Shah 2013) uses legitimate journals but Shah 2013 is a Global Issues blog post, not peer-reviewed. **Gap:** No card carries a dataset size, regression table, or methodological detail in-tag — critical for evidence-comparison tiebreaks.

### Collapse flexibility
**Present:** The case offers three distinct, non-redundant collapse paths: (1) **Civil conflict in Sool region** (Hoehne 2024, Misteli 2024) — a localized, active frontline with recent casualties; (2) **Interstate conflict Somalia-Somaliland-Puntland-Ethiopia-Djibouti-Egypt-Turkey-Qatar-Saudi** (Crisis Group 2024, Cannon 2024) — a regional escalation ladder; (3) **Democratic backsliding via aid-induced rentierism** (Eubank 2012 x4) — an institutional mechanism independent of conflict. These links do not rely on a single trigger (e.g., "recognition" generally) but differentiate between *unilateral recognition* (Crisis Group), *MoU implementation* (Hoehne), and *aid eligibility* (Eubank). The 2NC adds a fourth: **IMF structural adjustment harms** (Haaretz 2024 + Shandra/Casper/Shah). The case is not pinned to one link.

- **"IMF programs often create winners and losers among regime elites... increases probability of coup attempt by 207 percent" (Casper 2015)** → quantitative warrant with substantive effect estimates from Table 2.  
No tag is left as bare claim + cite.

### Internal consistency
**ABSENT:** The case contains a direct framing contradiction. Contention 1 argues **Somaliland is internally fractured and recognition triggers civil war** (Hoehne: "30% of territory contested," "open fighting in Las-Anod," "frontline runs through Somaliland"). Contention 2 argues **Somaliland is a "consolidating democracy" with "peaceful transfer of power" and "reputation for political stability"** (Crisis Group 2024). These cannot both be true at the same level of generality. The 2NC even concedes instability: *"Democracies and oil reliant couhave seen civil wars in the past - Greece, Nigeria, America, Finland, Spain - Sudan Venezual"* — undermining the "democracy = stability" warrant from Tusalem 2015. A cohesive narrative would need to reconcile: e.g., "Somaliland's democracy is fragile *because* of its unresolved internal conflict, and aid/recognition shatters the clan bargain holding it together." As written, the two contentions undercut each other's credibility.

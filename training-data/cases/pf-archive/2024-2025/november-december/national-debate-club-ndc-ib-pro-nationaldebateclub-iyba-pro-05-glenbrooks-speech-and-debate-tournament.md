## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing
**ABSENT:** The case lacks explicit pre-written weighing mechanisms — no impact calculus cards (magnitude, probability, timeframe), no framing cards establishing "escalation outweighs" or "risk of war > status quo harms," and no meta-weighing tags like "our impacts are existential and irreversible." A card filling this gap would carry a tag like "Escalation to great-power war outweighs all other impacts — nuclear extinction risk is infinite magnitude and near-term" citing a source like Allison 2017 or a nuclear winter study, placed at the top of the Escalation contention to frame all subsequent cards.

### Offensive spikes
**ABSENT:** No cards preemptively answer likely Con positions such as "deterrence works," "US credibility/alliances collapse," "Taiwan falls without US support," or "China takes Taiwan regardless." An offensive spike would be a card tagged "US military support increases Chinese threat perception and accelerates invasion timeline — deterrence fails because Taiwan is a core interest" citing a source like Glaser 2024 or Fravel 2023, embedded in the Escalation contention to undercut the Con's core offensive argument before they make it.

### Defensive spikes
**ABSENT:** No embedded frontlines against anticipated responses. The case does not include cards answering "corruption purge strengthens PLA long-term," "Replicator drones escalate conflict," "allies want US commitment," or "Taiwan needs US arms for defense." A defensive spike would be a card tagged "PLA corruption purge is superficial — structural incentives for graft remain, modernization continues" citing a source like Chase 2024, placed after the Tian 23 card to insulate the "corruption weakens PLA" claim from Con rebuttal.

### Evidence quality
**ABSENT:** Evidence quality is uneven and often weak. The AFP card is a self-description of a news platform, not evidence about China. The Oxford Analytica 2018 card is 7 years old on a rapidly evolving topic. The Peck 2024 Business Insider card relies on an unnamed "report" and "respondents" with no methodology. The strongest card is Tian 23 (Reuters, named reporter, specific purge details), but it is from Dec 2023 — 11 months old. A high-quality card would be a 2024 CSIS or RAND report with satellite-imagery-verified PLA readiness metrics, tagged "PLA amphibious capacity degraded 30% since 2022 — corruption hampers shipbuilding and missile production," placed in Escalation.

### Collapse flexibility
**ABSENT:** The case presents a single contention (Escalation) with one core link chain: US support → Chinese threat perception → military intimidation/war. There are no alternative impact scenarios (e.g., economic decoupling, US budget tradeoffs, alliance entrapment, Taiwanese political autonomy). A card adding flexibility would be tagged "US arms sales trigger Chinese economic coercion — Taiwan GDP falls 2% annually, independence movement strengthens" citing a 2024 Peterson Institute study, creating a second independent advantage in a new "Economy" or "Taiwan Autonomy" contention.

### Warrant depth
**ABSENT:** Most taglines receive no substantive warrant in the cited text. The AFP card's highlight ("Xi urges military to curb corruption") does not warrant "devastating for invasion plans." The Oxford Analytica card asserts "Taiwan-US military cooperation will prompt more aggressive Chinese efforts" without explaining the mechanism. The Grossman 24 card describes Replicator but does not warrant "reset the escalation ladder" — it speculates capabilities "remain classified." The Brazinsky 23 card discusses Cold War analogies, not Taiwan. The Peck 24 card cites an unnamed report's claim about Chinese goals. No card provides a causal warrant linking US military support reduction to reduced escalation risk. A warranted card would explicitly state: "US arms sales increase PLA perception of intervention likelihood by X% (per 2024 PLA writings), raising invasion readiness" with a citation to PLA doctrinal texts.

### Internal consistency
**PARTIALLY PRESENT:** The Escalation contention and the Japan/Grossman/Brazinsky/Peck cards share a broad framing: "US military involvement in the region increases Chinese aggression and risks war." The Tian 23 card (PLA corruption weakens invasion capacity) is consistent with "China not ready for war." However, the Grossman 24 card (Replicator drones could "reset the escalation ladder") suggests US military innovation *manages* escalation — this tensions with the Pro thesis that US military presence *causes* escalation. The Peck 24 card ("allies don't share US fears") supports reducing support but undermines the "allies want US leadership" framing elsewhere. No direct contradiction, but the Replicator card sits uncomfortably in a Pro case.

### Overall construction score
**Score: 3/10** — The case is a collection of loosely related news clips without a cohesive argument structure, missing embedded weighing, offensive/defensive spikes, collapse flexibility, and warrant depth; evidence quality is low and partially outdated, and internal consistency has tensions.

Michi and I negate.
Our Sole Contention is Regional Prosperity
For the last century, Koops 20 writes that immigrants have been damned as a threat to American
Prosperity, with every modern administration taking a different attempt at curbing migration.
The consequences have been devastating; life-saving medications confiscated, and crucial employment
documentation destroyed, all in the name of deterring future entry attempts.
Thankfully, the tides are shifting. The AIC confirms that our new Senate border bill is the first legislation
in 30 years to expand legal immigration quotas and maintain paths to citizenship for children crossing the
border.
The Center for American Progress quantifies that opening citizenship pathways is the way forward,
yielding 400,000 new jobs and over $1 trillion of economic growth.
A vote for the pro irreparably alters American relations with migrant populations and restores archaic
animosities. The American Civil Liberties Union 24 writes militarization efforts at the border shift public
perception of immigrants, presuming them all to be public safety threats, and guilty until proven
innocent, making them easy targets for mass data collection.
Specifically, an expansion of the border surveillance state threatens to violate both individual prosperity
and regional stability in 3 key ways.
The first is Riskier Routes (Tangled)
For the first time in decades, immigration policies have provided migrant communities an opportunity to
live as Americans. After 6 months of asylum, migrants are granted protection or work permit eligibility.
Because of these benefits, Isacson 24 explains that illegal border crossings have reached a record low.
Unfortunately, surveillance changes this dynamic by militarizing legal entry points, Hayes 18 writes
migrant data collected from surveillance technology is used to deny asylum claims.
With nowhere to turn, vulnerable migrants are tangled in a web of institutional violence. Molnar 24
finds migrants take longer, riskier routes, leading to a threefold increase in deaths at the border. Even
worse, Gilman 24 writes these harsh policies push migrants into the hands of organized crime groups,
who promise to help them cross the border, emboldening cartel finances and human trafficking
operations.
Because trafficking is a gateway to a life of subjugation, migrants who turn to cartels find themselves in
cycles of forced labor and servitude.

Overall the Minderoo Foundation quantifies that 850,000 Mexicans are subject to modern-day slavery.
The second the Mass Deportations (ICE Age)
After years of unchecked racial violence, ICE has finally come under mass scrutiny, prompting President
Biden to cut budget allocations by 400 million dollars.
Unfortunately, a vote for the pro would re-arm America’s deportation machine.
Cahn 19 reports pools of surveillance data inevitably make their way to ICE, who IMD 18 finds are ready
to use it for mass deportations at an unprecedented scale. And even if legislation is passed to limit
overreach, Cahn furthers that it is empirically never enforced.
The stakes are simply too high as Khan 17 quantifies that 11 million undocumented immigrants are at
risk of being deported.
Moreover, Campbell 24 concludes that the US labor force fundamentally depends on immigrants,
bolstering population growth and generating 7 trillion in GDP.
Beyond our borders, Mexican Immigrants to the United States send over $58 billion to Mexico to build
infrastructure and reinforce social safety nets to pull nearly 6 million people out of poverty.
The third is US-Mexican Relations (Inside Out)
After years of fraught US-Mexican relations, tensions have begun cooling, as the US-Mexico Bicentennial
Framework in 2022 outlined a step-by-step plan to restore regional prosperity in Mexico.
This is crucial, as Wilson 12 reports strong US-Mexico Relations are key to maintaining Latin American
stability. Mexico promotes robust democracy in the Western Hemisphere, and brings Latin American
countries together, to work alongside America to fight organized crime groups, reform justice systems,
and develop grassroots crime-prevention programs, such as in the Octobor Summit.
But a vote for the pro turns US-Mexico relations inside out.
Indeed, Torres 23 explains that Mexican government officials would “strongly reject” a sweeping border
security bill. Thats because Mexican President AMLO’s drug policy is predicated on a humanitarian
approach to tackle cartels.
Empirically, the University of California 24 contextualizes that when Mexico’s pleas fell on deaf ears
during the Trump presidency, they dramatically reduced cooperation efforts.

Absent cooperation, Grinberg 19 specifies that Mexico is on the path to becoming a failed state, losing a
civil war with various rallying cartels. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs 17 reports that because Mexico
serves as the lynchpin for regional cooperation, a Mexican failed state would pull Latin America into a
tailspin, as cartels use violence to overthrow governments and cement their power.
To preserve global prosperity, negate.

such, agency has the widest scope of any of our practical activities. Our whole practical world lies under
its shadow. This is one way in which agency is inescapable for us, as inhabitants of practical worlds.
The second feature that distinguishes agency from ordinary activities is agency’s closure under itself, or,
more precisely, its closure under “the operation of reflective rational assessment” (p. 308). In my view,
this idea is underexplained in “... the Inescapability of Agency”, raising some questions about how it can
do everything Ferrero requires of it. That said, the minimal sense of ‘closure’ which Ferrero recruits in
responding to the shmagency problems is not totally opaque. Ferrero’s use of ‘closure under’ appears to
be a loose application of a set-theoretical relation more commonly used in epistemology. A set X is said
to be closed under operation Y iff the application of Y to an instance of X will always result in another
instance of X (IEP, “Epistemic Closure Principles”). For example, the set of natural numbers (or,
non-negative integers) is closed under addition, but not under subtraction. Applying the operation of
addition to a non-negative integer will always result in another positive integer, but applying subtraction
to a positive integer may result in a negative integer, falling outside of the set of natural numbers.10 In
saying that agency is closed under reflective assessment, Ferrero means that if you apply the operation
of reflective assessment (the exercise of your reflective powers) to any practical object,11 you will be
instantiating agency. And, since reflective assessment is “agency’s own distinctive operation,” this
means that “agency is closed under itself” (Ferrero 2009, p. 309). This is not a clean application of the
‘closure under’ relation in its set-theoretic sense, as talking about agency as a set feels somewhat forced,
but nonetheless it draws a useful contrast between agency and other activities. With ordinary activities
“there is always the possibility of reflecting on their justification while standing outside of them” (p.
308), suspending our engagement with their characteristic operations and our responsiveness to their
internal norms. Thus, ordinary activities are not closed under the activity of reflective assessment —
reflective assessment can lead us out of their jurisdiction. The same is not true of agency: “One cannot
put agency on hold while trying to determine whether agency is justified…. This does not mean that
agency falls outside the reach of reflection. But even reflection about agency is a manifestation of
agency” (p. 309). We cannot suspend our engagement in agency in order to reflectively assess it, thereby

escaping its jurisdiction, because reflective assessment extends its jurisdiction. This is another way in
which agency is inescapable for us: we cannot escape it across the reflective gap.

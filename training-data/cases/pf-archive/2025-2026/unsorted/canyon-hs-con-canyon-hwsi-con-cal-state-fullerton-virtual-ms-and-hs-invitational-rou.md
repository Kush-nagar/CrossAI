## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing
**ABSENT:** The case never explicitly compares the magnitude, probability, or timeframe of its impacts (e.g., 22% GDP uplift from AI vs. £96k average Brexit cost per business vs. 1,200 days of political chaos). A weighing card — e.g., "AI-driven 22% GDP growth by 2030 outweighs transitional Brexit costs because it compounds annually and affects the entire economy, not just SMEs" (McKinsey/Bhardwaj '25) — should appear in the contention overview or as a standalone "weighing" subpoint so every later speech can extend it.

### Offensive spikes
**ABSENT:** No card preempts the Aff's strongest likely offense: single-market access / trade friction with the EU, regulatory alignment benefits for services/finance, or geopolitical leverage. An offensive spike would read: "Rejoining sacrifices UK regulatory autonomy for marginal single-market gains — Dhingra/Sampson '24 (LSE) show services trade barriers persist even inside the EU; the UK's independent trade deals (CPTPP, AUS, NZ) already offset goods friction." This belongs in the Economic Prosperity contention as a third subpoint labeled "Trade autonomy > EU single market."

### Defensive spikes
**ABSENT:** The case lacks frontlines embedded in constructive. For example, no card answers "Brexit caused the UK's current stagnation" — a defensive spike would be: "UK growth lag predates Brexit; IMF '25 attributes 2024–25 outperformance to domestic policy flexibility, not EU membership" (Milliken '25 tag extended with causality warrant). Another gap: no preempt to "EU AI Act is actually innovation-friendly" — needs a card like "EU AI Act compliance costs deter startup formation — OECD '24 finds 18% drop in EU AI venture funding post-Act." These belong as "Preempts" bullets under each subpoint.

### Evidence quality
**ABSENT:** Several key cards lack author qualifications, peer review, or dataset transparency:
- Kanthan '23 (Financial IT blog) — no academic/institutional affiliation shown; op-ed style.
- Grant '18 (IEA/FEE) — libertarian think-tank, pre-dates EU AI Act & GDPR implementation; no empirical dataset.
- London '25 (Dext survey) — n=500, self-reported, commercial survey; no methodology link.
- Robinson '25 (Entrepreneur.com) — practitioner commentary, not peer-reviewed.
- Brill '23 (MGA working paper) — cites academic literature but is itself a non-peer-reviewed PDF.
To win evidence tiebreaks, the case needs at least one high-quality card per subpoint: e.g., AI subpoint — "UK pro-innovation framework attracts 3× more AI venture capital per capita than EU — OECD AI Policy Observatory '24 (panel of 38 countries, 2012–2023)." SME subpoint — "Regulatory regime changes reduce SME investment by 11% — Bank of England '24 Decision Maker Panel (n=3,000 firms, quarterly panel)."

### Collapse flexibility
The case **demonstrates** collapse flexibility via two distinct impact scenarios within the single contention:
- **AI Growth** — Tag: "Bhardwaj this year writes that AI adoption could lift the UK's economy by 22% by 2030" (Bhardwaj '25) — links to tech-sector GDP, wage growth, global competitiveness.
- **Small Business Growth** — Tag: "The UK Government finds that small businesses consist of 52% of economic turnover for the UK economy" (GOV.UK '24) — links to employment (60%), turnover, survival of 54% at-risk firms.
If the AI link is neutralized (e.g., Aff proves UK can keep AI framework inside EU), the SME stability/survival narrative stands alone; vice versa. Both are extended in the case text and evidence block.

### Warrant depth
**Mixed — partially present, partially absent.**
- **AI regulation differential** — Kanthan '23 *does* provide a warrant: contrasts EU AI Act's "legally binding," "high-risk classification" (OpenAI example) with UK's "regulate only when essential," "outcome-based," "proportionate" white-paper approach. Mechanism: compliance burden → stifled innovation → firms exit.
- **EU consensus difficulty** — Grant '18 warrant: precautionary principle + 28-country veto → "too much bother to innovate" → historical analogies (GM crops, railways). Thin on recent AI-specific data.
- **Regulatory whiplash → SME harm** — Robinson '25 + Brill '23 chain: whiplash → compliance firefighting → diverted leadership bandwidth → reduced investment/hiring; Brill adds formal uncertainty taxonomy (proposed/unfinalized/enforcement discretion/inconsistent interpretation/political reversal) → risk premia → chilled investment. Warrant present but relies on non-peer-reviewed sources.
- **UK growth claim** — Milliken '25 tag ("second-fastest-growing in G7") is a claim + cite; the card text adds IMF "one-off factors, projected temporary" but no warrant for *why* Brexit caused the growth (just correlation).
- **EU stagnation** — McKeown '25 provides data (0.1% QoQ, unemployment +0.1%) but no causal warrant linking EU membership to stagnation.
**Missing warrants:** No card explains *why* rejoining forces UK to adopt EU AI Act (could UK negotiate opt-out?); no card quantifies "regulation whiplash" cost vs. Brexit adaptation cost already sunk (£96k).

### Internal consistency
**ABSENT — tension between contentions.**
- **AI subpoint** argues UK *benefited* from Brexit via regulatory freedom → "The UK is doing so great in AI because they left the European Union" (Kanthan '23).
- **SME subpoint** argues Brexit *imposed heavy costs* → "Brexit cost UK businesses an average of £96,281 each" (Pardo '24) and "73% of UK businesses report being less profitable" (same card).
- The case simultaneously claims Brexit *caused* AI leadership (offense) and Brexit *cost* SMEs heavily (defense: "they've already paid the price"). This undercuts the cohesion of "Economic Prosperity" — is Brexit a net economic win or a sunk cost we must protect? A consistent framing would either: (a) argue net benefit (AI gains > SME costs) with a weighing card, or (b) argue SMEs have *recovered* (OIM '24 "little evidence of substantial change") so past costs are irrelevant — but Pardo '24 says costs *persist* and 73% are *still* less profitable. The case needs to resolve this: either drop Pardo '24 or add a card "SME profitability rebounded 2023–24 — ONS '25 shows 12% YoY profit growth post-adjustment."

### Overall construction score
**Score: 4/10** — The case has two viable collapse paths and some warranted links, but lacks embedded weighing, offensive/defensive spikes, high-quality evidence on key claims, and suffers an internal inconsistency that lets Aff collapse the debate to "Brexit hurt the economy; rejoining fixes it."

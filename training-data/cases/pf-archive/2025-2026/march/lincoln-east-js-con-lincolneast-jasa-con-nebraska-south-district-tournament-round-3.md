## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing
ABSENT: The case contains no explicit comparative weighing mechanism (magnitude, probability, timeframe, reversibility, or prerequisite framing) linking its impacts to a coherent decision calculus. Cards are presented as standalone claims (e.g., “homelessness causes 16x sudden death,” “Democrats win midterms”) without a meta-argument explaining why the judge should prefer the Con’s impact cluster over the Pro’s. A card filling this gap would carry a tag like “Weighing — Scope & Probability: Corporate-ban harms are immediate, certain, and affect millions; Pro’s speculative benefits are long-term and contested” and would sit at the top of the case or as a dedicated “Framing” contention.

### Offensive spikes
ABSENT: No card is deployed to proactively puncture a likely Pro argument (e.g., “corporate buyers crowd out first-time buyers,” “institutional investors raise rents,” “ban reduces speculation”). The case plays pure defense/turn on nonprofits and midterms but never fires a preemptive shot at the resolution’s core mechanism. An offensive spike would be a card tagged “Corporate bans backfire — they reduce liquidity for nonprofit purchasers” (citing a housing-finance study) or “Institutional owners maintain units better than mom-and-pop landlords” (citing a property-maintenance dataset), placed in 1NC to force Pro to answer a disadvantage they didn’t initiate.

### Defensive spikes
ABSENT: The case lacks embedded frontlines that anticipate and neutralize specific Pro responses. For instance, Pro will almost certainly argue “nonprofits can still buy as LLCs/individuals” or “the ban only targets for-profit corporations.” No card preemptively defines “corporate acquisition” to include nonprofit corporations (Foundation Group ND comes closest but is used offensively, not as a definitional shield) or answers “nonprofits are a tiny share of purchases.” A defensive spike would be a card tagged “Definition — ‘Corporate’ includes nonprofit corporations under IRS code” (citing a statutory interpretation source) or “Empirics — Nonprofits account for 15%+ of single-family acquisitions in target markets” (citing a transaction database), inserted immediately after the Nonprofits contention.

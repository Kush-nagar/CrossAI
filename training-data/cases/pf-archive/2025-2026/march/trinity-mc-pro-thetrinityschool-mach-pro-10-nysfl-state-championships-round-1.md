### Embedded weighing
**ABSENT:** The case contains no explicit weighing mechanisms — no timeframe, magnitude, probability, or scope comparisons pre-written into the evidence. No card includes language like "outweighs on magnitude," "comes first on timeframe," or "controls the direction of the link." A card filling this gap would be a weighing card tagged "Magnitude — US military action causes exponentially more death than any alternative" citing a comparative study (e.g., Kavanagh & Frederick 2023's finding that interventions don't improve outcomes but do add casualties) placed at the end of the contention or as a standalone "Weighing" subpoint.

### Offensive spikes
**ABSENT:** No cards preempt specific negative positions (e.g., "hegemony good," "deterrence works," "allies need US security guarantees," "counterterrorism requires intervention"). The Kavanagh & Frederick 2023 card ("Why Force Fails") *could* function as an offensive spike against "intervention solves" arguments by proving interventions don't achieve objectives, but it is not framed or deployed as a preempt — it sits as a generic link card. An offensive spike would explicitly tag: "Spike — Interventions fail to achieve US objectives even on their own terms — Kavanagh & Frederick 2023" and be placed where the neg's "US solves" cards would be answered.

### Defensive spikes
**ABSENT:** No embedded frontlines against likely negative responses. The case does not include cards answering "withdrawal creates power vacuums," "US presence deters great power war," "sanctions don't work," or "allies will proliferate." The Mortlock & O'Toole 2018 sanctions card is a *link* card (sanctions as alternative), not a *frontline* answering "sanctions fail." A defensive spike would be a card tagged "Frontline — Power vacuum claims ignore empirical record of US withdrawal improving stability" citing a study on post-withdrawal outcomes (e.g., Vietnam, Afghanistan post-2021) placed after the second subpoint.

### Evidence quality
**ABSENT:** Evidence quality is uneven and several cards fail an evidence-comparison tiebreak. Only Hathaway 2019 (Yale Law professor, DoD experience) and Kavanagh & Frederick 2023 (Foreign Affairs, RAND-affiliated scholars, database of 222 cases) meet elite evidence standards. A competitive case would replace IBON, Chien, and Amnesty with peer-reviewed studies or government reports.

### Internal consistency
**ABSENT:** The case contradicts itself on the "alternatives" question. If outcomes are "largely the same" with or without intervention (Kavanagh), then US presence doesn't *cause* the additional deaths in Subpoint 2 — those deaths would occur anyway. The Mortlock & O'Toole sanctions card (sanctions as "middle ground") further muddles the narrative: if sanctions work, why does Kavanagh say non-intervention (which includes sanctions) yields same outcomes as intervention? The case lacks a unified theory of when US action helps/harms.

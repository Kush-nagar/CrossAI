Why Gamblers Choose Unlicensed Operators
Despite the legal risks, many gamblers continue to engage with unlicensed online operators.

Several key factors drive this behavior:

Better Odds and Higher Payouts
Unlicensed operators often offer more favorable odds and higher payout rates compared to their regulated counterparts. Licensed operators must adhere to taxation and compliance costs that cut into profitability, resulting in less competitive payouts for players.

Greater Game Variety and Innovation
Due to rigid regulatory constraints, licensed gambling operators often have limited offerings compared to their unlicensed competitors. Unregulated sites can offer a wider selection of games, including innovative betting formats, live dealer games, and cryptocurrency-based gambling.

Fewer Restrictions and Looser Betting Limits
Licensed operators impose various restrictions to ensure responsible gambling, such as deposit limits, self-exclusion tools, and mandatory cooling-off periods. While these measures promote player safety, some gamblers find them overly restrictive and turn to unlicensed sites where such limitations do not exist.

Access to Offshore Betting Markets
In jurisdictions where certain types of gambling (e.g., sports betting, online poker, or online casinos) are either prohibited or heavily restricted, players often turn to offshore operators that cater to their specific needs. These sites bypass local laws, offering a more seamless gambling experience.

Anonymity and Privacy
Regulated gambling operators require users to provide extensive personal information for identity verification and anti-money laundering (AML) compliance. Some gamblers prefer unlicensed operators because they allow for anonymous gambling, particularly when using cryptocurrencies.

Challenges Faced by Regulators in Eliminating the Black Market
Despite ongoing efforts to enforce gambling laws, regulators struggle to eliminate the presence of black-market operators. Several challenges contribute to this difficulty:

Jurisdictional Limitations and Enforcement Barriers
Online gambling is a global industry, and many unlicensed operators are based in jurisdictions with lax or nonexistent regulations. This makes enforcement difficult, as regulators have limited authority over offshore entities.

Ineffective <mark>Blocking Measures</mark>
Some regulators attempt to block access to unlicensed sites through domain blacklisting or ISP restrictions. However, these measures <mark>are</mark> often <mark>circumvented by VPNs</mark> and mirror websites, rendering them ineffective.

Lack of Consumer Awareness
Many gamblers are unaware of the risks associated with using unlicensed operators, including lack of legal recourse in case of disputes, potential fraud, and exposure to problem gambling. The black market thrives on the lack of consumer education about these dangers.

Rigid and Overly Restrictive Regulatory Approaches
Most state and provincial gambling regulators take a punitive and safety-focused approach, emphasizing responsible gambling at the cost of market competitiveness. This stifles innovation and drives players toward more flexible, player-friendly alternatives offered by unlicensed operators.

The Downside of Traditional Regulatory Approaches
While player protection and responsible gambling initiatives are essential, an excessively restrictive regulatory environment can create unintended consequences:

Stifling Competition Among Licensed Operators
Strict regulations, high taxation, and compliance burdens limit the ability of licensed operators to offer competitive promotions, bonuses, and game variety. This results in a less appealing market for consumers who seek better value elsewhere—often with the unregulated illegal market.

Creating Barriers to Entry for New Market Players
High licensing fees and operational requirements deter new operators from entering the market, leading to a lack of competition and innovation in the regulated sector. A monopolized market reduces consumer choice and incentivizes players to explore unlicensed alternatives.

Overburdening Operators with Compliance Measures
While responsible gambling measures are critical, excessive restrictions on deposits, betting limits, and self-exclusion requirements can alienate casual gamblers who do not perceive themselves as high-risk players.

Tactics for Regulators to Eliminate the Black Market
To create a more sustainable and competitive gambling environment while reducing black-market activity, regulators could adopt a more balanced and pragmatic approach. The following strategies can help achieve this objective:

Creating a Sustainable Regulatory Framework for Legal Operators

A well-regulated gambling market should ensure legal operators remain competitive while maintaining strong consumer protections. Steps to achieve this include:

Lowering Tax Burdens and Licensing Fees: Competitive taxation encourages more operators to enter the market, increasing competition and improving player value.
Allowing More Innovation: Regulators could enable greater flexibility in game offerings, betting structures, and payment methods (including cryptocurrencies) to meet player demand.
Flexible Responsible Gambling Measures: Instead of one-size-fits-all restrictions, regulators could offer customizable player protection tools that cater to different risk profiles.

Facilitating <mark>a More Uniform Regulatory Framework</mark> Across Jurisdictions

Gambling regulation varies widely across jurisdictions, creating inefficiencies that hinder effective monitoring and enforcement. A more harmonized regulatory approach <mark>would</mark>:

<mark>Encourage Interstate</mark> and International <mark>Cooperation: Cross-border agreements</mark> can help <mark>streamline compliance, reduce regulatory inconsistencies, and improve enforcement efforts against illegal operators.</mark>

Standardize Licensing Requirements: <mark>A more uniform</mark> licensing <mark>process across jurisdictions would make it easier for operators to comply</mark> while maintaining high industry standards.

Improve Information Sharing: Regulators should collaborate with financial institutions, internet providers, and international regulatory bodies to identify and take action against unlicensed operators more effectively.

Relegating the Black Market to a Small Percentage of Gambling Activity

Regulated operators should be positioned as the dominant choice for players by making the legal market more attractive than its unlicensed counterpart:

Offering Competitive Bonuses and Rewards: Licensed operators should be permitted to offer competitive promotional incentives that rival unlicensed sites.

Enhancing User Experience and Technology: Regulators should support the adoption of modern payment solutions, AI-driven responsible gambling tools, and mobile-first gaming experiences.
Public Awareness Campaigns: Educating consumers on the risks of black-market gambling and the benefits of regulated operators can drive more traffic to the legal sector.

Maximizing State and Provincial Tax Revenue from Legal Gambling

A thriving legal gambling market ensures increased tax revenues for state and provincial governments, which can be reinvested in social programs and responsible gambling initiatives. Achieving this requires:

Tax Optimization Strategies: High tax rates on legal operators drive players to unlicensed sites. Regulators should assess optimal taxation levels to maximize revenue while maintaining competitive payouts.
Encouraging Market Growth: Expanding the scope of legally regulated gambling—such as legalizing online casinos and sports betting where currently restricted—can capture a greater share of consumer spending.

Investing in Advanced Monitoring Technologies: Using AI and blockchain-based solutions for real-time compliance monitoring can enhance regulatory oversight and enforcement efforts.
Conclusion
State and Provincial gambling regulators face a complex challenge in eliminating the black market of unlicensed operators. The appeal of better odds, fewer restrictions, and greater innovation keeps many gamblers engaged with unregulated platforms. However, excessively punitive and restrictive regulatory frameworks often push players away from the legal market. By adopting a more balanced approach—one that fosters competition, enhances consumer experience, and promotes regulatory uniformity—governments can significantly reduce black-market activity. Achieving true market dominance for licensed operators will not only ensure robust player protections but also maximize State and Provincial tax revenues, creating a sustainable and responsible gambling ecosystem for the future.

Understanding the agency’s budget, sources, and allocation provides insight into the scale of its regulatory and law enforcement capacity.
The Source of Federal Trade Commission Funding
The FTC’s operations are primarily funded through the annual Congressional appropriations process, which is classified as discretionary spending. Congress determines the agency’s total funding level each fiscal year, sourced from a combination of general funds and “offsetting collections.” Offsetting collections reduce the need for general taxpayer funds and are composed of fees mandated by specific legislation. These include the Hart-Scott-Rodino (HSR) premerger filing fees and fees collected from the national Do Not Call (DNC) Registry. The HSR fees, paid by companies involved in large mergers and acquisitions, are the largest non-appropriated funding source.
Recent Annual Budget Figures
The total authorized funding levels for the FTC fluctuate based on economic conditions and Congressional priorities. For Fiscal Year (FY) 2025, the enacted appropriation level for the FTC was $425.7 million, representing the actual funding amount approved for the agency’s operations.

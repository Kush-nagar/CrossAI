## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing
**ABSENT:** The case contains no explicit weighing mechanism (e.g., "magnitude," "probability," "timeframe," or "scope" comparisons) embedded in taglines or card text. Cards like Flink 26 and SK 25 assert state superiority but never comparatively weigh state regulation against federal regulation on a shared metric. A card filling this gap would carry a tag like "State regulation outweighs federal on magnitude — 38 states already regulate, covering 90%+ of market" (cite: AGA 2025 market data) and belong in the "Local Realities" section to anchor the framework.

### Offensive spikes
**Yu 25** — "The US gaming industry operates under a complex legal and regulatory system that involves both federal and state laws... Each state decides which of these activities are permitted within its borders and establishes its own licensing and compliance systems."  
**How it executes:** Preempts the Pro's likely "federal uniformity solves patchwork problems" argument by establishing that the status quo already functions through state-by-state licensing, making federal intervention redundant.

**AGA 25** — "69% of Americans say that each state should decide whether to allow sports event contracts within its borders, underscoring the importance of state-level authority."  
**How it executes:** Spikes the Pro's democratic legitimacy claim by showing public opinion favors state control, turning a Pro "federal action reflects national will" argument into a Con offensive asset.

### Defensive spikes
**Adler 2007** — "Federal regulation will, to some extent, act as a substitute for state regulation... The existence of federal regulation will reduce the demand for state regulation... federal regulation has the potential to crowd out state-level environmental protections, even if the quantity of environmental protection demanded in the state is greater than that provided by the federal government."  
**How it executes:** Preempts the Pro's "federal floor + state ceiling" rebuttal by providing a mechanistic warrant (substitution effect) for why federal frameworks become ceilings, not floors — directly answering the most common Pro frontline.

**Michaelian 19** — "Any new federal regulations would likely allow states to opt out of authorizing any type of gambling within their state. New federal governance would surely impede on the states' ability to fine tune their regulations to how they see fit."  
**How it executes:** Defends against "states can still supplement federal rules" by evidencing that opt-out provisions and preemption logic strip state flexibility — a specific answer to the "cooperative federalism" Pro rebuttal.

**INCONSISTENCY DETECTED:** The "Trump" mini-contention (Kirkwood 25, Bruggeman 25) argues the FTC is *corrupt/partisan and will under-regulate* (capture by Big Tech, Trump's own betting app).

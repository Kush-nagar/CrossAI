Examining 11 treatment states with differing patterns of legalization across online and offline gambling channels, we find that online sports gambling spending increased by 369% following legalization (from $0.99 to $4.63 per individual-month), while the rate of irresponsible gambling – defined as monthly gambling expenditures exceeding 1% of income – rose by 372% (from 0.2% to 0.9% of individuals). ## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing
**ABSENT:** The case lacks explicit comparative weighing mechanisms embedded in the evidence. No card contains language that pre-emptively weighs the Pro's impacts (consumer protection, addiction reduction, financial harm mitigation) against likely Con impacts (states' rights, regulatory capture, economic benefits of state-level innovation, black market persistence). A card filling this gap would carry a tag like "Federal framework outweighs state experimentation — uniformity prevents regulatory arbitrage that fuels black markets and addicts vulnerable populations" and would cite a federalism scholar (e.g., Robert Mikos or Ernest Young) analyzing why minimum national standards in vice regulation produce net welfare gains over patchwork state regimes. It belongs in the 1AC overview or as a standalone "weighing" contention.

### Offensive spikes
**ABSENT:** No card proactively answers core Con positions: (1) anti-commandeering/Tenth Amendment challenges to FTC authority, (2) state laboratories of democracy arguments, (3) regulatory capture of a federal agency by major operators (DraftKings/FanDuel), or (4) the "illegal market persists regardless" claim. An offensive spike card would read: "FTC regulation preempts state capture — federal oversight reduces operator lobbying leverage by centralizing venue" (citing a political economy study like Carpenter & Moss 2014 on regulatory capture in fragmented vs. unified jurisdictions). It should appear in Contention 1 or 2 as a preemptive frontline.

### Defensive spikes
**ABSENT:** The case contains no embedded frontlines to the specific responses it will face. There is no card saying "FTC has rulemaking authority under Section 5 — Murphy v. NCAA confirms Congress can regulate sports betting directly" (answering "FTC lacks jurisdiction"), or "Federal floor doesn't prevent state innovation — states can exceed minimum standards" (answering "one-size-fits-all fails"), or "Black market shrinks when legal market is trustworthy — UK Gambling Commission data shows 90% channeling" (answering "prohibition doesn't work"). A defensive spike card would carry a tag like "Section 5 authority + Murphy = FTC can act — no non-delegation problem" (citing a 2023-2024 admin law piece on FTC UDAP rulemaking post-West Virginia v. EPA). It belongs at the bottom of each contention.

### Evidence quality
**ABSENT:** The evidence base is critically weak on author qualifications and source tier. Of 14 cited sources: 6 are law review student notes (Schreiber, Graham, Malnar, Miller, plus two unattributed excerpts) with no author credentials provided; 2 are advocacy/org reports (NCPG, AIBM) without peer review; 2 are media summaries (CNBC, Stateline) of underlying academic work; 1 is a ZipDo commercial statistics aggregator (Eser 25) with no methodology; 1 is an SSRN working paper (Taylor 25) not yet peer-reviewed; only 1 (Eisenshtadt 22) is a published law review article from a credentialed professor (Cole Eisenshtadt, Administrative Law Review). No card cites a government report (GAO, CBO, FTC), a top-tier economics journal (AER, QJE, JPE), or a recognized regulatory scholar with institutional affiliation. A card filling the gap would be: "GAO-24-106789 (2024) — Federal sports betting oversight feasible; FTC has expertise in consumer financial protection" or "AER-inspected study: 'National regulation reduces problem gambling 22% vs. state patchwork' (citing a 2023-2024 NBER working paper by Lindo, Schmerling, et al.)." These belong as the primary warrants for Contention 1 (harms) and Contention 2 (solvency).

### Collapse flexibility
**ABSENT:** The case funnels all offense through a single narrative: "state patchwork fails → federal framework solves consumer protection/addiction/fraud." There is no independent link chain: no contention on sports integrity/match-fixing (separate from consumer harm), no contention on interstate commerce/tax revenue leakage, no contention on data privacy/cybersecurity of betting apps, no contention on youth/adolescent targeting via social media. A card creating a second collapse option would tag: "Federal framework stops interstate fraud rings — state AGs cannot pursue cross-border operators" (citing a 2023 DOJ/IC3 report on sports betting cybercrime). Another: "FTC data-minimum standards protect bettor PII — state laws ignore biometric/location tracking" (citing a 2024 IAPP or FTC staff report). These belong as Contention 3 and 4, allowing the 2AR to collapse to integrity or privacy if consumer protection is mitigated.

### Warrant depth
**ABSENT:** Most taglines are asserted claims without the underlying causal mechanism explained in the card text. Examples: "Federal framework reduces addiction" (Malnar 20) — card describes a committee funded by revenue but never explains *how* that committee's programming reduces addiction rates (what interventions, what evidence they work). "FTC should ban 'risk-free' ads" (Eisenshtadt 22) — card asserts the phrase is misleading like cigarette samples but provides no study showing "risk-free" language increases problem gambling initiation. "Online betting causes bankruptcy" (Larsen/AIBM) — card reports correlations with statistical controls but the excerpt omits the identification strategy (difference-in-differences? event study?) and the magnitude of the treatment effect on the treated (not just population averages). A warrant-rich card would read: "Taylor 25 (SMU) — DiD on 230k households: legalization → 369% spending spike → 372% rise in >1% income gambling → effect driven by existing gamblers, not new users — mechanism: frictionless app access + loss-chasing" (the actual SSRN abstract provides this; the case excerpt truncates it). The case needs to *keep* the full warrant text, not just the conclusion.

### Internal consistency
**ABSENT:** The case contains a direct tension between two solvency mechanisms. Malnar 20 advocates a **federal advisory committee** (leagues, players, addiction experts) that "nudges states into adopting more uniform laws" — a soft-law, cooperative federalism model.

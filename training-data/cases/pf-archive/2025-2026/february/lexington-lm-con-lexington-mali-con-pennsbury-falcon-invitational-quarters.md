This compliance overview details the core principles that govern advertising and the consequences of violating these federal guidelines. The
Core Standard for Deceptive Advertising The FTC determines whether an advertisement is deceptive by
applying a comprehensive three-part test focused on the consumer experience. First, the
agency examines the advertisement for any representation, omission, or practice that is likely
to mislead consumers acting reasonably under the circumstances. This analysis includes not only explicit
statements but also implied claims that the overall context or visual presentation might suggest to a typical viewer. The second component
requires determining whether the consumer’s interpretation of the claim is reasonable, meaning the consumer’s belief must be one that a
significant minority of the target audience would also share. A claim is evaluated based on the net impression it creates on the average person,
rather than requiring the claim to be literally false. The standard for reasonableness is applied based on the sophistication and vulnerability of
the audience targeted by the specific advertisement. Finally, the agency must establish that the misleading representation or omission is
material, meaning it is likely to affect the consumer’s decision regarding the product or service. Information concerning product attributes, cost,
performance, safety, or guarantees is presumed to be material because it directly influences purchasing behavior. Advertisements that hide
significant costs, use misleading “before and after” comparisons, or fail to disclose important limitations are common examples of practices that
meet this standard for deception.

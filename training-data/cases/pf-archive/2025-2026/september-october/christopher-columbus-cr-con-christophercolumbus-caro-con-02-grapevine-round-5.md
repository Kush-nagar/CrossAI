Rejoining the European Union is no simple matter, just as leaving it was tricky and complicated. Even if everyone in the United
Kingdom voted to rejoin the EU, that would not automatically make the United Kingdom an EU member state. The UK would have to
apply to join. It would need to attain EU candidate status. This could take several years. It would then need to be accepted
to rejoin. Turkey has been stuck on candidate status since 1987!
There are 27 EU member states. For the UK to be allowed to rejoin would require unanimous support. There is still some
rancour over Brexit in other EU member states.
Some British Europhiles reckon the price of rejoining the EU would be too high. The EU might accept the British back,
subject to several provisos. Cyprus might demand the retrocession of the British Sovereign Base Areas of Akrotiri
and Dhekelia. The Republic of Ireland might demand Northern Ireland. Spain might demand the annulment of the
Treaty of Utrecht, which makes Gibraltar the territory of the UK. The EU might insist that the UK scrap the Pound
Sterling and adopt the Euro as its currency. The United Kingdom might be obliged to join the Schengen Agreement and pay more to the
European Union. The UK might be required to agree never to leave again.
The terms of accession might be too ugly. On the other hand, there are those in the EU who would welcome back the United Kingdom with
open arms. The UK rejoining would be the ultimate vindication. Therefore, it makes sense to offer the British as alluring a package as
possible.
The UK could join the EU again in the same way it joined before, and as it joined the European Economic Community: by a vote in Parliament,
not by a referendum. There is an elite consensus about rejoining. If there were a referendum, it would probably be before the terms of a

## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing
**ABSENT:** The case contains no explicit weighing mechanisms — no magnitude, probability, timeframe, or reversibility comparisons between impacts. Cards like Callaghan '25 ("40% chance... not for at least 10 years and more like 20") and Miguel 25 ("reset" of relations) establish factual predicates but never translate them into comparative weighing language (e.g., "outweighs on magnitude because..." or "probability >10x higher than AFF solvency"). A card filling this gap would carry a tag like "Weighing — Con's DA turns case: UK rejoining triggers veto gridlock that collapses EU cohesion *before* AFF can access any benefit — Callaghan '25 timeline proves 10-20yr delay means AFF impacts never materialize" and would sit as a standalone subpoint under Contention 1 or as an overview.

### Offensive spikes
**ABSENT:** No card preemptively attacks a specific AFF contention or link story. The case reads defense (veto gridlock, opt-out demands) but never says "AFF's [X] contention fails because [Y]." For example, Small '21 describes UK as "China's chief ally" blocking EU tariffs on Chinese steel — this could be an offensive spike tagged "Spike — AFF claims EU unity counters China; UK rejoining *reverses* that unity by restoring China's intra-EU advocate — Small '21" placed before Contention 1. Instead, Small '21 is used only as link support for Con's own China DA.

### Defensive spikes
**Present:** Callaghan '25 — "If the UK does rejoin the EU, it will not be for at least 10 years and more like 20. The chance that the UK eventually rejoins the EU is 40%." This functions as a defensive spike against AFF solvency: it preempts "UK rejoining solves [X]" by establishing a 10-20 year delay and sub-50% probability, making AFF impacts non-unique or time-barred. It is embedded in the "Integration Fails" module, directly answering the core AFF mechanism (rejoining).

**Mixed — Callaghan '25 (George Callaghan; Reporter for Madhyamam; 08-11-2025)** demonstrates recency (August 2025) and topic-specificity, but author quals are thin — "Reporter for Madhyamam" (an Indian Malayalam-language daily) with no stated expertise in EU constitutional law or UK-EU negotiations. **Score: 6/10** — The case executes collapse flexibility and internal consistency at a high level with three clean, non-contradictory impact paths and a coherent "status quo good, reentry bad" narrative, but loses points for absent embedded weighing, absent offensive spikes, thin author quals on the newest cards, and one major tag-overclaim (Barford '19). It is structurally sound but strategically incomplete — built to extend, not to close.

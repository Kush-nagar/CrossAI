## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing
ABSENT: The case presents a collection of evidence on UK economic decline (Inman 2025), Eurozone weakness (Kirby 2025), Brexit trade damage (Chu 2025, Du 2024), EU customs union limitations (Berg 2025), growth-poverty links (Burchi 2022), poverty-mortality data (Mueller 2023), and NHS crisis (Castle 2024, Keen 2022, Campbell 2022), but nowhere does it include explicit comparative weighing arguments—such as "magnitude: a 4% GDP loss outweighs any sovereignty benefit," "probability: OBR forecasts are the government's own official assumptions," or "timeframe: NHS collapse is immediate while EU renegotiation takes years"—embedded in card text or taglines that could be extended in rebuttal/summary. A card filling this gap would be a 2024-2025 think-tank brief (e.g., Institute for Fiscal Studies or Resolution Foundation) tagged "Weighing — Magnitude & Probability: UK GDP loss of 4% (£100bn/yr) is the largest single structural shock to UK welfare; OBR/BoE consensus makes it >80% likely; NHS mortality impacts are already occurring" placed as a standalone weighing module after the Du 2024 evidence.

### Offensive spikes
ABSENT: The case contains no evidence that preempts likely Con positions such as "UK can negotiate bespoke deals/Swiss-style agreements," "sovereignty/regulatory autonomy outweighs economic cost," "EU is stagnating so rejoining locks UK into decline," "rejoining requires adopting Euro/Schengen/CAP," or "transition costs of rejoining exceed long-term gains." A card filling this gap would be a 2024-2025 CER or UK in a Changing Europe brief tagged "Spike — Bespoke Deals Fail: Swiss model replicates single market rules without vote; EU rejects sectoral cherry-picking; any deep access requires dynamic alignment" placed after Berg 2025, and a second card tagged "Spike — EU Stagnation False: Eurozone per-capita growth matches UK since 2016; single market deepening (digital, green, capital markets) creates new gains from rejoining" placed after Kirby 2025.

### Defensive spikes
ABSENT: The case has no embedded frontlines answering the specific responses it will face: "OBR 4% figure is a long-run static model ignoring dynamic gains," "UK services exports are up post-Brexit (Chu admits this)," "NHS staffing recovered via non-EU recruitment (Campbell admits offset)," "customs union ≠ single market so trade friction remains (Berg admits)," or "rejoining requires unanimous EU27 approval — political impossibility." A card filling this gap would be a 2024 OBR/IFS response note tagged "Frontline — OBR 4% Is Conservative: Excludes services barrier reduction, FDI rebound, and regulatory certainty gains; dynamic models show 6-7% long-run cost" placed after Du 2024, and a 2024-2025 Nuffield Trust update tagged "Frontline — Non-EU Recruitment Unsustainable: Red-list recruitment ethically/legally fragile; retention worse; EU staff have 2x retention rates" placed after Campbell 2022.

### Evidence quality
The Du 2024 card (Aston Business School, Centre for Business Prosperity, full report September 2024) demonstrates high evidence quality: four named academic authors with institutional affiliations, a 2024 dataset covering monthly trade data 2021-2023 with robustness checks, and sector-level granularity (consumer/intermediate/capital goods, 27 EU partners) that wins evidence-comparison tiebreaks against generic news summaries. The Chu 2025 card (BBC Verify, January 2025) also meets the standard: named specialist correspondents (Ben Chu, Policy & Analysis; Tamara Kovacevic), cites OBR as the government's official forecaster, and references specific methodological debates (counterfactual construction, 6% vs 30% range) showing transparency. However, the Inman 2025 and Kirby 2025 cards are single-month newspaper snapshots (Guardian/WSJ, August 2025) with no author expertise beyond journalism; Burchi 2022 and Mueller 2023 are tangential (developing-country multidimensional poverty, US poverty-mortality) with no UK/EU relevance; Berg 2025 is a CER blog post, not peer-reviewed; and the three NHS cards (Castle 2024, Keen 2022, Campbell 2022) include two from 2022, predating post-2023 policy changes.

### Collapse flexibility
The case offers three distinct impact pathways — trade/GDP (Du 2024, Chu 2025), poverty/mortality (Burchi 2022, Mueller 2023), and NHS workforce/supply (Keen 2022, Campbell 2022, Castle 2024) — allowing collapse to any one if another is mitigated. However, the poverty/mortality pathway is structurally detached: Burchi 2022 studies low/middle-income countries (not UK) and Mueller 2023 studies US poverty-death links, so they do not function as UK-specific links. The trade and NHS pathways are UK-specific and independent (goods trade vs. services/health), providing real collapse flexibility between two viable scenarios. A card filling the poverty gap would be a 2024-2025 Resolution Foundation or IFS brief tagged "UK Poverty Link: Brexit-driven 4% GDP loss → 1.5M additional in relative poverty (IFS 2024); UK-specific mortality gradient (Marmot 2024)" placed after Chu 2025.

### Warrant depth
The Du 2024 card provides deep warrants: it explains the mechanisms (extensive margin export variety -33%, intensive margin import value -28%), identifies drivers (NTMs/SPS standards, geographic proximity, market structure), and shows heterogeneity (consumer goods -43% varieties, agrifood hardest hit, chemicals resilient). The Chu 2025 card warrants the 4% GDP claim by citing the OBR's counterfactual methodology and noting the 15% trade reduction assumption. The Berg 2025 card warrants the customs union limitation by citing EU treaty constraints (member-state only), Turkey precedent (movement certificates, VAT exclusion), and FTA incompatibility. In contrast, the Inman 2025 and Kirby 2025 cards are descriptive snapshots with no causal warrants linking current confidence drops to Brexit or rejoining. The Burchi 2022 and Mueller 2023 cards have warrants for their specific findings (growth-poverty elasticity, poverty-mortality risk) but zero warrants connecting them to UK rejoining the EU.

### Internal consistency
ABSENT: The case suffers from framing incoherence. The trade cards (Du 2024, Chu 2025) argue Brexit caused structural trade decoupling requiring single market re-entry; the Berg 2025 card argues a customs union (the most likely "rejoin" short of full membership) "substantially cut[s] paperwork at the border but not eliminate it" and is incompatible with UK's independent FTAs — directly undercutting the trade benefit claim. The Kirby 2025 card shows Eurozone sentiment falling (95.2, down from 95.7) and Germany shrinking (-0.3% Q2), contradicting the narrative that rejoining a growing bloc solves UK stagnation. The poverty cards (Burchi 2022, Mueller 2023) apply developing-country and US evidence to a UK context where absolute poverty mechanisms differ. The NHS cards (Keen 2022, Campbell 2022) document Brexit damage but the Berg card implies a customs union wouldn't restore free movement of workers (EU treaties reserve customs union for members; Turkey model lacks labor mobility), leaving the NHS workforce link unresolved. A consistency fix would require either: (a) committing to full membership (single market + customs union + free movement) and cutting the Berg card, or (b) re-tagging Berg as a "Con spike we answer" and adding a card "Full Membership Required: Only Article 49 accession restores free movement of workers/services/capital; customs union alone fails NHS & services" placed after Berg 2025.

### Overall construction score
**Score: 4/10** — The case has one elite evidence set (Du 2024) and a strong secondary source (Chu 2025), but lacks embedded weighing, offensive/defensive spikes, and internal consistency; the poverty pathway is evidentiarily unmoored; and two of three impact scenarios rely on pre-2023 or non-UK evidence.

Due to a combination of the precautionary principle enshrined in the Lisbon Treaty and the difficulties of

creators of new technologies to prove their invention is safe where some risk may exist—even if there’s no scientific consensus to
suggest any actual harm will occur. The result? It’s often too much bother to innovate. During the 19th century, many viewed the emerging railways

fear. Missed Technological Opportunities Of course, moral panic over new technology is nothing new. Now, as in the 1850s, over-cautiousness risks hampering important drivers of future growth. So far, the European Union has taken

the latest in a long line of missed technological opportunities. Take genetically modified crops. Since their commercialization in many parts of the world during the 1990s, GM crops have raised the quantity and quality of the global

precautionary thinking has meant a de facto ban on GM crops, only one variety of which has ever been approved and grown in Europe. While farmers outside the EU continue to develop newer, better technologies, hysteria over

## Construction-criteria audit

### Embedded weighing
ABSENT: The case contains no explicit weighing mechanisms — no magnitude, probability, timeframe, or scope comparisons pre-written into the tags or card text. A card filling this gap would carry a tag like "AI GDP uplift (22% by 2030) outweighs any transition costs — magnitude and permanence" (Bhardwaj '25) or "Banking sector collapse triggers immediate global spillover — probability and reversibility" (Véron '25 + Grant '22), placed at the end of each subpoint to extend in Summary/Final Focus.

### Offensive spikes
ABSENT: No cards preempt likely Pro arguments such as "EU single market access boosts trade," "regulatory alignment reduces compliance costs," or "UK loses influence over EU rulemaking." A spike would be a card tagged "Rejoining EU forces regulatory harmonization that eliminates UK's competitive deregulation advantage — link turn to Pro's 'market access' claim" (e.g., Arnold '25 on trapped capital/liquidity), placed after the banking internal link or as a separate mini-contention.

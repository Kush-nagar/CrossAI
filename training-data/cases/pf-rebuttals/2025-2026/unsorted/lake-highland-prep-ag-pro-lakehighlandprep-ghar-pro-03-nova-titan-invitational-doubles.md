1 -- AI development is declining now.
Kevin Dallas 25. 8/7/2025, CEO of EnterpriseDB. "The UK is falling behind in the global race for digital sovereignty." TechRadar. https://www.techradar.com/pro/the-uk-is-falling-behind-in-the-global-race-for-digital-sovereignty //arrguy

At London Tech Week we heard Kier <mark>Starmer make</mark> a <mark>commitment to</mark> the <mark>UK</mark> people that <mark>we would “become</mark> an <mark>AI maker, not</mark> an <mark>AI taker”. But</mark> how did this shift from an assumed frontrunner to an AI underdog happen?
Economic and geopolitical instability, including tariffs and ever-changing political alliances, has caused global technology leaders to realize that both their physical and digital infrastructure is best kept in-region. This allows them to protect themselves and not let innovation be hampered by outside forces. This has led to what we are seeing with Starmer’s comments - investment and commitment towards keeping AI systems developed and controlled within the UK.
Enterprises and governments are being shown that sovereign AI and data platforms are no longer a nice-to-have, but a must-have. These are defined as open source-based systems where data and AI are governed together at the edge, on-prem, or in-country cloud. This feels new to many organizations as it requires more than just turning a dial to more or less cloud.
Where we stand today
Research shows that the <mark>UK</mark> is <mark>falling behind AI innovations, despite strong</mark> IT <mark>infrastructure and</mark> a <mark>robust workforce</mark> in place. Top <mark>enterprise</mark> leaders <mark>aren’t</mark> currently <mark>matching</mark> the <mark>government’s urgency or investment focus</mark> on AI and data. This <mark>disconnect</mark> is particularly <mark>stark in</mark> the <mark>banking sector</mark>, which was <mark>seen as</mark> the <mark>UK’s most likely AI growth engine</mark> only a year ago.
Sovereignty over AI and data must be mission-critical and applied quickly, for every economy and every enterprise within it. If we continue to hesitate, concerns are that the UK could lose its economic edge. In today’s world, if you can’t control your data and AI, you’ll struggle to stay ahead.
So what needs to be done to fix this growing problem and reposition the UK as an AI leader with a solid base to scale in-region?

While <mark>position</mark>ing <mark>itself as a global leader in</mark> artificial intelligence (<mark>AI</mark>) regulation with the EU AI Act, the EU has not historically been a global leader in innovative AI achievements. In a move to better position the <mark>EU</mark> as a global leader in AI, the European Commission (the Commission) <mark>unveiled the “AI Continent Action Plan</mark>” on April 9, 2025. This ambitious plan will <mark>involve investing €200 billion</mark> towards increasing the EU’s AI capabilities, aiming to transform Europe's robust talent pool and prowess in traditional industries into engines that will <mark>accelerate AI innovation</mark> in the EU.

Brazil The Big Push After Legalization Following the legalization of fixed-odds betting, Brazil has
intensified its efforts to curb unlicensed operators. The National Telecommunications Agency (Anatel), in
collaboration with the Secretariat of Prizes and Bets (SPA), has blocked over 5,200 illegal gambling sites
since October 2024 and submitted over 12,500 domain names for blocking. This initiative is part of a
broader strategy to ensure that only authorized entities operate within the country's regulated market.
Beyond domain blocking, Brazil has implemented strict payment restrictions. Operators are prohibited
from accepting deposits via credit cards or cryptocurrencies, forcing them to use approved methods,
such as Pix, the national instant payment system. This measure aims to prevent financial transactions
with unlicensed platforms and enhance consumer protection. To further strengthen enforcement, Anatel
and SPA signed a Technical Cooperation Agreement in December 2024. This pact facilitates a rapid
exchange of information, enabling swift action against illegal operators. Despite these moves,
enforcement has struggled to keep pace. Domain cycling, evasive hosting, and fragmented oversight
across Anatel, COAF, and the judiciary have made coordinated crackdowns difficult. Singapore A
Controlled Model Built on DPI Singapore doesn’t just block illegal gambling sites. It tracks, filters, and
neutralizes them with quiet precision. In early 2025, under the Gambling Control Act 2022, enforcement
powers shifted to the Singapore Police Force, giving authorities more direct control to dynamically block
offshore operators in real time. But it’s not just about pulling the plug. Singapore’s tech stack goes
deeper, deploying tools like DPI and Layer-7 filtering to inspect traffic, flag gambling-related activity, and
take down access with remarkable speed and efficiency. On mobile devices, ISPs are enlisted to enforce
network-level filtering, while VPN detection tools catch attempts to sneak around the system. Over 3,800
gambling sites and SGD 37 million worth of transactions have already been blocked using these methods.

Businesses often fall into the trap of viewing compliance as a checklist designed only to prevent
penalties. This limited perspective turns compliance into a reactive measure, something pursued out of
fear rather than opportunity. Yet, regulatory compliance holds far greater potential. When approached
as a strategic asset, compliance unlocks trust, strengthens reputations, enhances operations, and builds
a lasting foundation for growth. The evolution of modern business environments, especially with
increased digitalization and global expansion, has made regulatory landscapes more complex. However,
those same complexities carry opportunities for businesses that prioritize compliance as a core part of
their strategy. Such companies not only avoid costly fines but also cultivate resilience, attract top-tier
talent, and earn stakeholder loyalty in ways that elevate them above competitors.

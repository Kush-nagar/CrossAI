1. [T] Encryption protects trafficking victims
IS 20 [IS, "Fact Sheet: Understanding Encryption: The Connections to Survivor Safety", 12/18/2020, Internet Society,
https://www.internetsociety.org/resources/doc/2020/understanding-encryption-the-connections-to-survivor-safety/. The Internet Society is an
American non-profit advocacy organization whose purpose is to promote an open and safe internet. Founded in 1992, it has offices in Reston,
Virginia, United States, and Geneva, Switzerland, with local chapters around the world. Accessed 11/28/2025] //GKA
For many survivors of domestic violence, sexual violence, stalking, and trafficking, having private and secure ways to
communicate and store files electronically is a vital part of their safety. For victim service providers and other professionals working with survivors
of abuse, using communication and storage tools that prioritize privacy and security help ensure they’re supporting the survivor’s privacy and safety. Strong
encryption is a critical part of the solution. Here are some examples of how E2E encryption and no-knowledge encryption can help mitigate the impacts
of technology-facilitated abuse. Safety Planning & Relocating: End-to-end encryption can provide a secure channel for a survivor to make plans and
communicate with trusted individuals, like when they are trying to relocate and secure housing and support. (This is particularly true when
the abusive person does not have access to the survivor’s devices or accounts.) Protecting the Integrity of Evidence: Using E2E and no-knowledge encryption
when storing and transferring digital evidence to law enforcement, prosecutors, or other professionals in the legal system
helps maintain the integrity of that evidence. When digital evidence is passed through insecure sources or there is the possibility of interception, the integrity of the
evidence can be diminished, increasing the likelihood that its authenticity will be called into question in a court of law. Protection Against
Unauthorized Access: Unauthorized access to data can happen when a communication being sent (data in transit) is intercepted or when
information stored in an online database or online file vault (data at rest) is breached. This is why it’s critical that all data – that in transit and that at rest – be
protected with the highest level of encryption. Information communicated without E2E encryption is at an increased risk of being breached by third parties.
Furthermore, information stored without no-knowledge encryption is at increased risk of being breached. Both types of breaches can negatively impact
a survivor’s privacy, safety, and well-being by revealing sensitive information. This could result in their abuser finding them after
they’ve relocated, or to the loss of evidence related to their pending court case, or to revealing
personal information that could negatively impact their ability to secure or maintain housing or
employment. Seeking Help: E2E encryption allows survivors to communicate safely and securely when they decide to seek help. It helps protect against
interception, deletion, and alteration. It helps ensure that private communications stay private, so that only the sender’s and intended recipient’s devices can access
the messages.

1. [NL] Terrorists use TOR - no solvency
Shai 25 [Farber, Shai, "The evolving nexus of cybercrime and terrorism: A systematic review of convergence and policy implications",
04/23/2025, SpringerLink, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41284-025-00471-7. Dr. Shai Farber is a senior lecturer at the School of
Law and Public Policy at the Academic College of Law and Science [Hod Hasharon], where he serves as Head of the Public Policy Department and
a senior faculty member at the Faculty of Law. In his previous position, he was a senior faculty member in the Department of Criminology. Dr.
Farber holds a legal education and has been a licensed attorney since 2006. He has authored two books and published 40 articles in refereed
journals., Accessed 11/25/2025] //GKA
Over the past decade, the growth of the dark web and the development of advanced anonymization technologies have provided threat
actors within the cybercrime-terrorism nexus with powerful tools to conceal their identities and activities. The dark web, accessible
through encrypted networks such as Tor, serves as a platform for trading illicit goods, coordinating criminal operations,
and recruiting extremists, all while maintaining a high degree of anonymity and evading the scrutiny of law enforcement
(Weimann 2016). Dark web forums and marketplaces have become a hub for cybercriminals and terrorists to share
knowledge, acquire tools and services, and plan attacks (Türen et al. 2023). These hidden platforms enable the trade of
stolen data, hacking tools, weapons, and other illicit goods, facilitating the convergence of criminal and
terrorist activities (Tavabi et al. 2019). The use of anonymity-enhancing cryptocurrencies, such as Monero and Zcash, further compounds the challenges of
tracing and investigating illicit transactions on the dark web (Foley et al. 2021). These cryptocurrencies employ advanced cryptographic techniques to obscure the
identities of senders and recipients, as well as the amounts being transacted, making it extremely difficult for law enforcement to track the flow of funds (Foley et al.
2019). Moreover, cybercriminals and terrorists have access to a wide range of anonymization tools and techniques that enable
them to further conceal their online activities and evade detection. These include the use of virtual private networks (VPNs), proxy servers, and
anonymous communication platforms like I2P and Freenet (Gehl 2018). Metadata stripping, code obfuscation, and strong encryption further enhance the ability of
threat actors to anonymize their malware, communications, and attacks, making attribution and investigation increasingly challenging (Park et al. 2021).

Hall 15 [Joseph Lorenzo Hall. .4-20-2015, 10-30-2025. The NSA’s Split-Key Encryption Proposal is Not Serious. https://cdt.org/insights/the-nsas-split-key-encryption-proposal-is-not-serious/.]//sag
<mark>NSA Director</mark> Michael Rogers has launched a new trial balloon to address what law enforcement and intelligence agencies are calling “Going Dark.” As reported by Ellen Nakashima and Barton Gellman’s on a front page story in the Washington Post, <mark>Admiral Rogers shared a proposal that would require tech companies to create a</mark> <mark>“golden key</mark>” that would allow access to encrypted data and communications. <mark>The</mark> new twist in Rogers’ <mark>proposal was to cut this golden key into pieces</mark> so that no one entity – the NSA or the company making the product – could decrypt the data without all the pieces of the key.
Sorry Admiral Rogers, but requiring <mark>split-key encryption is not a serious proposal.</mark>
It’s time to stake that trial balloon to the ground of technical reality. Sorry Admiral Rogers, but requiring split-key encryption is not a serious proposal.
The technical community and the US Government have long been engaged in a simmering fight about encryption, dubbed the “crypto wars.” While many had thought that the crypto wars had all but ended in the late 1990s, we were clearly wrong. The FBI has been arguing for years that it is “going dark,” namely that methods of wiretapping telephones don’t work on the internet or with encrypted data. The NSA has also been engaged in a variety of efforts to undermine cryptography. The government has been concerned in the past that strong encryption would be helpful to criminals, terrorists, and foreign nations, so it has long maintained that cryptography should be carefully controlled. It all but lost the series of earlier battles in the war: strong encryption is available worldwide to protect data and communications, and it is a fundamental feature of the modern internet and world wide web.
<mark>FBI Director</mark> James Comey <mark>was unable to cite a single instance when encryption frustrated FBI investigations.</mark>
And yet, despite this, the sky has not fallen. Even as encryption has become a common tool of internet and communication technologies, it has not proven problematic to preventing crime in the manner predicted. In fact, when recently speaking in support of anti-encryption mandates, FBI Director James Comey was unable to cite a single instance when encryption frustrated FBI investigations.
To boot, the US Government has flirted with essentially the exact proposal from Adm. Rogers in the past. When CDT was merely three years young, in 1997, we coordinated a technical report entitled, “The Risks of Key Recovery, Key Escrow, and Trusted Third-Party Encryption”. In the report, we demonstrated that <mark>there would be no provable secure way to communicate using split key</mark> key escrow systems, so certain types of sensitive transactions involving health information, financial information, and intimate <mark>information would be more vulnerable to interception in the case of a flaw, compromise, or abuse of the system.</mark> Also, <mark>securing repositories</mark> of keying material, <mark>validating requests</mark> for keys, <mark>and distributing keys</mark> <mark>would be</mark> exceedingly complex, and likely much <mark>more complex than the underlying encryption itself</mark>. This is costly to say the least, but it can also be dangerous in that <mark>adding complexity to a system will inevitably lead to additional methods to undermine it and find vulnerabilities that can be used to attack it.</mark>
If the United States creates a split-key encryption mandate, autocratic countries are likely to follow suit and require access to encrypted services.
<mark>The split key approach also raises a host of concerns for business,</mark> privacy, and human rights. If the United States creates a split-key encryption mandate, autocratic countries are likely to follow suit and require access to encrypted services, a measure already being pursued in China despite U.S. criticism. <mark>This mandate would</mark> also leave <mark>US companies</mark> – that have already <mark>lost significant business abroad in recent years due to surveillance concerns</mark> – labeled by foreign competitors as “NSA accessible.” American companies won’t be able to assure foreign or domestic customers that their data is secure if the NSA hacks into the company, as reportedly happened to Google and Yahoo. A company would also face logistical issues if a customer requested a recovery key, but the company had to coordinate with the government to provide it. And because criminals could still use foreign electronic products and software, the mandate would not even cut off their access to encryption technology and would “hobble” US security products that seek to use modern cryptography (such as encryption that uses very short-lived keys, called “perfectly forward secret” encryption).

In recent years, technology has dramatically transformed almost every aspect of our lives—and the world of drug trafficking is no exception. As law enforcement agencies become more sophisticated, drug traffickers have also embraced cutting-edge technologies to enhance their operations. From communication methods to surveillance and logistics, technological advancements now play a crucial role in the efficiency, secrecy, and success of modern drug trafficking.
While traditional trafficking methods relied on couriers and physical routes, today’s drug cartels use digital tools to manage operations, track shipments, and evade detection. Understanding the role of technology in drug trafficking helps us comprehend the scope of this illicit industry and highlights the challenges faced by law enforcement agencies in combating it.
Digital Communication Tools: The Backbone of Modern Cartels
One primary way technology has reshaped drug trafficking operations is through digital communication tools. Cartels now heavily rely on encrypted messaging apps, such as Signal, WhatsApp, and Telegram, to communicate covertly. These platforms provide a high level of security, ensuring that drug traffickers can plan their operations without the risk of interception by law enforcement agencies.
Additionally, <mark>the rise of the “dark web” has given traffickers an online marketplace where</mark> <mark>they can buy, sell, and distribute illicit drugs while staying largely anonymous</mark>. These hidden platforms facilitate transactions involving cryptocurrencies, further complicating the tracing of illegal activities. While <mark>law enforcement continues to track these developments, traffickers</mark> <mark>have adopted increasingly sophisticated encryption techniques, making monitoring their</mark> <mark>digital movements harder.</mark>
These tools provide essential benefits for drug cartels. They allow quick and efficient coordination across vast distances, reducing the risks of face-to-face meetings or phone calls. Furthermore, encrypted communication ensures that law enforcement cannot easily intercept or decode traffickers’ messages, giving traffickers a safer environment to plan.
Drones: Changing the Smuggling Game
The rise of drone technology has revolutionized drug smuggling in ways that traditional methods never could. Drones enable traffickers to bypass traditional checkpoints and carry small amounts of drugs over borders or into hard-to-reach areas. These uncrewed aerial vehicles (UAVs) are faster than conventional transport methods and more challenging for authorities to detect.
Smuggling drugs via drones allows cartels to operate in a more decentralized manner. Instead of relying on human couriers who must navigate roadblocks, checkpoints, and border patrols, traffickers can send drones to drop off shipments in remote locations or drop them directly into prison yards for incarcerated members of the cartel. Drones have increased dramatically over the past decade, especially in regions where traditional routes are heavily monitored.
Additionally, drones are versatile. They can carry lightweight packages and are equipped with advanced GPS tracking systems, allowing traffickers to plan their deliveries precisely. However, as this technology becomes more widely used, law enforcement agencies have started to adapt by developing counter-drone systems to detect and disable these UAVs.
GPS and Geospatial Technology: Aiding Logistics and Tracking
Another key technology that has empowered drug trafficking is the Global Positioning System (GPS) and geospatial technology. Drug cartels can now track the movement of their shipments with pinpoint accuracy, allowing traffickers to avoid detection and take alternative routes if necessary.
GPS tracking has also enabled cartels to manage their logistics operations better. With the ability to track shipments in real time, traffickers can adjust delivery schedules, reroute shipments, or even track rival operations. Cartels use this information to stay one step ahead of law enforcement, making it much harder for authorities to dismantle their operations.
Geospatial technology also aids in strategically positioning drug production facilities and hidden stash houses. Traffickers can now use satellite imaging and other geospatial data to identify areas difficult for law enforcement to surveil. This intelligence enables cartels to optimize their supply chain and hide their operations where they are least likely to be detected.
Blockchain and Cryptocurrency: Facilitating Anonymous Transactions
Cryptocurrency and blockchain technology have revolutionized how money moves in illicit markets, including drug trafficking. With cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin and Ethereum, drug cartels can conduct transactions without the need for traditional banking systems, which are often easier to track.
Blockchain provides a decentralized ledger, making it nearly impossible to trace financial transactions. As a result, traffickers use cryptocurrencies to launder money and pay for operations while maintaining a high degree of anonymity. The use of cryptocurrency allows drug traffickers to remain anonymous, hiding their financial dealings and making it harder for authorities to trace the flow of illicit funds.
Moreover, traffickers have also begun using cryptocurrency to make payments for goods and services, including weapons, fake passports, and even bribes for officials. This ability to move vast sums of money anonymously has made cryptocurrency an essential tool in modern drug trafficking.
The Use of Social Media: A New Frontier in Recruitment and Communication
Social media platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, and Twitter have also found their way into drug trafficking operations. While many of these platforms are used for legitimate purposes, traffickers often use them to recruit new members, arrange meetings, and advertise illegal products.
Traffickers may create fake profiles to disguise their identities and engage with potential drug buyers or sellers. Social media also helps them monitor the market, stay updated on law enforcement activities, and communicate with suppliers and buyers.
With the ability to reach thousands, if not millions, of potential customers, social media has become a powerful tool for expanding drug trafficking operations. Although platforms have taken measures to
monitor and block illegal content, traffickers continue to adapt by using encrypted messaging apps or smaller, less regulated social media platforms.
The Role of Artificial Intelligence in Surveillance and Detection
As drug cartels continue to use technology for their operations, law enforcement agencies are also turning to advanced technology to counter these activities. Artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning are now employed to enhance surveillance and detect unusual activities within digital networks.
AI-driven systems can process vast amounts of data quickly, identifying patterns that would be difficult for human analysts to uncover. For example, AI can detect encrypted messages or track patterns in cryptocurrency transactions, which could indicate drug trafficking. Additionally, AI-powered drones are being used by law enforcement to monitor borders and detect the movement of drugs and traffickers.
By using AI, law enforcement agencies can stay ahead of the technological innovations traffickers use. However, the constant battle between traffickers and law enforcement technology is ongoing, with each side working to outsmart the other.
The Battle Between Innovation and Regulation
Technology has undeniably changed the landscape of drug trafficking. While it has provided traffickers with the tools to operate more efficiently, covertly, and profitably, it has also enabled law enforcement to develop new methods to combat this growing problem. The use of drones, encryption, blockchain, and AI has made drug trafficking operations more sophisticated than ever. Still, it also offers governments and international agencies new opportunities to fight back.
As technology continues to evolve, so too must the methods used to combat drug trafficking. The future will likely see an even greater convergence between technology and criminal activity, making it essential for governments, law enforcement agencies, and tech companies to work together to stay one step ahead. The battle between traffickers and authorities is far from over, but with innovation on both sides, the fight against drug trafficking can continue to evolve.

When talking about education<mark> people </mark>often <mark>confuse</mark> it <mark>with schooling. </mark>Many think of places like schools or colleges when seeing or hearing the word. They might also look to particular jobs like teacher or tutor. The problem with this is that while looking to help people learn, <mark>the way a lot of schools</mark> and teachers <mark>operate is not </mark>necessarily something we can properly call <mark>education</mark>. They have chosen or fallen or been pushed into ‘schooling’ – trying to drill learning into people according to some plan often drawn up by others. Paulo Freire (1973) famously called this banking – making deposits of knowledge. Such ‘schooling’ too easily descends into treating learners like objects, things to be acted upon rather than people to be related to.

<mark>Education</mark>, as we understand it here, <mark>is </mark>a process of<mark> inviting truth</mark> and possibility, of <mark>encouraging </mark>and giving time to <mark>discovery</mark>. It is, as John Dewey (1916) put it, a social process – ‘a process of living and not a preparation for future living’. In this view educators look to learning and being with others rather than acting upon them. What Is <mark>Schooling</mark>?
Dictionary.com offers three common <mark>definitions</mark> for schooling:.
the process of <mark>being taught in a school</mark>.
instruction, education, or training, especially when received in a school.
the act of teaching.

As we can see from the definition of schooling, <mark>education is a separate concept.</mark> Schooling may include education, but it’s certainly not limited to education.

The descriptions given by the dictionary are the bluntest, most concise meanings of the word. However, practically and culturally, schooling is a much more nuanced concept. We’ll examine these nuances later.

What Is Education?
According to Dictionary.com, <mark>education </mark>has several definitions, but we’ll look at the first three as they relate most to the scope of this article to keep things even.

<mark>the </mark>act or <mark>process of </mark>imparting or <mark>acquiring </mark>general <mark>knowledge</mark>, <mark>developing </mark>the powers of <mark>reasoning and judgment,</mark> and generally of preparing oneself or others intellectually for a mature life.
the act or process of imparting or acquiring particular knowledge or skills, as for a profession.
a degree, level, or kind of schooling
<mark>Education encompasses </mark>the <mark>entire learning </mark>experience. While it<mark> can include</mark> knowledge obtained in a formal <mark>school setting, </mark>it’s<mark> not limited to this environment. </mark>

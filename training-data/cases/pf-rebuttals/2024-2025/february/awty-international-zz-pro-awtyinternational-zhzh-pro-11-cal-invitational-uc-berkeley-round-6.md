1. NU - The US military is waning. 
Filkins 2/3 [Dexter Filkins, journalist @ the New Yorker, 2-3-2025, "The U.S. Military’s Recruiting Crisis", New Yorker, https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2025/02/10/the-us-militarys-recruiting-crisis (accessed 2-7-2025)] ME
In 2022 and 2023, <mark>the Army missed</mark> its <mark>recruitment goal by</mark> nearly twenty-five per cent—about <mark>fifteen thousand </mark>troops<mark> a year</mark>. It hit the mark last year, but only by reducing the target by more than ten thousand. <mark>The Navy</mark> has also fared badly: it <mark>failed</mark> to reach its goals in 2023, then met them in 2024 by filling out the ranks with recruits of a lower standard; nearly <mark>half</mark> <mark>measured below average</mark> on an aptitude exam. The Army <mark>Reserve</mark> hasn’t met its benchmark since 2016, and the <mark>ranks</mark> <mark>are</mark> so <mark>depleted</mark> that active-duty officers have been put in charge of reserve units. Some experts worry that, if the country went to war, many reserve units might be <mark>unable to deploy</mark>. A U.S. official who works on these issues put it simply: “We can’t get enough people.” At the end of the Second World War, the American military had twelve million active-duty members. It now has 1.3 million—even though the population has more than doubled, and women are now eligible for armed service. “The U.S. <mark>military</mark> has been <mark>shrinking</mark> <mark>for thirty years</mark>,” Lawrence Wilkerson, a former senior State Department official who leads a task force on the challenges facing the armed services, said. “But its global commitments haven’t changed.” The military operates out of bases in more than fifty countries, and routinely deploys Special Operations forces to about eighty. Now, Wilkerson said, “it’s not clear that the military is large enough anymore for America to uphold its promises.”

Secessionist violence has been increasing
BTI “West and Central Africa.” BTI, 16 Apr 2024, https://bti-project.org/en/reports/west-and-central-africa. Accessed 8 Jan. 2025.//CT
The security situation remains tense in several West and Central African states, imposing a substantial strain on the governance capacities of these countries. In four cases,<mark> conflict intensity has escalated</mark>, namely in Burkina Faso, Chad, Liberia and Sierra Leone. In another four cases, however, we see a decline in conflict intensity: the high-intensity conflict observed in the Central African Republic has eased, as have the more moderate intense conflicts in Guinea and the Republic of the Congo, as well as the low-intensity conflict in Gambia. Violent conflicts <mark>driven primarily by rebellion, jihadism and</mark>, to a lesser extent, <mark>separatism</mark>,<mark> continue to present significant challenges</mark> to various states within the region. Jihadism is taking its toll on the Sahel region in particular, impacting countries like Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger.

Continued militarization within the region could potentially either help to stabilize the security situation or exacerbate existing tensions. The fact remains that all governments in the region – except those of Gambia and Mali – have substantially increased their military spending during the reporting period. According to data from SIPRI, Togo registered an 80% rise in military spending from 2021 to 2022, marking the highest growth in expenditure during this span. Following Togo are Guinea, with 43.3%, and Niger, with 28.9%. Nigeria’s military spending reached its peak in 2021, with an investment of approximately $4.4 billion in absolute numbers, which is 10 times greater than the average expenditure of all states in the region.
<mark>Anti-democratic actors</mark>, including the military, parties or companies,<mark> can be found in many countries </mark>across the region. The capacity of reform agents to manage these forces generally remains weak. Exceptions can be seen in Gambia, Ghana and Senegal. However, the situation is quite different in Equatorial Guinea, the two Congos and Chad. In Chad, under the leadership of the deceased president’s son, military rule has solidified. A protest in October 2022 against the extension of the transitional period, intended to lead to elections, was brutally suppressed by security forces. The circumstances are also challenging for reform advocates in Mali, where coups occurred in 2020 and 2021, leaving military juntas assuming now in power. In Burkina Faso, Mali’s neighboring country, the prominence of anti-democratic actors is doubly apparent due to the two coups that transpired in 2022.

Escalation is happening
Mustaqbal 12/9 [Mustaqbal; 12-9-2024; Mustaqbal Media - (English); "Heavy Clashes Erupt Between Somaliland and SSC-Khaatumo Forces in Erigabo," https://mustaqbalmedia.net/en/heavy-clashes-erupt-between-somaliland-and-ssc-khaatumo-forces-in-erigabo/; accessed: 12-21-2024] ds recut tristan
Fresh details have emerged about a <mark>heavy battle</mark> that <mark>took place</mark> last night <mark>between Somaliland forces and SSC</mark>-Khaatumo <mark>fighters</mark> in the town of Ceerigaabo, the capital of Sanaag region in northern Somalia.
Local sources reported that the confrontation erupted in the middle of the town. However, the exact casualties from the clashes remain unclear, as the incident occurred during nighttime hours.
So far, neither side has released an official statement regarding the skirmishes in Ceerigaabo.
Reports further indicate that tensions remain high in the town this morning. Armed <mark>troops and</mark> military <mark>vehicles</mark> have reportedly been <mark>deployed</mark> to the area, creating fear and unease among the local population.
In recent days, the region has been reportedly on edge, with public demonstrations taking place to protest a series of killings in the district. The SSC-Khaatumo leadership has accused Somaliland forces of being behind the violence.
At the same time, Somaliland has yet to respond to these accusations or address the escalating tensions in the region.
These clashes come amid recent political developments in Somaliland, following the election of President Abdirahman Cirro. However, the ongoing <mark>renewed conflicts</mark> <mark>indicate</mark> that the <mark>tensions</mark> between Somaliland and SSC-Khaatumo <mark>remain unresolved and continue to escalate.</mark>

Secession’s inevitable.
Marche 21 [Stephen Marche, columnist @ Washington Post, 12-31-2021, Perspective, Washington Post, https://www.washingtonpost.com/outlook/2021/12/31/secession-civil-war-stephen-marche/] tristan
It’s not that secession can’t work. The rest of the world is busily at it all the time. <mark>Separatism is a global</mark> political <mark>trend</mark>. <mark>The number of nations</mark> in the world <mark>has tripled since 1945</mark>. And there will soon be more. “Right now, there are about 60 secessionist movements worldwide. Sixty independence movements is a pretty large number by historical standards,” says Ryan Griffiths, a professor at Syracuse University who focuses on the dynamics of secession and the study of sovereignty. “<mark>In the long run,</mark> there will be another secessionist movement in the United States. <mark>It will just happen</mark>. <mark>No country is permanent</mark>. It will change. It will break apart in some way.”

Clan violence flaring post election
CrisisGroup 1-7. December Trends 2024 And January Alerts 2025. Crisis Group, 7 Jan. 2025, https://www.crisisgroup.org/crisiswatch/december-trends-2024-and-january-alerts-2025#somaliland. Accessed 8 Jan. 2025.//CT
<mark>New administration took power but violence flared between govt and</mark> Dhulbahante <mark>clan forces.</mark>
New administration promised push for international recognition. President Irro sworn in during 12 Dec inauguration ceremony, concluding fraught election cycle that culminated in peaceful Oct elections; Irro pledged to intensify efforts to gain international recognition for Somaliland. U.S. ambassador to Somalia attended Irro’s inauguration amid growing speculation that incoming U.S. President Trump could increase ties with govt – and potentially even announce diplomatic recognition of Somaliland – after he takes office in Jan 2025. Waddani party announced govt priorities including pacifying eastern regions, health and infrastructure; many smaller clans given ministry-level govt positions.
Security forces clashed with Dhulbahante clan in Sanaag region. <mark>Armed confrontations in Sanaag capital </mark>Erigabo – home to Haber Yonis, Dhulbanate and Warsengeli clans – <mark>continued</mark> following late-Nov retaliatory killing;<mark> govt forces mobilised</mark> in support of Haber Yonis clan, <mark>clashing with militias from SSC-Khatumo</mark> (self-declared administration for Dhulbahante community) including 14 Dec in city; UN reported <mark>43,000 displaced</mark>. Tensions then moved outside Erigabo with Dhulbahante fighters remobilising in areas 20-30kms south west of city, raising fears of further clashes.
In another important international development. FM 11 Dec said Ethiopia had “insisted” it had not withdrawn from Jan 2024 deal despite Addis Ababa-Mogadishu diplomatic agreement (see Somalia, Ethiopia); FM noted Somalia-Ethiopia declaration did not mention deal with Hargeisa.

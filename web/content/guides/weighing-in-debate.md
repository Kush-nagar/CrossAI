Weighing tells the judge why your argument matters more than theirs. If both teams win some offense, the team with better weighing usually wins. Even strong defense doesn't save you if the other team wins any risk of offense *and* the weighing.

## The common mechanisms

- **Magnitude:** how big the impact is. More people, more harm.
- **Probability:** how likely it is to happen. Fewer steps in the link chain, stronger evidence.
- **Timeframe:** how soon it happens. Impacts that come first can prevent or shape later ones.
- **Scope:** how many people or how much of the world it reaches.
- **Severity:** how bad the harm is for each person affected.
- **Reversibility:** whether the harm can be undone. Deaths and extinction can't.
- **Brink:** how close we are to the impact right now. A strong brink makes your link matter immediately.

## Weighing has to compare

"Our impact is big" isn't weighing. "Our impact affects two million people; theirs affects a single industry" is. Always name their impact and say why yours comes first.

## Link-ins and prerequisites

Sometimes your argument causes theirs, or theirs depends on yours:

- **Link-in:** "Their economic harms only happen through our instability scenario, so we access their impact too."
- **Prerequisite:** "No economy survives a war, so resolving the conflict comes first."

These are some of the strongest weighing because they take the other team's impact for yourself.

## When both teams weigh: metaweighing

If they say "prefer magnitude" and you say "prefer probability," the judge needs a reason to pick. That's metaweighing:

- "Prefer probability: magnitude is meaningless if the impact doesn't happen, and their scenario needs four unlikely steps."
- "Prefer timeframe: our impact happens first and changes whether theirs can happen at all."

## Where to weigh

Start in [rebuttal](/guides/pf-rebuttal) if you can, so the back half is framed early. Weigh in [summary](/guides/pf-summary) and repeat it in [final focus](/guides/pf-final-focus). The best weighing is already set up in your case: see [how to write a PF case](/guides/how-to-write-a-pf-case).

title: How to frontline in debate
description: How to answer responses to your own case in second rebuttal and summary, and how to get faster at it before the round.

---

Frontlining means answering the other team's responses to your case. In Public Forum it starts in **second rebuttal** and continues into summary. Teams that frontline well get to go for their case in the back half. Teams that don't end up re-reading their contention while the judge flows "conceded."

## Get better before the round

Frontlines are mostly built before you walk in.

- **Know your case deeply.** Read the full articles behind your cards, not just the highlighted lines. Context is where analytical answers come from.
- **Do background research first.** Spend the first days of a topic reading, not cutting. You can't predict responses to an area you don't understand.
- **Predict the responses.** For each contention, write down the likely non-unique, no-link, link-turn, impact-defense and impact-turn responses. Most of them are predictable by the second week of a topic.
- **Rep it.** Give practice frontlines out loud against a response you haven't seen. Speed comes from repetition, not from more files.

## How to frontline in the round

1. **Group responses that share a flaw.** "Responses one through three all assume the policy is enforced. Our Smith card says it isn't." One answer, three responses gone.
2. **Answer the most dangerous response first.** Turns before defense: a turn left standing is offense for them, while defense only mitigates.
3. **Weigh inside the frontline.** "Even if their delay response is true, our impact happens first."
4. **Extend as you go.** Re-state the link and impact in one clause so the argument stays alive on the flow.
5. **Don't frontline what you're kicking.** If you plan to collapse to contention one, spend almost nothing on contention two. Make that call before the speech, not during it.

## Labeling helps

Name each response by type when you answer it: "Their first response is non-unique...". Judges flow faster, and you'll spot which kinds of responses you keep losing to.

## Time split in second rebuttal

In a fast round, second rebuttal is often split roughly evenly between frontlining your case and attacking theirs. Adjust by which of their responses are most damaging and which of your arguments you want to go for in summary. Every response you frontline now is one less thing summary has to clean up.

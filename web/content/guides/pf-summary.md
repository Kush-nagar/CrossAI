---
title: How to give a Public Forum summary
description: How to choose what to collapse on, extend it cleanly, and close the door on the other team's path to the ballot in 3 minutes.
order: 4
---

Summary is 3 minutes, and it's where most rounds are actually decided. Its job is to **collapse**: go for less, and win it completely.

## Pick the collapse

Choose the argument that gives you the easiest path to the ballot. In order of importance:

1. **The most weighable offense**, especially offense that interacts well with their weighing.
2. **The least answered argument.** The one with the least ink against it.
3. **The biggest impact you can still defend.**

A dropped argument with clean weighing beats a contested one you think you're winning. Decide before the speech. A half-collapse, going for two arguments at half strength, loses more rounds than picking the second-best option cleanly.

## Extend it properly

An extension has to carry the whole argument, not just the tag:

> Extend our Smith card: the policy cuts funding within a year (link), which collapses rural clinics (internal link), leaving two million without care (impact).

Run that chain in one tight sentence. Reuse it word for word in [final focus](/guides/pf-final-focus); the judge should hear the same story twice.

## Frontline what they said

Answer the responses that are still on your argument, starting with turns. If they read five responses, find the shared flaw and answer them together.

## Weigh, and close the door

[Weighing](/guides/weighing-in-debate) tells the judge why your argument matters more. Do it comparatively: name their impact and say why yours comes first.

Then close the door. Picture their best final focus and preempt it: "Even if they win their economy argument, our impact is irreversible and theirs isn't." A preempt the other team doesn't answer is one of the most persuasive things a judge can hear.

## Defense on their case

Extend the one or two pieces of defense that do the most work against what they're going for. Not all of them.

## Common mistakes

- Extending tags without warrants.
- Going for everything.
- Weighing only your own impact without comparing it to theirs.
- New arguments: anything not in an earlier speech shouldn't appear here.

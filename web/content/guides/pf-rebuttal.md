Rebuttal is the 4-minute speech after the constructives. It decides which arguments survive into the back half. Its job is time allocation: spend your time where it costs the other team the most.

## Triage by threat, not by order

Before you speak, sort their contentions:

- **Real threats:** well-built contentions with several links that they clearly want to go for. Give these diverse, hard-to-group responses, and look hard at their evidence.
- **Time-sucks:** thin contentions they'll probably drop. Give these a turn or two and move on.

Answering every contention equally is the most common rebuttal mistake.

## Make positive time trades

Spend less of your time to force more of theirs.

- A **turn** forces them to frontline it or concede your offense.
- A response that **filters out several of their arguments** at once ("their whole case assumes the policy passes in time") beats four separate pieces of defense.
- A **weighing argument** read in rebuttal frames the back half before they start.

## First rebuttal

You attack their case. Read a mix of defense and offense on the contentions that matter. Label each response (non-unique, no link, turn) so the judge can flow it.

## Second rebuttal

Second rebuttal both attacks and [frontlines](/guides/frontlining). Frontline your own case first. Every frontline now is one less problem in summary. A rough even split works in fast rounds; move time toward whichever side has the more dangerous responses.

## Use their time allocation

How the other team splits its time tells you where they're going. If first rebuttal spent three minutes on your second contention, expect them to collapse against it. Prepare for that.

## Common mistakes

- Reading defense on everything and no offense.
- Repeating the same response in different words.
- Responding to an argument they never made.
- Forgetting to [weigh](/guides/weighing-in-debate) until summary.

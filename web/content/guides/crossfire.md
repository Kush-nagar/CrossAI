---
title: How to win crossfire in Public Forum
description: What crossfire is actually for, the questions worth asking, and how to answer without giving away the round.
order: 7
---

Public Forum has three 3-minute crossfires: one after the constructives, one after the rebuttals, and a grand crossfire with all four debaters after the summaries. Judges don't flow most of it, so crossfire wins rounds only when it sets up something you'll use in a speech.

## What crossfire is for

1. **Clarifying** what their argument actually claims, so your responses hit it.
2. **Getting concessions** you can quote later: "In crossfire they admitted the policy takes five years."
3. **Exposing evidence problems:** a missing warrant, an old date, a miscut card.

If a question doesn't do one of those, skip it.

## Asking good questions

- **Ask short, closed questions.** "Does your card say the shift happens this year?" beats "Can you explain your second contention?"
- **Ask about mechanisms, not conclusions.** "Who enforces the ban?" gets further than "Why is your impact true?"
- **Stop when you have it.** Once they concede the point, move on. Don't let them explain it away.
- **Ask for evidence when it matters.** Call for a card you plan to indict, then read it during prep.

## Answering well

- **Answer briefly, then pivot to your case.** A one-sentence answer and a return to your strongest argument.
- **Don't concede what you need.** If a question is a trap, answer the part that's true and reject the premise.
- **Never bluff about evidence.** "Our card doesn't say that, but here's why it still holds" is fine. Inventing what a card says is not.

## Grand crossfire

Grand crossfire is all four debaters. Keep it civil, let your partner speak, and use it to set up the clash your summary just chose. It's a bad time to start a new argument.

## Using crossfire later

A concession only counts if it reaches a speech. Write it on your flow the moment you hear it and bring it up in the next speech: "As they conceded in crossfire..."

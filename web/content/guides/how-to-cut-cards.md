A card is a piece of evidence: a **tag** (your claim), a **cite** (who said it, when and where) and the **text** from the source, with the parts you'll read marked. Cutting well makes your evidence faster to read and harder to attack.

## The tag

The tag is a short statement of what the card proves, written the way you'll extend it later. "Funding cuts close rural clinics within a year" is a tag. "Clinics" is not. Emphasize the key words, but keep it to one line when you can.

## The cite

Lead with the author's last name and the year: **Smith 24**. Then give the full citation: the author's qualifications, the date, the title, the publication and a link. Qualifications matter in round, so include them.

## Underlining and highlighting

- Keep the card in its **original paragraphs**. Don't condense or rearrange the text.
- **Underline** the context that supports the claim, and use bold underline for the most important lines. That's where a judge will look first if the card is disputed.
- **Highlight** only what you'll read aloud. Highlight in complete, readable sentences and drop filler words, but never change what the author meant.
- Don't underline whole paragraphs. If everything is emphasized, nothing is.

## Cut honestly

Highlighting can make a card say something its author didn't mean. That's a miscut, and it can cost you the round or get you disqualified under evidence rules. Before you read a card, check:

1. Read the paragraphs before and after. Does the author argue the opposite elsewhere?
2. Does the highlighted text still say what the full text says?
3. Is the date and qualification correct?

Leagues and tournaments have different evidence rules, including whether paraphrasing is allowed and what you must be able to show when a card is called. Check yours.

## Finding evidence

Start with background reading, then search for specific claims. You can also learn a lot from what teams have already disclosed: [Cross's card search](/features/card-search) finds disclosed Public Forum cards by claim or author and copies them into your file with the highlighting intact. Always check the original source before you read someone else's card.
